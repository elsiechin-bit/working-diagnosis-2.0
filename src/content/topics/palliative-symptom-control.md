## Overview


Follow the WHO ladder, but in palliative care reach strong opioids early when pain warrants it. Morphine is first-line; oxycodone where morphine is not tolerated; **reduce doses and use caution in renal impairment** (consider oxycodone, or fentanyl/methadone with specialist input).

  #### Practical conversions (always check locally)
  
   **Oral morphine → subcut morphine:** divide oral 24-h dose by 2.
   **Oral morphine → oral oxycodone:** roughly halve.
   **Breakthrough dose:** 1/6 to 1/10 of the 24-h total, PRN.
   Co-prescribe a **stimulant laxative** with every opioid, and a PRN antiemetic for initiation.
  
  Always pair opioids with a laxativeOpioid constipation is near-universal and does not tolerate. Anticipate it from day one.

      🫁Breathlessness▾
      Treat the reversible (effusion, infection, anaemia, heart failure) where appropriate, then manage the symptom directly.

   **Non-drug:** a handheld fan to the face, positioning, reassurance, pacing - genuinely effective.
   **Low-dose opioids** reduce the sensation of breathlessness (e.g. low-dose morphine).
   **Benzodiazepines** (e.g. lorazepam sublingual) for the anxiety component.
   - Oxygen helps only if hypoxaemic - it is not a treatment for the sensation in a non-hypoxic patient.

      🤢Nausea & vomiting▾
      Choose the antiemetic by the likely mechanism rather than reaching for one drug.

  CauseFirst choice
   Chemical / metabolic (opioids, hypercalcaemia, uraemia)Haloperidol
   Gastric stasis / functionalMetoclopramide (prokinetic)
   Raised intracranial pressureCyclizine + dexamethasone
   Broad / unclearLevomepromazine (broad-spectrum)

      🛏The last days: anticipatory prescribing▾
      When a patient is recognised to be dying, prescribe anticipatory subcutaneous PRN medicines so the four common terminal symptoms can be treated immediately. A syringe driver delivers continuous symptom control when the oral route is lost.

  SymptomTypical SC PRN
   Pain / breathlessnessMorphine (or oxycodone)
   Nausea / agitationHaloperidol or levomepromazine
   Terminal restlessness / distressMidazolam
   Respiratory secretions ("death rattle")Hyoscine butylbromide / glycopyrronium
  
  Treat secretions early and reassure familyAntimuscarinics dry secretions already present poorly, so give them at the first sign. The rattle usually distresses relatives more than the patient - explanation matters.

   ✦**Prescribe ahead.** The worst symptom crises happen at 2am when nothing is charted.
   ✦**Review medicines:** stop everything not aimed at comfort.
   ✦**Renal impairment** changes opioid choice - get advice early.
   ✦**Hospice teams** are a phone call away - use them for complex symptoms.
