#### The lipid paradox
          Cholesterol is essential for cell membranes, hormones, and vitamin D synthesis. However, elevated LDL cholesterol, low HDL, and high triglycerides increase atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk.

          #### Key lipid markers
          
            **LDL-C (low-density lipoprotein):** "Bad" cholesterol; drives atherosclerosis. Lower is better for ASCVD prevention.
            **HDL-C (high-density lipoprotein):** "Good" cholesterol; protective. Higher is better.
            **Total cholesterol:** Sum of all cholesterol; less useful alone.
            **Triglycerides:** Energy storage molecules. Elevated triglycerides (>150 mg/dL) increase ASCVD and pancreatitis risk, especially if >500 mg/dL.
            **Non-HDL cholesterol:** Total cholesterol minus HDL; captures all atherogenic particles (LDL, VLDL, IDL).
            **Lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)]:** Genetic marker; elevated levels (~>50 mg/dL) increase ASCVD risk independent of LDL.
            **ApoB:** Better predictor of ASCVD risk than LDL-C in some patients; reflects total atherogenic particle count.

        💊Pharmacological management▾
        
          #### Statins (first-line)
          
            **Mechanism:** HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors; reduce LDL-C by 30–50% depending on intensity.
            **High-intensity:** Atorvastatin 40–80 mg, Rosuvastatin 20–40 mg (reduce LDL-C by >50%).
            **Moderate-intensity:** Simvastatin 20–40 mg, Pravastatin 40–80 mg, Atorvastatin 10–20 mg (reduce LDL-C 30–49%).
            **Side effects:** Muscle pain (myalgia) in 5–10%; myositis rare (
            **Monitoring:** Lipids at 4–12 weeks, then annually. LFTs and CK only if symptomatic.
          
          #### Ezetimibe
          
            **Mechanism:** Inhibits cholesterol absorption in intestine; reduces LDL-C by additional 15–20% when added to statin.
            **Use:** Added to statin in moderate to high-risk patients not at goal, or statin-intolerant.
            **Side effects:** Well-tolerated; minimal interactions.
          
          #### PCSK9 inhibitors
          
            **Mechanism:** Monoclonal antibodies (evolocumab, alirocumab) or inclisiran (siRNA); increase LDL receptor clearance; reduce LDL-C by additional 50–60%.
            **Use:** Very high-risk patients not at goal on statin + ezetimibe; familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH).
            **Cost:** Expensive (~$5,000–10,000/year); often requires special funding approval in NZ and Australia.
            **Side effects:** Injection site reactions, flu-like symptoms (rare); generally well-tolerated.
          
          #### Bempedoic acid
          
            **Mechanism:** Xanthine oxidase inhibitor; reduces uric acid and LDL-C by ~15%.
            **Use:** Statin-intolerant patients or add-on therapy in high-risk.
            **Caution:** May increase gout flares; contraindicated in uncontrolled gout or severe renal disease.
          
          #### Fibrates (for elevated triglycerides)
          
            **Mechanism:** Activate PPAR-alpha; reduce triglycerides by 20–50%, modestly raise HDL-C.
            **Options:** Fenofibrate, bezafibrate (more common in UK/NZ than USA).
            **Use:** Severe hypertriglyceridaemia (>500 mg/dL or ~5.6 mmol/L) or persistent elevation despite statin + lifestyle.
            **Side effects:** Myositis (especially with statin), renal impairment, gallstones, hepatotoxicity.
            **Drug interactions:** Increases statin levels; monitor CK and LFTs closely.
          
          #### Omega-3 and icosapent ethyl
          
            **Omega-3 (fish oil):** Modest triglyceride reduction; no clear ASCVD benefit in trials.
            **Icosapent ethyl (Vascepa):** High-dose EPA; reduces triglycerides and modestly reduces ASCVD events in high-risk patients with elevated triglycerides on statin. Not widely funded.

        🧬Familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH)▾
        
          #### Overview
          Autosomal dominant disorder (1:250–1:500 frequency); LDL-C typically >5 mmol/L (>190 mg/dL) from birth. High ASCVD risk in childhood if untreated.

          #### Diagnosis
          
            **Clinical:** Dutch Lipid Clinic Network or MEDPED criteria (combination of LDL-C level, tendon xanthomas, skin xanthomas, corneal arcus, family history).
            **Genetic testing:** LDLR, APOB, PCSK9 mutations; recommended for all suspected FH.
          
          #### Management
          
            **Children:** Statins from age 8–10 in heterozygous FH; some recommend earlier in high-risk families.

            **Homozygous FH:** Rare; requires aggressive combination therapy, lipoprotein apheresis, and evolocumab or inclisiran.

        🍎Lifestyle and non-pharmacological management▾
        
          #### Diet
          
            **Saturated fat reduction:** Target 
            **Plant sterols and stanols:** 2 g/day can reduce LDL-C by ~10%.
            **Soluble fibre (oats, beans):** 10–25 g/day reduces LDL-C by 5–10%.
            **Mediterranean or DASH diet:** Evidence-based patterns that lower ASCVD risk.
            **Trans fats:** Avoid; increase LDL and lower HDL.
          
          #### Exercise
          
            - 150 min/week moderate-intensity aerobic activity; raises HDL-C by 5–10%.
            - Modest LDL-C reduction (~5–10%) with sustained exercise.
          
          #### Weight loss
          
            - 5–10% weight loss improves triglycerides and HDL-C; modest LDL-C reduction.
          
          #### Smoking cessation
          
            - Raises HDL-C and reduces ASCVD risk immediately.

        ❓Common questions▾
        
          #### Do I need statins if my cholesterol is normal?
          Not necessarily. Statin benefit depends on ASCVD risk, not cholesterol level alone. Someone with LDL-C 3.5 mmol/L but prior MI or diabetes benefits more than someone with 6 mmol/L and no risk factors.

          #### Are statins safe long-term?
          Yes. Decades of data show statins are safe in millions. Muscle pain is usually reversible; serious myositis is rare. Benefits in preventing heart attacks and strokes far outweigh risks for most people at moderate to high risk.

          #### Should I check lipids fasting?
          LDL-C and total cholesterol: not affected by food, so non-fasting OK. Triglycerides: affected by recent food, so fasting preferred for accurate measurement.

          #### Why is my LDL still high on statin?
          Non-adherence (most common), insufficient dose, genetic statin resistance, or need for combination therapy. Add ezetimibe or consider PCSK9 inhibitor if very high risk.
