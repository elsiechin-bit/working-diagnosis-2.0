#### What is restless legs syndrome?
        **Restless Legs Syndrome (RLS)** is a neurological disorder characterised by an irresistible urge to move the legs, usually accompanied by uncomfortable sensations. It is a sleep-related movement disorder that impairs sleep quality and quality of life.

        #### Essential diagnostic criteria (IRLSSG)
        
          **Urge to move the legs:** Usually accompanied by uncomfortable sensations in the legs (deep ache, creeping, crawling, burning, restlessness)
          **Circadian pattern:** Symptoms worsen in evening and night; may persist during sleep as periodic leg movements
          **Relief with movement:** Symptoms improve or resolve with walking, stretching, or moving legs; relief lasts only while moving or shortly after
          **Nocturnal predominance:** Most bothersome at night or when trying to rest

        #### Pathophysiology (incompletely understood)
        
          **Dopamine dysregulation:** Low dopamine in putamen and midbrain; dopamine agonists effective
          **Iron deficiency:** Low brain iron (measured by CSF ferritin or iron imaging) associated with RLS; mechanism unclear but dopamine metabolism impaired
          **Genetics:** 40-60% familial; multiple loci identified (MEIS1, BTBD9, MAP2K5)
          **Sleep-wake regulation:** Circadian pattern suggests hypothalamic involvement; periodic leg movements (PLMs) may disrupt sleep architecture

        #### Classification
        
          **Primary (idiopathic) RLS:** 60-80%; genetic predisposition; no underlying secondary cause
          **Secondary RLS:** Associated with specific conditions (see below); symptoms may improve with treatment of underlying cause

      🔍Clinical presentation and secondary causes▾
      
        #### Typical presentation
        
          **Sensations in legs:** Deep ache, creeping, crawling, burning, twitching, tingling; bilateral and symmetrical (rarely unilateral)
          **Location:** Usually lower legs; can involve thighs, feet, or even arms
          **Timing:** Evening and night; worsens during rest, sitting, lying down
          **Impact on sleep:** Difficulty falling asleep; frequent arousals; non-restorative sleep
          **Periodic leg movements:** Involuntary leg jerks during sleep; often noted by bed partner; occur in ~80% of RLS patients
          **Daytime consequences:** Insomnia, daytime somnolence, fatigue, impaired cognition, mood disturbance

        #### Secondary causes (15-40% of cases)
        
          **Iron deficiency anaemia:** Most common secondary cause; serum ferritin 
          **Chronic kidney disease:** Especially on dialysis; iron loss, uraemia, electrolyte imbalance implicated
          **Pregnancy:** Third trimester common; usually resolves postpartum
          **Medications:** Antidepressants (especially SSRIs), antipsychotics, metoclopramide, antihistamines, some anticonvulsants
          **Neurological conditions:** Parkinson's disease, neuropathy, spinal cord lesions
          **Metabolic:** Diabetes, thyroid disease, liver disease
          **Caffeine and alcohol:** Can exacerbate symptoms

        #### Severity assessment (IRLS rating scale)
        
          **Mild:** Occasional symptoms; minimal impact on sleep/function
          **Moderate:** Frequent symptoms; some night-to-night variability; noticeable impact on sleep
          **Severe:** Nightly symptoms; significant sleep disruption; daytime consequences
          **Very severe:** Symptoms throughout day and night; major disability

      📋Diagnosis and investigations▾
      
        #### Clinical diagnosis
        
          **Mostly clinical:** Based on history and essential diagnostic criteria; no specific biomarker
          **Mimics to exclude:** Akathisia (movement disorder from antipsychotics; response to beta-blockers); leg cramps (painful but localized spasm); positional discomfort; leg pain from other causes (neuropathy, claudication)
          **Key history:** Onset, progression, family history (familial RLS earlier), impact on sleep, medications, comorbidities

        #### Investigations (targeted)
        
          **Full blood count and ferritin:** Screen for iron deficiency; ferritin 
          **Renal function and electrolytes:** CKD is secondary cause; assess K+, Mg2+, Ca2+
          **TSH:** Rule out hypothyroidism
          **Glucose:** Diabetes associated
          **Polysomnography (sleep study):** Not required for diagnosis; useful if sleep apnoea suspected or symptoms atypical; documents periodic leg movements and sleep architecture
          **EMG/NCS:** Only if neuropathy suspected based on exam

        #### Medication review
        
          - Discontinue culprit medications if possible (SSRIs, metoclopramide, antihistamines)
          - Some patients improve significantly with medication change alone

      💊Management and treatment▾
      
        #### Non-pharmacological (first-line)
        
          **Sleep hygiene:** Regular sleep schedule, cool dark bedroom, avoid screens before bed
          **Avoid triggers:** Limit caffeine (coffee, tea, chocolate), alcohol (worsens sleep and RLS), nicotine
          **Leg massage and stretching:** Evening stretches, leg massage, warm baths
          **Distraction:** Mental engagement (reading, hobbies) during episodes
          **Movement:** Walking, light exercise (especially in afternoon/evening)
          **Iron supplementation:** If ferritin 
          **Manage secondary causes:** Treat anaemia, CKD optimisation, medication adjustment

        #### Augmentation (major concern with dopamine agonists)
        
          **Definition:** Paradoxical worsening of RLS despite adequate dopamine agonist dose; symptoms appear earlier in day, spread to other body parts, or increase in intensity
          **Mechanism:** Unclear; possibly dopamine receptor changes or circadian dysregulation
          **Management:** Rotate to alternative class (anticonvulsant), add iron, slowly taper dopamine agonist, or switch to rotigotine (lower augmentation risk)
          **Prevention:** Use lowest effective dose; consider iron supplementation; avoid rapid escalation

        #### Prognosis and long-term management
        
          **Chronic condition:** Usually lifelong; symptoms may wax and wane
          **Secondary RLS:** May improve with treatment of underlying cause (iron repletion, CKD management)
          **Medication tolerance:** Some patients develop reduced response; rotation to alternative class or dose increase needed
          **Quality of life impact significant:** Sleep fragmentation, daytime somnolence, mood disturbance; supportive management and patient education important

      ⚠️Complications and red flags▾
      
        #### Sleep-related complications
        
          **Insomnia:** Difficulty initiating and maintaining sleep; non-restorative sleep
          **Sleep fragmentation:** Periodic leg movements (PLMs) cause frequent arousals
          **Daytime somnolence:** Secondary to poor sleep; impairs safety (driving), cognition, mood
          **Sleep apnoea:** May coexist; screen if snoring, apneic episodes reported

        #### Psychological and social impact
        
          - Mood disturbance (depression, anxiety)
          - Relationship strain (bed partner disruption)
          - Occupational impairment (shift work intolerance)

        #### When to refer
        
          - Diagnostic uncertainty
          - Severe RLS refractory to first-line therapy
          - Suspected sleep apnoea or other sleep disorder
          - Augmentation despite management
          - Need for sleep study or specialised autonomic testing
