## Overview


**Developmental milestones are skills or behaviours a child acquires within an expected age range,** reflecting maturation of motor control, cognition, communication, and social-emotional capability. Milestones provide a framework for surveillance; wide normal variation exists (2–3 months variability is common & unremarkable), and achievement is influenced by genetics, environmental enrichment, physical health, and social factors.

        #### Five core developmental domains
        
          **Gross motor:** Large muscle control (head control, sitting, crawling, walking, running, jumping). Progresses cephalocaudal (head→trunk→limbs) and proximal→distal (centre→periphery).
          **Fine motor:** Small hand/finger control (grasp, reach, finger dexterity, object manipulation). Shifts from reflexive (palmar grasp) to voluntary, precise pincer grasp (thumb + fingers) by 9–12 months.
          **Communication (language & speech):** Receptive (understanding) and expressive (speaking, vocalisations) language. Includes pre-verbal (cooing, babbling, gesture) and verbal milestones.
          **Cognitive:** Problem-solving, learning, reasoning, memory, attention. Progresses from reflexive responses to symbolic play, imitation, cause-effect understanding.
          **Personal-social (socioemotional):** Interaction with others, emotional expression, self-regulation, play, feeding/toileting independence. Includes attachment, social smile, separation anxiety (normal developmental fear ~6–24 months).
        
        #### NZ screening framework
        
          **Plunket Well Child:** Universal health checks (birth, 6 weeks, 3–4 months, 6 months, 12 months, 2 years, 3 years, 4 years). Surveillance via parental history & direct observation; referral pathway to specialists if concern.
          **Before School Check (B4SC):** Funded health screening for all 4-year-olds; includes developmental & behavioural assessment via parental questionnaire & preschool teacher input. Identifies need for early intervention.
          **AAP guidelines (applied in NZ):** Formal developmental screening recommended at 9, 18, 24 months using validated tool (Ages & Stages Questionnaire, ASQ; Bayley Scales; Brigance; DIAL). Autism-specific screening at 18, 24 months.
          **Early intervention (Ministry of Education):** Free services for children birth–3 years with developmental delay or disability; can access up to age 5 if eligible. Accessed via GP referral, Plunket, or direct contact to local Ministry office.
        
        #### Important caveats
        
          **Individual variation normal:** Children develop at different rates; achievement ±2–3 months from expected age rarely concerning. Uneven development (e.g., advanced language, delayed gross motor) common & often resolves.
          **Environmental factors matter:** Parental language exposure, play opportunities, trauma/neglect, poverty, limited healthcare access all influence milestone achievement. Context essential in interpretation.

      👶0–3 months (newborn & early infancy)▾
      
        **Newborns are predominantly reflex-driven;** purposeful movement & social awareness emerge gradually. Assessment focuses on reflex presence/absence, head control, and response to stimuli.

          DomainTypical Milestones (0–3 months)
          Gross motorHolds head up briefly (1–2 months); lifts head off surface when prone (1–2 months); arm/leg movements jerky & uncoordinated initially, become smoother.
          Fine motorPalmar (reflexive) grasp present at birth, weakens ~3–4 months; follows objects with eyes (tracking) from ~6 weeks; occasional swatting at objects near face (2–3 months).
          CommunicationCries, grunts, reflexive sounds; startled by loud noises; coos begin ~6–8 weeks (vowel sounds, proto-conversation with caregiver).
          CognitivePrefers faces to objects; startle (Moro) reflex present; limited attention (gaze-aversion after brief focus); no object permanence (out of sight = gone).
          Personal-socialSocial smile emerges ~6–8 weeks (huge developmental milestone); fixates on caregiver's face; calmed by soothing voice/touch. Beginning of attachment.

          Red flags (0–3 months)
          Persistent asymmetry (always head turned one direction; one-sided grasp); no eye contact or social response; excessive irritability; poor feeding; seizures (even subtle: eye deviation, lip smacking). Refer if persistent concern.

      👶4–6 months (early infancy)▾
      
        **Increased purposeful movement, emerging hand control, and social engagement.** Babies begin to reach for objects intentionally & show emerging stranger/separation awareness.

          DomainTypical Milestones (4–6 months)
          Gross motorHolds head up steadily when held upright; rolls prone→supine (4–5 months); beginning to sit with support (~5 months); weight-bearing when held standing (~5–6 months).
          Fine motorReaches for objects (not always accurate); raking grasp (all fingers scoop object, thumb not opposed); transfers objects hand→hand; brings hands to mouth.
          CommunicationBabbling begins (repetitive consonant-vowel: "bababa"; "dadada"). Responds to own name. Turns head toward voice. Laughs aloud.
          CognitiveBeginning object permanence (searches for dropped object briefly); watches falling toys; interested in exploration; primitive cause-effect (shakes rattle to hear sound).
          Personal-socialShows stranger anxiety (~5–6 months, normal); enjoys looking at self in mirror; initiates simple social games (peek-a-boo, tickling); seeks comfort from familiar adult.

          Red flags (4–6 months)
          No babbling by 6 months; no reaching; persistent asymmetry; not vocalising; no social smile; does not turn to sound; poor eye contact. Note: speech delay in isolation (babbling only, no syllables) common; hearing screen recommended if concern.

      🍼7–9 months (late infancy)▾
      
        **Mobility increases; purposeful hand skills emerge; separation anxiety peaks.** Introduction of solid foods (6+ months); pincer grasp developing.

          DomainTypical Milestones (7–9 months)
          Gross motorSits independently (7–8 months); begins crawling (7–10 months; some skip to cruising); pulls to stand while holding furniture (~8–10 months). Locomotion begins, increasing access/danger.
          Fine motorInferior pincer grasp (thumb + side of index finger; 7–8 months), progressing toward neat pincer (tips of thumb + index by 9–10 months). Deliberate grasp & release of objects. Explores objects by mouthing.
          CommunicationBabbling more complex (multisyllabic: "mamama", "dadada", "bababa"). First words emerging by 12 months (most achieve 1–3 words by 12 months). Follows simple one-word commands ("no", "bye"). Waves bye-bye (~8–10 months).
          CognitiveObject permanence well-established (searches for hidden objects); understands simple cause-effect; plays with toys (banging, dropping); mimics actions (clapping, waving hands).
          Personal-socialAttachment to primary caregiver strong; separation anxiety peaks (~6–24 months, normal). Shows fear of strangers. Explores environment with caregiver nearby ("safe base"). Beginning of independence within secure attachment.

          Separation anxiety (7–24 months)
          Distress when caregiver leaves; difficulty with unfamiliar people—normal developmental phase, peaks ~12–18 months. Not pathological; reassure parents. Predictability & routine reduce distress. Avoid sneaking away (erodes trust); brief, consistent goodbyes better.

      👣12–15 months (toddlerhood begins)▾
      
        **Walking emerges; language explosion beginning; increasing independence & assertiveness.** Crucial age for screening per AAP (12-month well visit). Peak age for formal developmental assessment.

          DomainTypical Milestones (12–15 months)
          Gross motorStands alone briefly (11–13 months); walks with support (cruises along furniture; 11–14 months); independent walking (12–15 months, ±2–3 months normal). Some children walk as early as 9 months; others not until 18 months (both normal). Climbs on furniture.
          Fine motorNeat pincer grasp well-established (picks up small objects precisely); deliberately places objects into containers; intentional grasp & release. Beginning to self-feed (utensil use clumsy).
          Communication1–3 words by 12 months (highly variable; 10–50 words by 15 months normal range); understands 20–50 words. Points to objects (protodeclarative pointing: "look at that"). Responds to own name. Babbling continues; speech becoming clearer.
          CognitiveObject permanence consolidated; understands simple commands ("Give me..."); problem-solving emerging (figures out how to open containers, cause-effect clear). Play becoming more symbolic (pushing toy car, pretend feeding doll).
          Personal-socialShows affection (hugs, kisses caregivers); parallel play begins (plays alongside other children, not with them). Increasing assertiveness/negativity (oppositional behaviour, temper tantrums emerging). Enjoys games (peek-a-boo, pat-a-cake). Fear of strangers waning.

          Language variation at 12–18 months
          Wide normal range: some toddlers 50+ words by 18 months; others  expressive word count. "Late talkers" without other delays often resolve; ~70% catch up by age 3 without intervention.

          Screening recommendation: 12–15 months
          AAP recommends formal developmental screening at this age. Use validated tool (ASQ, Bayley, etc.) in primary care. Gross motor delay (not walking by 18 months), language delay (few words, poor comprehension), or social concerns warrant further evaluation.

      🧒18–24 months (toddlerhood consolidated)▾
      
        **"Terrible twos" beginning; language explosion; increasing autonomy & negativity.** Peak for tantrums, toilet training readiness assessment, and autism screening (AAP recommends at 18 & 24 months).

          DomainTypical Milestones (18–24 months)
          Gross motorWalks independently (well-established by 18 months); runs (stiff-legged initially, by 18–20 months); walks backward; kicks ball (18–24 months). Climbs stairs with support (railing). Jumps (emerging by 24 months). Occasional falls normal.
          Fine motorScribbles (first mark-making, not purposeful); turns pages of book (multiple at once initially); stacks 2–4 blocks. Self-feeding with utensil (spills common). Removes socks/shoes (emerging independence).
          Communication50+ words by 18 months; 200–300 words by 24 months (range ~50–500 normal). 2-word phrases emerging (18–24 months: "more milk", "daddy go"). Understands simple questions ("Where's your nose?"). Follows 2-step commands. Speech intelligible to family ~50% by 24 months (not to strangers yet).
          CognitiveSymbolic play flourishing (pretend eating, drinking, sleeping with toys). Identifies body parts (point to nose, eyes on request). Interest in pictures, books. Attention span 2–5 minutes. Sorts objects by colour/shape (emerging). Problem-solving more sophisticated.
          Personal-socialParallel play dominant (plays alongside peers, not with); negative behaviours peak (tantrums, hitting, biting, "no" reflex strong). Affection for familiar people; separation anxiety declining. Shows empathy/concern for distressed others (inconsistent). Toilet training readiness signs emerge (interest in bathroom, staying dry 2+ hours).

          Autism screening at 18 & 24 months (AAP)
          Use validated tool (M-CHAT-R: Modified Checklist for Autism in Toddlers, Revised). Red flags: limited eye contact, no pointing (protodeclarative), limited social reciprocity, repetitive play, no/few words, poor response to name, unusual sensory interests. Early identification → early intervention (evidence-based).

          Second screening milestone: 18–24 months
          AAP & NZ guidelines recommend formal screening here. Language concerns (few words, poor comprehension) most common referral reason. Also assess social communication, motor skills, adaptive behaviour. Screen for vision/hearing problems.

      🎯2–3 years (early toddlerhood)▾
      
        **Language flowering; play becoming increasingly imaginative; self-regulation beginning.** Toilet training typically begins; behavioural challenges (oppositional defiance, aggression) common. Transition to preschool often occurs by age 2.5–3 years.

          DomainTypical Milestones (2–3 years)
          Gross motorRuns smoothly; walks up stairs alternating feet (down still with support); walks backward; stands on one foot (2–3 seconds by 3 years). Kicks ball with direction. Throws ball overhand (immature form). Pedals tricycle (emerging by 3 years).
          Fine motorCopies circular scribble (24–30 months); beginning to copy vertical & horizontal lines (30+ months). Stacks 6–8 blocks. Uses spoon/fork with some spilling. Removes clothing (shirts, socks). Washes & dries hands (with help). Turns pages singly in book.
          Communication300–1000+ words by 3 years (ranges from 200 to 1500+). 2–4 word sentences ("Want more juice"; "Daddy go work"). Tells simple stories about recent events. Asks endless "why" questions (language hunger). Speech 75%+ intelligible to strangers by 3 years. Stuttering common & normal (usually resolves by 4–5 years). Understands 3-step commands.
          CognitiveImaginative/symbolic play dominant (tea parties, doctor's office, pretend cooking). Recognises self in photos/mirror. Sorts by colour/size. Counts 1–3 (by 3 years, some to 10+). Names pictures in books. Attention 5–10 minutes. Beginning to understand sequencing (before/after).
          Personal-socialToilet training mastery variable (many trained day by 3 years; night dryness lags, normal until age 5–6). Shows self-awareness (embarrassment, pride). Cooperative play brief (2–3 minutes). Aggression (hitting, biting) peaks & begins to decline with language skills improving. Follows simple rules inconsistently. Increasing independence; beginning to dress self (buttons difficult). Parallel play transitions to associative play (beginning interaction with peers).

          Stuttering at 2–4 years
          Normal developmental disfluency; occurs when language demand exceeds motor planning. "D-d-dog", prolongations ("sssun"), word repetitions common. No referral needed unless: >25% of speech disfluent, speech anxiety emerging, parental coaching counterproductive. Speech-language pathology referral if persisting past age 4–5 or increasing.

      🎨3–4 years (preschool age)▾
      
        **Social play emerging; preoperational thinking evident; self-regulation improving.** Most children in preschool by 3–4 years; transition to peer group & adults other than parents (important for social-emotional development). Creative play flourishing.

          DomainTypical Milestones (3–4 years)
          Gross motorRuns smoothly, alternating feet; walks on tiptoes; stands on one foot 5–10 seconds. Jumps with both feet together. Pedals tricycle with direction. Throws ball overhand (more accuracy). Climbing agility increases. Balance improving.
          Fine motorCopies circle & beginning to copy cross (+) by age 4. Draws person (head, 2 arms, 2 legs by 4 years, though crude). Cuts paper with scissors (poorly initially). Builds tower 9–10 blocks; builds bridge. Holds pencil with developing tripod grip. Buttons large buttons.
          Communication1000–1500+ words by 4 years. 4–5+ word sentences; tells longer stories. Speech intelligible to strangers ~100% by 4 years. Asks meaning of words. Understands jokes/riddles (emerging). Listens to stories without pictures (attention 10–15 minutes). Counts to 4, knows 1–2 colours by name. Rhymes emerging. Stuttering usually resolved by 4–5 years.
          CognitivePreoperational thought: egocentrism high (difficulty seeing others' perspective); magical thinking (monsters, wishes). Imagination flourishing; pretend play elaborate (full scenarios, multiple roles). Sorts by one characteristic. Beginning classification. Interested in cause-effect. Memory improving (recalls events from weeks prior). Problem-solving emerging (figures out how to reach high object).
          Personal-socialAssociative play flourishing (plays alongside & with peers); beginning cooperative play (games with turn-taking). Shows empathy/concern consistently. Tells lies/fantasies (not malicious; developmental, egocentrism). Play themes: family, occupational roles, adventure. Emotional regulation improving; tantrums declining. Follows simple rules (often). Increasingly independent (dresses, toilets with reminding, washes hands). Separation from parent for preschool easier by 4 years.

          Before School Check (B4SC) at ~4 years
          NZ universal health screening; parent + teacher questionnaires assess developmental & emotional health. Identifies children needing early intervention before school entry. Referral pathway to specialists; free early childhood education support available.

      🌟4–5 years (late preschool)▾
      
        **School readiness emerging; academic skills (letter recognition, counting) developing; peer relationships central.** Transition to school (age 5 in NZ) major developmental milestone. School readiness complex (motor, cognitive, social-emotional, adaptive).

          DomainTypical Milestones (4–5 years)
          Gross motorRuns smoothly with coordination; hops on one foot 4+ times; balances on one leg 10+ seconds. Walks on straight line. Gallops. Skips (2-foot coordination step-and-hop). Throws ball with direction & accuracy improving. Catches bounced ball with arms (hands lag). Rides tricycle/early bicycle skills.
          Fine motorCopies cross (4 years), beginning to copy square by 5 years. Draws person with head, body, 4 limbs, beginning features (hair, eyes). Writes first name (invented spelling emerging). Pre-writing skills (copies lines, shapes); pencil grip more mature (tripod). Cuts on line with scissors. Traces shapes.
          Communication1500+ words; complex sentences (5+ words, subordinate clauses: "I want to go to the park because my friend is there"). Speech completely intelligible. Understands jokes, riddles, puns (egocentric interpretation, but enjoying language play). Recites nursery rhymes. Beginning letter-sound correspondence. Early reading emerging (sight words, simple phonics). Vocabulary explosion; asks meaning of new words constantly.
          CognitiveConcrete operational thinking beginning (age 5–6+). Classification improving (groups objects by multiple characteristics). Conservation emerging (mass, number; often fails still). Counting to 10+ reliably; begins addition/subtraction concept. Memory for events detailed; can recall 3–4-month-old events. Attention 15–20 minutes for interesting task. Problem-solving sophisticated (multiple strategies tested).
          Personal-socialCooperative play dominant; beginning competitive games (understanding rules, though rule flexibility high). Friendship preferences clear ("best friend"). Empathy well-established; shows concern, helps peers. Self-regulation improved; follows directions more consistently; awaits turn (though impatience common). Describes feelings ("I'm sad/angry/happy"). Independence high (dresses independently, toilets reliably, manages hygiene with reminding). Separation from parent easy. School transition anticipated with mix of excitement & anxiety (normal).

          School readiness assessment
          Multifactorial: motor skills (fine + gross), language (expressive + receptive), cognitive (attention, learning, problem-solving), social-emotional (cooperation, emotional regulation, self-care), adaptive (toileting, feeding). Early intervention services can support; readiness varies widely; not all 5-year-olds ready for formal academics (large normal range acceptable).

      ⚠️Red flags & when to refer▾
      
        #### General red flags across ages (any age)
        
          **Loss of skills:** Regression (child previously achieved milestone, then loses it: stopped walking, lost words) always concerning. Suggests neurological, medical, or psychological issue. Urgent evaluation warranted.
          **Persistent asymmetry:** Consistent favoring one side (hand, arm, leg); unilateral neglect. Suggests neurological concern (cerebral palsy, hemiplegic pattern, stroke history).
          **Seizure-like activity:** Staring spells, lip smacking, jerking, loss of consciousness. Immediate referral.
          **Hearing concern:** No response to name/sounds, speech/language delay, developmental regression. Formal audiometry recommended.
          **Vision concern:** Not fixing on objects, not tracking, eye misalignment (strabismus), light sensitivity, poor visual exploration. Ophthalmology referral.
        
        #### Age-specific red flags
        
          AgeRed Flags for Concern
          0–6 monthsNo social smile (by 8 weeks); no tracking; no vocalising/cooing; persistent asymmetry; poor feeding; high/low muscle tone (hypotonia/hypertonia); seizure-like activity.
          6–12 monthsNot babbling (by 9 months); no reaching for objects; no sitting with support (by 8 months); not responding to own name; poor eye contact; no stranger anxiety (by 8+ months); not crawling/cruising by 12 months (though some skip to walking).
          12–18 monthsNo words (by 15 months); not walking (by 18 months, though 15-18 months normal); not gesturing (pointing, waving); poor comprehension; no parallel play interaction; not following simple commands.
          18–24 monthsFewer than 50 words (by 24 months); no 2-word phrases (by 24 months); loss of words (regression); limited social interest; no pointing; repetitive/stereotyped play; difficulty with transitions; poor eye contact.
          2–3 yearsSpeech 
          3–4 yearsSpeech 
          4–5 yearsSpeech unintelligible; not counting/naming colours; not copying simple shapes; extreme behavioural/emotional dysregulation; severe social withdrawal; academic skills absent (letters, sounds); significant anxiety/fearfulness.
        
        #### Referral pathway in NZ
        
          **GP assessment first:** Developmental concern → GP gathers history, performs developmental screening, excludes medical causes (hearing, vision, metabolic, neurological).
          **Early intervention (birth–3 years):** Referral to Ministry of Education Early Intervention Services (free); accessed via GP, Plunket, or direct contact to local Ministry office. Multidisciplinary assessment; free services if eligible.
          **Preschool/school age (3–5 years):** Referral to paediatrician (community health or hospital), speech-language pathology, occupational therapy, psychology. Funding varies (some free via Health NZ, some private). B4SC at age 4 provides pathway.
          **Specialist referrals:** Paediatrician can refer to neurology, genetics, ophthalmology, audiology, developmental psychology as indicated.
        
        #### Counselling & reassurance
        
          **Parental anxiety:** Wide normal variation; ±2–3 months common. Many concerns resolve without intervention. Reassurance + watchful waiting often appropriate.
          **Late talkers (18–30 months):** Few words, normal comprehension, normal social/play skills → ~70% catch up by age 3 without intervention. Monitor; speech referral if not improving by 3 years or if comprehension/social skills concerning.
          **Prematurity adjustment:** Explain corrected age; milestone expectations adjusted accordingly until age 2–3 years. Child born 10 weeks early is developmentally 10 weeks "younger" until age 2–3.
          **Environmental enrichment:** Encourage play, language exposure, reading, social interaction. Poverty, limited stimulation, neglect all impair development; social work referral if indicated.

      📋Clinical pearls & NZ-specific considerations▾
      
        #### Pearls
        
          **Milestones are ranges, not deadlines.** Explain variation to parents; extreme anxiety common & often unwarranted. 2–3 months variation normal for most skills.
          **Parental report & observation both valuable.** Parents know child best; trust parent concern. But parental anxiety high; balanced assessment necessary.
          **Always screen for hearing & vision.** Sensory impairment masks true developmental potential. Newborn hearing screen routine in NZ; vision less standardised → ask about family hx, concerns at each visit.
          **Corrected age matters until age 2–3.** Parent confusion common; explain: "Your child is 12 months old, but 2 months adjusted. We expect milestones at 10 months, not 12."
          **Early intervention works.** Evidence strong for speech, occupational, and developmental services. Refer early if concern; no harm in assessment; early intervention improves outcomes.
          **Developmental progress irregular.** Uneven development common (advanced language, delayed motor, or vice versa). Often resolves; track trends over months rather than weeks.
          **Screen for autism at 18 & 24 months.** Early identification → early intervention (evidence-based ABA, speech/OT). Use M-CHAT-R or similar. Red flags: limited eye contact, no pointing, repetitive play, language delay.
        
        #### NZ-specific
        
          **Plunket access universal:** Every family has Plunket nurse contact (birth, 6 weeks, 3–4 mo, 6 mo, 12 mo, 2yr, 3yr, 4yr visits). Use Plunket for screening, reassurance, referral pathway. Free.
          **Before School Check (B4SC):** Funded universal screening at ~4 years; good opportunity to identify children needing early intervention before school. Parent + preschool teacher questionnaires; referral pathway built-in.
          **Early Intervention Services (Ministry of Education):** Free multidisciplinary assessment/services for birth–3 year-olds with developmental delay or disability. Also can access up to age 5 if delay identified early. Accessed via GP referral or direct contact to local Ministry office.
          **Health NZ funding variability:** Speech-language pathology, OT, psychology services vary by region/DHB (community vs private). GPs can refer; patient may wait or choose private (cost variable). Community services typically free; wait times variable.
          **Equity gaps:** Taupori/Pasifika children sometimes under-identified for early intervention; parental language barriers, healthcare access disparities. Culturally respectful assessment & family-centred approach essential.
