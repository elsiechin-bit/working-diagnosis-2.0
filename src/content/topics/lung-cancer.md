## Overview


**Lung cancers are classified by histology (WHO 2024) and increasingly by molecular profile.** Non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC; ~85% of cases) is further stratified by mutation status (EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET, KRAS, PD-L1 expression) to guide targeted & immunotherapy.

        #### Non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC)
        
          **Adenocarcinoma (~50% of all lung cancers; 60% of NSCLC):**
            
              - Most common in never-smokers & young patients
              - Peripheral location (distal airway origin)
              - Subtypes: acinar, papillary, solid, mucinous (invasive mucinous adenocarcinoma)
              - Molecular heterogeneity: EGFR mutations (10–50% Asian populations, 10–15% Caucasian), ALK rearrangements (3–7%), KRAS mutations (30–50%), PD-L1 expression variable
              - Better prognosis than squamous cell historically; improved outcomes with targeted therapy

          **Squamous cell carcinoma (~25–30% of NSCLC):**
            
              - Strongly associated with smoking history
              - Central airway location (primary bronchus involvement)
              - Higher risk of necrosis, cavitation, hypercalcaemia
              - Molecular: KEAP1, TP53, SOX2 mutations; PD-L1 expression often high (~55% >50% expression)
              - Historically worse prognosis; improved with checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy

          **Large-cell lung cancer (~5% NSCLC):**
            
              - Poorly differentiated, high-grade
              - Intermediate prognosis between small-cell & other NSCLC
              - Often diagnosed late (central or peripheral)

        #### Small-cell lung cancer (SCLC; ~15% of all lung cancers)
        
          **Biology:** Rapidly progressive, aggressive; neuroendocrine differentiation; almost all associated with heavy smoking
          **Presentation:** Often advanced at diagnosis (67% have metastatic disease); mediastinal/hilar masses common
          **Subtypes:** Limited-stage disease (LD; confined to one hemithorax + ipsilateral supraclavicular nodes; 30% at diagnosis) vs extensive-stage disease (ED; distant metastases; 70% at diagnosis)
          **Treatment:** Chemotherapy-based (platinum + etoposide) ± radiation; immunotherapy (atezolizumab/durvalumab) increasingly added for ED-SCLC
          **Prognosis:** LD-SCLC ~15–20% 5-year survival; ED-SCLC ~3–5%; rapid chemotherapy response but early recurrence common

      🔍Presentation & clinical features▾
      
        #### Early-stage presentation (often asymptomatic)
        
          **Discovered incidentally on imaging:** CT chest for unrelated indication; nodule 
          **Symptom development:** Cough (persistent, >3 weeks), change in chronic cough character, haemoptysis
          **Diagnosis timing:** Many early-stage patients diagnosed via screening (low-dose CT in high-risk), not symptoms
        
        #### Locally advanced presentation
        
          **Respiratory:** Persistent cough, dyspnoea, recurrent pneumonia (obstructive lesion), chest pain (pleural involvement)
          **Neurological:** Horner syndrome (superior sulcus/Pancoast tumour: ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis), hoarseness (recurrent laryngeal nerve involvement)
          **Mediastinal involvement:** Superior vena cava syndrome (facial swelling, venous distension), pericardial effusion (tamponade risk)
        
        #### Metastatic presentation (40–50% at diagnosis)
        
          **Brain metastases:** Headache, focal neurological deficits, seizures; ~25% of adenocarcinoma at diagnosis
          **Bone metastases:** Bone pain, pathological fracture; ~20% at diagnosis
          **Liver metastases:** Hepatomegaly, rarely jaundice
          **Adrenal:** Often incidental; ~10% have functional metastases
        
        #### Paraneoplastic syndromes
        
          **SIADH:** Hyponatraemia, confusion; especially SCLC
          **Cushing syndrome:** ACTH-secreting SCLC, ectopic cortisol
          **Hypercalcaemia:** Squamous cell carcinoma (PTHrP secretion)
          **Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS):** Muscle weakness, autonomic symptoms; SCLC association ~3%

      🔬Diagnosis & molecular testing▾
      
        #### Imaging & staging
        
          **CT chest/abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast:** Primary tumour size (T stage), mediastinal nodes (N stage), metastases
          **Brain MRI:** Indicated for stage III–IV NSCLC & all SCLC; ~25% have occult brain metastases
          **PET-CT:** ¹⁸FDG-avidity for staging (N & M stages); SUV >2.5 suggests malignancy but not diagnostic alone
          **Bone scan or NaF-PET:** Bone metastasis detection if clinically indicated
        
        #### Tissue diagnosis
        
          **Bronchoscopy:** Central lesions, proximal endobronchial involvement
          **EBUS-TBNA:** Hilar & mediastinal lymph node sampling; high sensitivity (~90%) for N staging
          **CT-guided percutaneous biopsy:** Peripheral nodules, wedge resection if diagnosis uncertain
          **Transoesophageal ultrasound (EUS):** Posterior mediastinal nodes
          **Pleural tap/pleural biopsy:** If effusion present (aids staging M1a)
        
        #### Molecular testing (essential for NSCLC therapy selection)
        **All adenocarcinoma & NSCLC with PD-L1 high expression should be tested for:**

          **EGFR mutations (exons 18–21):** Activating mutations (~10–15% Caucasian, 30–50% East Asian); sensitise to EGFR TKIs (erlotinib, gefitinib, afatinib). Test early in adenocarcinoma; associated with never-smoker/light-smoker phenotype.
          **ALK rearrangement (by FISH or IHC):** ~3–7% adenocarcinoma; associated with younger age, never-smoker; sensitise to ALK inhibitors (crizotinib, ceritinib, alectinib). High-risk CNS involvement.
          **ROS1 rearrangement:** ~1–2% adenocarcinoma; sensitise to ROS1 inhibitors (crizotinib, ceritinib). Also high CNS involvement risk.
          **BRAF V600E mutations:** ~3% adenocarcinoma; sensitise to BRAF inhibitors (dabrafenib + trametinib)
          **MET exon 14 skipping or amplification:** ~3–4% adenocarcinoma; sensitise to MET inhibitors (capmatinib, tepotinib)
          **KRAS G12C mutations:** ~13% adenocarcinoma; newly druggable with sotorasib/adagrasib (2024–2025 expanding access)
          **PD-L1 expression (IHC):** >50% (high), 1–49% (moderate), 
          **Tumour mutational burden (TMB) / microsatellite instability (MSI):** Research markers; emerging role in patient selection for immunotherapy
        
        #### NZ testing access
        
          **EGFR, ALK, BRAF, ROS1:** Funded testing at regional DHB labs; essential before first-line therapy
          **PD-L1:** Routine in adenocarcinoma; guides immunotherapy vs chemotherapy
          **Emerging mutations (KRAS G12C, MET, NTRK):** Increasingly available; check regional pathology protocols

      💊Treatment landscape 2024–2025▾
      
        #### Stage I–II NSCLC (resectable)
        
          **Surgery (gold standard):** Lobectomy ± mediastinal node dissection; video-assisted thoracoscopic (VATS) or open approaches. Adjuvant chemotherapy (platinum-doublet) for stage IB–II.
          **Radiation (if unresectable/declined surgery):** Stereotactic ablative radiotherapy (SABR) for stage I; hypofractionated external beam for II
          **Adjuvant therapy:** Stage IB–II receive pemetrexed (adenocarcinoma) or platinum ± gemcitabine (squamous); adjuvant PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitor (atezolizumab, durvalumab) now standard for PD-L1 high expression (stage II–III resected disease)
        
        #### Stage III NSCLC (locally advanced, unresectable)
        
          **Concurrent chemoradiotherapy:** Platinum ± pemetrexed/vinorelbine + high-dose radiation (60 Gy); consolidation immunotherapy with durvalumab (PD-L1 inhibitor) improves OS vs chemoRT alone (PACIFIC trial)
          **Resectable IIIA:** Induction chemoradiation followed by surgery if feasible
        
        #### Stage IV (metastatic) NSCLC
        **First-line therapy driven by molecular profile & PD-L1 expression:**

          **EGFR-mutant:** EGFR TKI (erlotinib, gefitinib, afatinib) preferred over chemotherapy; improved OS, better tolerated. CNS-penetrant agents (afatinib, osimertinib) if brain metastases.
          **ALK-rearranged:** ALK inhibitor (alectinib preferred; superior CNS penetration vs crizotinib)
          **ROS1-rearranged:** Crizotinib or ceritinib; similar CNS risk as ALK
          **BRAF V600E:** Dabrafenib + trametinib combination
          **KRAS G12C:** Sotorasib or adagrasib (monotherapy); emerging combination data
          **PD-L1 high (>50%):** Monotherapy checkpoint inhibitor (pembrolizumab) vs chemotherapy + pembrolizumab; monotherapy increasingly preferred if fit
          PD-L1 low/intermediate ( Chemotherapy + checkpoint inhibitor (pembrolizumab, atezolizumab, durvalumab) or chemotherapy alone if unfit
          **Driver mutation-negative, PD-L1 low:** Platinum doublet chemotherapy (pemetrexed + cisplatin/carboplatin for adenocarcinoma; gemcitabine/vinorelbine + platinum for squamous)
        
        #### SCLC
        
          **Limited-stage disease:** Concurrent chemotherapy (cisplatin/etoposide) + thoracic radiation; prophylactic cranial irradiation (PCI) for responders
          **Extensive-stage disease:** Chemotherapy (platinum + etoposide) ± immunotherapy; atezolizumab or durvalumab added to chemo improves OS (IMpower & CASPIAN trials)
          **Prognosis:** Median OS ~12 months (ED-SCLC); rapid response but early recurrence; CNS involvement risk ~50%

      🔍Screening & prevention▾
      
        #### Low-dose CT screening
        
          **Criteria (NELSON, NLST trials):** Age 50–75 with ≥30 pack-year smoking history; annual LDCT × 3 years (then individualised)
          **Efficacy:** 24% mortality reduction (NLST); improved with shorter screening interval in high-risk subgroups
          **NZ access:** Not widely funded; opportunistic screening in high-risk smokers often done privately or in specialist centres; discuss benefits/harms
          **Nodule management:** Fleischner Society 2024 guidelines govern follow-up (8 mm rapid follow-up/investigation)
        
        #### Primary prevention
        
          **Smoking cessation:** Gold standard; absolute risk reduction ~20–30% per decade after quitting
          **Environmental exposures:** Minimize asbestos, radon (home testing/mitigation), occupational silica/diesel
          **Air quality:** Avoid high-pollution areas; air filtration in homes if living in high-pollution zone
          **Diet/exercise:** Mediterranean diet, regular exercise; limited direct evidence but cardiopulmonary benefits

      🏥NZ-specific access & prognosis▾
      
        #### Publicly funded treatments (NZ DHBs 2025–2026)
        
          **Standard chemotherapy:** Platinum-based doublets (cisplatin + pemetrexed/gemcitabine) for all stages
          **Targeted therapies (molecular-driven):**
            
              - EGFR TKIs (erlotinib, gefitinib): funded for EGFR-mutant adenocarcinoma
              - ALK inhibitors (crizotinib, alectinib): funded for ALK-rearranged NSCLC
              - Ongoing funding expansion: BRAF inhibitors, KRAS G12C inhibitors pending 2026 approvals

          **Immunotherapy:** Checkpoint inhibitors (pembrolizumab, atezolizumab, durvalumab) increasingly funded for stage III–IV; adjuvant durvalumab for resected stage II–III funded since 2024
          **Radiation:** External beam & stereotactic radiotherapy funded; access varies by DHB
        
        #### Private access (costs NZD)
        
          **Emerging agents:** Newer checkpoint inhibitors (nivolumab, atezolizumab monotherapy off-label), newer EGFR TKIs (osimertinib for EGFR mutation-positive), multi-kinase inhibitors (~$5000–15,000/month private)
          **Clinical trials:** Enrolled patients access new agents via trials (no direct cost); access varies by centre
        
        #### Prognosis & survival (NZ data)
        
          **Overall 5-year survival:** ~20–25% (all stages); improved to ~30% with expanding immunotherapy access
          **Stage-dependent:** Stage I ~50%, Stage II ~35%, Stage III ~10%, Stage IV ~5%
          **Molecular subgroup impact:** EGFR-mutant adenocarcinoma median OS ~3–5 years (vs 1–2 years chemotherapy); ALK-rearranged similar; KRAS G12C-mutant improving with new agents
          **Immunotherapy impact:** Checkpoint inhibitors extend median OS by ~3–6 months; select patients with PD-L1 >50% gain most benefit

      💡Clinical pearls & pitfalls▾
      
        #### Pearls
        
          ✦**Molecular testing is standard of care for NSCLC.** EGFR, ALK, PD-L1 must be tested; directs therapy selection & improves outcomes significantly.
          ✦**Adenocarcinoma is rising in never-smokers.** Do not assume all lung cancers are smoking-related; young, never-smoker with lung cancer warrants aggressive molecular testing.
          ✦**EGFR-mutant tumours are chemotherapy-naive first-line.** EGFR TKI monotherapy superior to chemotherapy in mutant-positive disease; improves OS & tolerability.
          ✦**ALK-rearranged NSCLC requires CNS prophylaxis.** High risk of brain metastases; use CNS-penetrant inhibitors (alectinib preferred); prophylactic PCI considered.
          ✦**PD-L1 high (>50%) favours checkpoint inhibitor monotherapy.** Better OS & fewer toxicities than chemo + immunotherapy in fit patients.
          ✦**Adjuvant durvalumab is now standard for resected stage II–III.** Improves disease-free survival; consider for all fit patients post-surgery.
          ✦**SCLC is aggressive; early referral to oncology critical.** Median OS ~12 months ED-SCLC; rapid chemo response but early recurrence expected.
          ✦**Incidental lung nodules are common; context guides follow-up.** Fleischner 2024 criteria; 8 mm rapid investigation.
        
        #### Pitfalls
        
          **Diagnosing NSCLC without molecular testing.** Delays targeted therapy initiation; results in chemotherapy-first strategy with inferior outcomes in driver-mutant disease.
          **Assuming all adenocarcinoma patients are smokers.** Never-smoker phenotype increasing; assumes bias → potentially delayed diagnosis in young patients.
          **Overlooking brain metastases in high-risk subtypes.** ALK/ROS1-rearranged should get brain MRI; CNS involvement up to 50% if not screened.
          **Delaying curative-intent surgery in resectable stage I–II.** Surgery offers best outcomes (~50–70% 5-year survival); avoid watching small peripheral nodules indefinitely.
          **Inadequate staging workup.** All stage III–IV require brain MRI, PET-CT, molecular testing; incomplete staging delays treatment, misses metastases.
          **Missing paraneoplastic syndromes.** SIADH, Cushing, LEMS associated with lung cancer; early recognition improves management & diagnosis timing.
