## Overview


Obesity is a chronic, relapsing, multifactorial disease in which a defended higher body-weight set-point resists loss through compensatory hunger and metabolic adaptation. Treating it as a moral failing is both wrong and ineffective; the consultation should be non-judgmental and complication-focused.

   **Measure BMI and waist circumference** - central adiposity adds risk at any BMI.
   **Use ethnicity-specific thresholds** - cardiometabolic risk rises at lower BMI in Asian populations; consider Māori and Pacific body composition.
   **Assess complications** (the "ABCD" staging idea): type 2 diabetes, OSA, MASLD, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, osteoarthritis, PCOS, mental health, cancer risk.
   **Screen for secondary/contributing factors:** hypothyroidism, Cushing’s (rare), weight-promoting drugs, depression, binge eating, sleep, medications.

      🥗Lifestyle foundation▾
      
   **Dietary change** with a sustained energy deficit - the specific diet matters less than adherence; build it around the patient’s preferences and culture.
   **Physical activity** aids weight maintenance and cardiometabolic health more than weight loss itself.
   **Behavioural support**, sleep, and addressing disordered eating and mental health.
   Set realistic goals: even **5-10% loss** meaningfully improves glycaemia, BP, lipids and OSA.
  
  Weight regain is physiology, not relapse of characterMetabolic adaptation defends lost weight - this is why long-term pharmacological or surgical support is often needed, just as for other chronic diseases.

      💉Pharmacotherapy▾
      Consider when BMI ≥30 (or ≥27 with complications), alongside lifestyle. See the Weight Management Medications explainer for detail.

   #### GLP-1 receptor agonistsSemaglutide, liraglutide - reduce appetite; ~15% weight loss with semaglutide; cardiovascular benefit
   #### Dual GLP-1/GIP agonistTirzepatide - up to ~20-22% weight loss in trials
   #### OthersNaltrexone-bupropion, orlistat - more modest; access and funding vary in NZ
   #### CaveatsGI side effects, cost/funding, and weight regain on stopping - these are long-term treatments

      🔪Bariatric surgery▾
      
   Most effective durable treatment for severe obesity; consider at **BMI ≥40, or ≥35 with complications** (lower thresholds with type 2 diabetes / for some ethnicities).
   Sleeve gastrectomy and Roux-en-Y bypass produce large, sustained loss and frequently **remission of type 2 diabetes**.
   Requires lifelong **micronutrient supplementation and monitoring** (iron, B12, calcium, vitamin D, thiamine) and dietetic follow-up.

   ✦**Lead with complications, not the number** - it sets a treatable, non-judgmental frame.
   ✦**5-10% loss** already delivers major metabolic benefit.
   ✦**Incretin therapies need to be ongoing** - stopping reverses the loss.
   ✦**Post-bariatric patients** need lifelong nutritional monitoring.
