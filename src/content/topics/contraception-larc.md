## Overview


Long-acting reversible contraception is the most effective contraception available and should be offered first because efficacy does not depend on the user remembering anything.

   #### Etonogestrel implant (Jadelle in NZ)**Duration:** up to 5 years**Efficacy:** >99%, the most effective method**Main issue:** unpredictable bleeding (the commonest reason for removal) - counsel up front
   #### Levonorgestrel IUS (Mirena/Kyleena)**Duration:** 5-8 years; also treats heavy menstrual bleeding and gives endometrial protection**Bleeding:** lighter over time, often amenorrhoea
   #### Copper IUD**Duration:** 5-10 years; hormone-free**Also:** most effective emergency contraception (within 5 days)**Issue:** heavier, more painful periods
   #### DMPA injection (Depo-Provera)**Duration:** 12-weekly**Issues:** delayed return of fertility (up to a year), reversible BMD loss; weight gain in some
  
  Offer LARC to everyoneNulliparity and young age are not contraindications to an IUD. The main barrier is clinician hesitancy, not patient suitability.

      💊Combined hormonal contraception▾
      The combined oral contraceptive (COC), patch and ring contain oestrogen + progestogen. They are effective when used perfectly but typical-use failure is ~9%/year because they depend on adherence. The oestrogen component drives both the non-contraceptive benefits (cycle control, acne, less benign breast disease, lower ovarian/endometrial cancer risk) and the key risks.

  #### Absolute contraindications (UKMEC 4) - the ones to memorise
  
   **Migraine with aura** - at any age (ischaemic stroke risk)
   **Current/past VTE**, known thrombogenic mutation
   **Smoking ≥15/day at age ≥35**
   **BP ≥160/100**; established cardiovascular/cerebrovascular disease
   **<6 weeks postpartum and breastfeeding**; major surgery with immobilisation
   **Breast cancer** (current); active liver disease
  
  Always ask about migraine aura before a COCIt is the single most commonly missed UKMEC 4. Aura = focal neurological symptoms (visual, sensory, dysphasic) preceding headache.

      🔹Progestogen-only & barrier methods▾
      The **progestogen-only pill (POP)** is the default when oestrogen is contraindicated - safe in breastfeeding, migraine with aura, VTE history, and most cardiovascular disease. Desogestrel POPs inhibit ovulation and have a 12-hour missed-pill window (vs 3 hours for older POPs). Irregular bleeding is the main drawback.

  **Barrier methods** (condoms) are the only methods that also reduce STI transmission - recommend alongside any hormonal method where STI risk exists. Typical-use efficacy is lower (~82%).

  **Fertility awareness** methods can work with commitment and training but have high typical-use failure.

  Quick-start the ongoing methodDon’t send someone away with only emergency contraception - start or arrange an ongoing method at the same visit.

      🧭Choosing & counselling▾
      #### A practical approach
  
   **Lead with efficacy and LARC**, then narrow by eligibility and patient priorities (bleeding pattern, hormone-free preference, STI cover, future fertility timing).
   **Screen for oestrogen contraindications** with BP, migraine aura, VTE history, smoking/age.
   **Counsel on the expected bleeding pattern** - unanticipated bleeding is the main reason methods are abandoned.
   **Quick-start** any method when you are reasonably sure the person is not pregnant, with appropriate bridging.
   Provide **STI prevention** advice and condoms alongside.

   ✦**Efficacy first, then fit.** Start the conversation at the most effective end (LARC) and work down.
   ✦**Migraine with aura = no oestrogen,** at any age.
   ✦**The POP is the safe default** when oestrogen is out.
   ✦**Bleeding counselling** keeps a method in place - set expectations before insertion.
