#### When to suspect IBD
      
        - Chronic diarrhoea (>4 weeks), especially with blood or mucus (UC)
        - Abdominal pain + weight loss + fatigue (Crohn's)
        - Perianal disease (fistula, fissure, abscess) — Crohn's until proven otherwise
        - Iron deficiency anaemia, elevated inflammatory markers without clear cause
        - Extraintestinal manifestations: uveitis, episcleritis, arthropathy, erythema nodosum, pyoderma gangrenosum, PSC
      
      #### Investigations
      
        **Faecal calprotectin:** first-line non-invasive test; >250 µg/g = likely active inflammation, refer for colonoscopy
        **Stool MCS + C. diff:** exclude infection before attributing to IBD
        - FBC, CRP, ESR, albumin, iron studies, B12, folate, LFTs, renal function
        - TPMT and NUDT15 genotyping before starting thiopurines
        **Colonoscopy with biopsies:** gold standard for diagnosis, extent and histological confirmation
        - MRI enterography (Crohn's): small bowel extent, fistulae, strictures, perianal disease

    💊Medical therapy — UC▾
    
      #### Mild-to-moderate UC
      
        **5-ASA (mesalazine):** rectal foam or enema for proctitis/left-sided; oral for extensive disease. Mainstay of mild-moderate UC.
        - Combination oral + rectal mesalazine superior to either alone
        - Oral prednisolone 40 mg/day for flares — taper over 8 weeks; do not use as maintenance
        - Budesonide MMX 9 mg for mild-moderate extensive UC (less systemic absorption)
      
      #### Moderate-to-severe UC / steroid-dependent or refractory
      
        **Thiopurines** (azathioprine 2–2.5 mg/kg or 6-mercaptopurine) — steroid-sparing maintenance; check TPMT before starting; 3–6 months onset
        **Anti-TNF** (infliximab, adalimumab) — first-line biologic; infliximab preferred for acute severe UC
        **Vedolizumab** (anti-integrin) — gut-selective; good safety profile; preferred if infection concern
        **Ustekinumab** (anti-IL12/23) — effective in moderate-severe UC
        **JAK inhibitors** (tofacitinib, upadacitinib) — rapid onset; thrombosis and infection risk; caution in cardiovascular risk
      
      Acute severe UC — hospitalStool frequency >6/day + systemic toxicity (fever, tachycardia, anaemia, raised CRP) = Truelove & Witts criteria for acute severe UC. IV hydrocortisone 100 mg 6-hourly; rescue infliximab or IV ciclosporin if no response by day 3; colectomy if failure by day 7.

    💊Medical therapy — Crohn's▾
    
      #### Remission induction
      
        **Prednisolone** 40 mg/day — effective for luminal Crohn's; taper over 8–12 weeks
        **Budesonide** 9 mg/day — for ileocaecal Crohn's; less systemic effect; 8-week course
        **Exclusive enteral nutrition (EEN):** first-line for paediatric Crohn's; 8-week liquid diet highly effective
        5-ASA is *not* effective in Crohn's beyond mild colonic disease — do not use routinely
      
      #### Maintenance therapy
      
        **Thiopurines** — check TPMT/NUDT15; azathioprine 2–2.5 mg/kg; monitor FBC and LFTs
        **Methotrexate** — 15–25 mg SC weekly; alternative to thiopurines; folate supplementation
        **Anti-TNF** (adalimumab, infliximab) — early use in moderate-severe Crohn's, penetrating/fistulising disease, perianal disease
        **Ustekinumab** — effective in Crohn's; good safety and tolerability; preferred if previous anti-TNF failure
        **Vedolizumab** — approved for moderate-severe Crohn's; slower onset than anti-TNF
        **Risankizumab** (anti-IL23p19) — effective in moderate-severe Crohn's, 2023 approval
      
      #### Perianal Crohn's
      Complex perianal fistulae: combined surgical (seton drainage) + medical (anti-TNF ± azathioprine). MRI pelvis to characterise anatomy. Proctology + gastroenterology co-management.

    📈GP monitoring & long-term care▾
    
      #### Monitoring on thiopurines
      
        - FBC fortnightly for 3 months, then 3-monthly — leukopenia is the main risk
        - LFTs every 3 months
        - Annual skin check — significantly increased non-melanoma skin cancer risk; sun protection, dermatology if suspicious
      
      #### Monitoring on biologics
      
        - TB and hepatitis B screen before starting anti-TNF
        - Live vaccines contraindicated on biologics and immunosuppressants
        - Faecal calprotectin + CRP at each clinical review — tracks mucosal inflammation
      
      #### Cancer surveillance
      
        - Colonoscopy starting 8–10 years after IBD symptom onset (earlier for PSC)
        - Surveillance frequency based on endoscopic extent and activity: every 1–5 years
      
      #### Vaccinations
      
        - Influenza (annual), pneumococcus, Hep A and B, COVID-19, Tdap
        - HPV for all women <45 and men on immunosuppression
        - Avoid live vaccines (MMR, varicella, yellow fever) on immunosuppressants
