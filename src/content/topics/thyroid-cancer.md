#### Papillary thyroid cancer (PTC)
        
          **Frequency:** 80 percent of thyroid cancers.
          **Behaviour:** Slow growth, often spreads to lymph nodes (40 percent), rarely to distant sites.
          **Prognosis:** 10-year survival >95 percent (excellent).
          **RET/PTC fusion:** Indicates radiation exposure (e.g. Chernobyl).
        
        #### Follicular thyroid cancer (FTC)
        
          **Frequency:** 10-15 percent.
          **Behaviour:** Vascular invasion; spreads to bone, lung, brain (more distant spread than PTC).
          **Prognosis:** 10-year survival 80-90 percent.
        
        #### Medullary thyroid cancer (MTC)
        
          **Frequency:** 3-5 percent.
          **Genetics:** 25 percent familial (RET mutation); part of MEN 2A/2B syndromes.
          **Behaviour:** Earlier aggressive spread; produces calcitonin (tumour marker).
          **Prognosis:** 10-year survival 50 percent (worse than PTC).
        
        #### Anaplastic thyroid cancer (ATC)
        
          **Frequency:** 1-2 percent.
          **Behaviour:** Highly aggressive; rapid growth, distant metastases.
          **Prognosis:** Median survival 
        
        #### Primary thyroid lymphoma
        
          - Rare; usually MALT lymphoma; associated with Hashimoto's thyroiditis.

      📋Diagnosis and staging▾
      
        #### Initial presentation
        
          - Thyroid nodule (palpable or incidental on imaging); lymph node enlargement in neck; distant metastases (lung, bone).
        
        #### Evaluation
        
          **Thyroid ultrasound:** Assess nodule size, echogenicity, vascularity, lymph nodes.
          **FNA (fine-needle aspiration):** Bethesda classification (non-diagnostic, benign, atypia, follicular neoplasm, suspicious, malignant).
          **TSH:** Usually normal in cancer; elevated may suggest malignancy risk.
          **MTC marker:** Calcitonin, CEA (MTC); thyroglobulin post-ablation (PTC, FTC).
        
        #### Staging (TNM)
        
          Stage I (PTC  Any T, any N, no distant metastases; excellent prognosis.
          Stage II (PTC  Distant metastases (M1).
          **Stage I-IV (PTC >45y and FTC):** Based on tumour size, nodal involvement, metastases.

      🔪Treatment▾
      
        #### Surgery (thyroidectomy)
        
          **Total thyroidectomy:** Removes entire thyroid; allows for radioiodine ablation and thyroglobulin monitoring.
          **Extent of lymph node dissection:** Central and/or lateral neck dissection based on nodal involvement.
        
        #### Radioactive iodine (RAI) ablation
        
          **Indication:** PTC/FTC with distant metastases, nodal metastases, or high-risk features; not used for MTC or ATC.
          **Preparation:** Thyroid hormone withdrawal (hypothyroidism) or recombinant TSH (rhTSH) to stimulate iodine uptake.
          **Dose:** 150 mCi for remnant ablation; higher doses for metastatic disease.
          **Side effects:** Salivary gland dysfunction, pulmonary fibrosis (with repeated doses), secondary malignancy (rare).
        
        #### TSH suppression therapy
        
          **Levothyroxine:** High-dose (usually TSH 0.1-0.5 mIU/L) to suppress TSH-stimulated growth of any remaining cancer cells.
          **Duration:** Lifelong for PTC/FTC with metastases or high-risk features; may reduce dose after 5-10 years if disease-free.
        
        #### MTC and ATC
        
          **MTC:** Total thyroidectomy; consider prophylactic neck dissection. Targeted therapy (vandetanib, cabozantinib) for advanced disease.
          **ATC:** Multimodal: surgery, external beam radiotherapy (EBRT), chemotherapy. Survival still poor; consider clinical trials.

      📊Follow-up and surveillance▾
      
        #### PTC and FTC (low-risk)
        
          **TSH:** Target 1-2 mIU/L (reduced suppression after 5 years if disease-free).
          **Thyroglobulin:** Undetectable on levothyroxine therapy is excellent; detectable may indicate recurrence.
          **Imaging:** Neck ultrasound annually x5 years; further imaging if thyroglobulin detectable.
        
        #### PTC and FTC (high-risk)
        
          **TSH:** Target 0.1-0.5 mIU/L (more suppressive).
          **Thyroglobulin + anti-thyroglobulin antibodies:** Periodic monitoring.
          **Imaging:** Neck ultrasound + whole-body imaging (RAI scan, FDG-PET, or CT) at intervals.
        
        #### MTC
        
          **Calcitonin and CEA:** Baseline and periodically; doubling time predicts prognosis.
          **Imaging:** Neck imaging, consider whole-body scan if elevated calcitonin.
          **Genetic screening:** All MTC patients should have RET testing; family members of RET-positive patients should be screened (prophylactic thyroidectomy if mutation detected in childhood).
