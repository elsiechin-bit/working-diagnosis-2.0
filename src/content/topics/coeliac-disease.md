#### Who to test
      Test for coeliac disease in anyone with:

        - GI symptoms: chronic diarrhoea, bloating, abdominal pain, steatorrhoea, unexplained weight loss
        - Unexplained iron deficiency anaemia, folate or vitamin B12 deficiency
        - Unexplained elevated transaminases
        - Type 1 diabetes, autoimmune thyroid disease, Down syndrome, Turner syndrome, Williams syndrome
        - First-degree relatives of confirmed coeliac disease
        - Dermatitis herpetiformis, unexplained osteoporosis, infertility, recurrent miscarriage
      
      Must be eating glutenSerology and biopsy are only valid when the patient is on a normal gluten-containing diet. A gluten challenge (1–2 slices of bread daily for ≥6 weeks) is required if they have already started a GFD before testing.

      #### Serological pathway
      
        **tTG-IgA + total IgA** — first line. Total IgA identifies IgA deficiency (~1 in 50 coeliac patients)
        If IgA deficient: use **DGP-IgG** (deamidated gliadin peptide IgG) or tTG-IgG
        - EMA (endomysial antibody) — highly specific; used for confirmation
        - HLA-DQ2/DQ8 testing — high NPV; useful when serology and biopsy are discordant, or to exclude coeliac in a first-degree relative
      
      #### Biopsy
      
        - Upper endoscopy with duodenal biopsy — gold standard for confirmation
        - Required before initiating GFD (unless paediatric with very high tTG-IgA >10× ULN + positive EMA)
        - 4 biopsies from D2 and 2 from duodenal bulb — increases diagnostic yield

    🌾Gluten-free diet▾
    
      #### What to avoid
      
        **Gluten-containing grains:** wheat (including spelt, kamut, durum), barley, rye
        - Oats — contain avenin; most patients tolerate uncontaminated oats but introduction should be gradual and monitored
        - Hidden gluten: sauces, soups, soy sauce, processed meats, medications, communion wafers
      
      #### Safe grains and starches
      
        - Rice, corn, quinoa, millet, buckwheat, amaranth, teff, sorghum
        - Potatoes, tapioca, arrowroot
        - All fresh meat, fish, vegetables, fruit, legumes, dairy, eggs are naturally gluten-free
      
      #### Dietitian referral
      Refer to a dietitian with coeliac disease experience at diagnosis. GFD is complex and adherence requires education, label-reading skills and management of cross-contamination. Poor adherence is the most common cause of persistent symptoms.

      Cross-contaminationEven trace amounts of gluten (20 ppm threshold) can cause mucosal injury. Shared toasters, chopping boards, colanders and spreads are common sources. Dedicated kitchen equipment may be needed in shared households.

    🧪Nutritional deficiencies▾

        DeficiencyMechanismAction

          FolateJejunal malabsorptionSerum folate; folate 5 mg/day if deficient
          Vitamin D + CalciumProximal small bowel; associated secondary hyperparathyroidism25-OH vitamin D; DEXA scan at diagnosis if deficient
          Vitamin B12Terminal ileal absorption usually preserved; deficiency in severe diseaseSerum B12 if macrocytosis or neurological symptoms
          Zinc, magnesiumDiarrhoeal loss + malabsorptionReplace if symptomatic or confirmed deficient

      Most deficiencies resolve with strict GFD within months — re-check at 12 months. Supplementation may be needed long-term if adherence is imperfect.

    📈Monitoring & complications▾
    
      #### Follow-up
      
        - Repeat tTG-IgA at 6–12 months — should normalise on strict GFD (may take up to 2 years)
        - FBC, iron, folate, B12, vitamin D, LFTs at 12 months and then annually
        - DEXA scan at diagnosis if vitamin D deficient or symptoms >5 years before diagnosis
        - Annual dietitian review ideally; symptom review every 12 months
      
      #### Persistent symptoms on GFD — causes
      
        **Inadvertent gluten ingestion** — most common; detailed dietary review
        - Irritable bowel syndrome (common coexistence)
        - Microscopic colitis, lactose intolerance, small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO)
        - Refractory coeliac disease (RCD) — rare; specialist management; risk of enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATL)
      
      #### Long-term risks
      
        - Osteoporosis — especially if delayed diagnosis; DEXA monitoring
        - Small bowel lymphoma (EATL) — rare (~3×); risk reduced by strict GFD
        - Hyposplenism — vaccinate for encapsulated organisms (pneumococcus, Hib, meningococcus)
        - Other autoimmune disease — type 1 diabetes, thyroiditis; screen periodically
