## Overview


- Characterise the pattern, triggers, fluid and caffeine intake, and impact on quality of life
            **Bladder diary** over a few days is the single most useful tool
            **Urinalysis** to exclude infection and haematuria
            - Examination for pelvic organ prolapse, pelvic floor tone and (in men) prostate; assess post-void residual if overflow suspected
            - Review contributing factors: diuretics, constipation, diabetes, mobility, cognitive impairment, oestrogen deficiency
          
          Refer / investigateVisible haematuria, recurrent UTI, pelvic pain, suspected fistula, significant prolapse, neurological symptoms, palpable bladder or a suspicious mass warrant referral.

        💪Stress incontinence▾

            **Supervised pelvic floor muscle training** for at least 3 months is first-line and effective
            - Weight loss where relevant; treat chronic cough and constipation; consider continence pessaries or devices
            **Surgery** (mid-urethral sling, colposuspension) for persistent bothersome symptoms after conservative therapy
            - Duloxetine is a second-line pharmacological option where surgery is declined or unsuitable

        🚽Urge incontinence / overactive bladder▾

            **Bladder training** over at least 6 weeks, with caffeine reduction and sensible fluid management, is first-line
            **Antimuscarinics** (oxybutynin, solifenacin) reduce urgency but carry anticholinergic burden
            **Mirabegron** (beta-3 agonist) is preferred when anticholinergic load is a concern, especially in older adults
            - Refractory cases: refer for intravesical botulinum toxin or neuromodulation
            - Consider vaginal oestrogen in postmenopausal women with genitourinary symptoms
          
          Anticholinergic burden in older adultsAntimuscarinics add to total anticholinergic load and are associated with cognitive decline and falls in older people. Prefer bladder training and mirabegron, and review the whole medication list.

        🧓Overflow & functional incontinence▾

            **Overflow:** identify and treat the cause (obstruction, hypotonic bladder, neurological); may need catheterisation and urological referral
            **Functional** incontinence in frailty: optimise mobility and access to toileting, review medications, manage constipation and cognitive factors
