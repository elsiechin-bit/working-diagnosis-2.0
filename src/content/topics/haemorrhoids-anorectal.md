## Overview


Haemorrhoids are symptomatic vascular cushions, presenting with bright-red painless bleeding (on the paper/coating the stool), prolapse, itch or discomfort. Graded I-IV by prolapse. **Thrombosed external haemorrhoids** cause acute severe pain and a tender lump.

   **Conservative first:** fibre, fluids, stool softeners, avoid straining and prolonged toilet sitting; topical preparations give short-term symptom relief.
   **Office procedures** (rubber-band ligation, sclerotherapy) for persistent grade I-III.
   **Surgery** (haemorrhoidectomy) for large/grade IV or refractory disease.
   **Acutely thrombosed external pile:** if within ~72 h and very painful, excision helps; otherwise analgesia and conservative care.
  
  Bleeding is haemorrhoids only after you’ve excluded cancerChange in bowel habit, dark/mixed-in blood, weight loss, iron-deficiency anaemia, age or family history all mandate examination (PR) and colorectal investigation. Do not label bleeding as "just piles" on history alone.

      ⚡Anal fissure▾
      A tear in the anoderm causing **severe pain on and after defecation**, often with a streak of bright blood. Most are posterior midline; lateral or multiple fissures suggest secondary causes (Crohn’s, infection, malignancy).

   **Treat constipation** aggressively - soft, bulky stool is the cure foundation.
   **Reduce sphincter spasm:** topical GTN or diltiazem to relax the internal sphincter and improve blood flow for healing; warm baths.
   **Chronic/refractory:** botulinum toxin or lateral internal sphincterotomy - refer.

      🔴Abscess, fistula & the rest▾
      
   **Perianal abscess:** hot, tender, fluctuant swelling - needs **incision and drainage** (antibiotics alone are inadequate); refer urgently.
   **Anal fistula:** may follow an abscess; recurrent discharge - surgical assessment; consider Crohn’s.
   **Pruritus ani:** usually multifactorial; treat moisture, avoid over-cleaning and topical irritants, exclude dermatoses, threadworm and secondary causes.
   **Pilonidal disease, anal warts, proctalgia fugax** - recognise and direct to appropriate care.
   Always consider **anal cancer** with a persistent ulcer, mass or non-healing lesion.

   ✦**PR exam and red-flag triage** before blaming bleeding on piles.
   ✦**Soft stool** heals fissures and settles haemorrhoids.
   ✦**Abscess = drainage,** not antibiotics.
   ✦**Lateral/atypical fissure** → think Crohn’s or other secondary cause.
