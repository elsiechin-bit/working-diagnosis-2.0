          ### Where they actually differ
          
            **No population screening programme exists** anywhere  -  all use individual informed discussion rather than an organised recall, though EAU/ACS are moving toward risk-stratified detection.
            **Start age diverges:** NZ/UK focus on 50 - 69; ACS starts the discussion at 40 for very high-risk men; USPSTF recommends considering it from 55.
            **PSA threshold for referral:** NZ and AU use ~4 ng/mL; UK uses 3 ng/mL for ages 50 - 69; EAU uses age-specific thresholds as low as 2.5 ng/mL at age 50 - 59.

          ### Where they actually differ
          
            **Start age:** NZ (60 - 74) is the latest-starting programme; AU and UK start at 45 - 50; US guidelines moved to 45 (ACS 2018, USPSTF 2021).
            **Modality:** NZ, AU and UK use FIT exclusively in organised programmes. US guidelines uniquely offer colonoscopy, CT colonography and stool DNA as alternatives.
            **Interval:** Biennial FIT is standard across NZ/AU/UK; US guidelines also allow annual FIT and 10-yearly colonoscopy.

          ### Where they actually differ
          
            **Start age:** The USPSTF 2024 reversal to screening from 40 (biennial) is the biggest recent change. UK still starts at 50 (plans to extend to 47). NZ starts at 45 (self-referral from 40).
            **Interval:** UK uses three-yearly mammography  -  the longest interval internationally. NZ, AU and USPSTF recommend two-yearly for most.
            **Dense breast tissue:** US has federal notification requirements; NZ/AU/UK have no unified policy on routine supplemental imaging for average-risk women.

          ### Where they actually differ
          
            **HPV primary everywhere, different timing:** Australia led the switch in 2017; NZ followed in 2023; UK is fully HPV-primary  -  all replacing cytology as the first test.
            **Self-collection and equity:** NZ and Australia offer self-collection as a mainstream choice for all eligible people  -  a deliberate lever to close equity gaps.
            **Longer intervals:** HPV-negative results now permit ~5-year recall, with England moving from 3 to 5 years. WHO supports 5 - 10 years with a validated HPV test.
