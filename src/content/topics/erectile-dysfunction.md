## Overview


**Erectile dysfunction is not a symptom of vascular disease—it is a vascular disease**, and an independent cardiovascular risk factor. The penile arteries are small (~1-2 mm), so endothelial dysfunction manifests there first. Recent systematic reviews and meta-analyses confirm: ED is an independent predictor of coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, stroke, and cardiovascular mortality, even after adjusting for traditional risk factors.

          2024 Princeton IV and EAU 2025: ED is a risk-enhancing factor
          Men with ED, especially those borderline for ASCVD risk (10-year risk 5-7.5%), warrant CAC (coronary artery calcium) scoring to better stratify true CV risk. All organic ED merits full CV assessment: BP, lipids, glucose/HbA1c, smoking, weight, and a formal ASCVD risk calculator. Early recognition can prevent MI or stroke.

      🔍Causes▾
      
        Most ED in older men is organic and vascular, often with a psychological overlay; in younger men psychogenic causes dominate. The two frequently coexist.

            #### Vascular (most common organic)
            Atherosclerosis, endothelial dysfunction, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, smoking, obesity

            #### Neurogenic
            Diabetic neuropathy, spinal cord injury or disease, multiple sclerosis, post-pelvic/prostate surgery

            #### Hormonal
            Hypogonadism (low testosterone), hyperprolactinaemia, thyroid dysfunction

            #### Drug-induced
            Thiazides and beta-blockers, SSRIs and antipsychotics, finasteride, opioids, excess alcohol, recreational drugs

            #### Psychogenic
            Performance anxiety, depression, stress, relationship difficulties - typically sudden onset, situational, with preserved morning erections

            #### Anatomical / local
            Peyronie's disease, penile trauma, prior priapism

        #### History: organic vs psychogenic clues
        
          FeatureOrganicPsychogenic
          
            OnsetGradualSudden
            PatternConstant, all situationsSituational / intermittent
            Morning / nocturnal erectionsReduced or absentPreserved
            LibidoOften preserved (unless low testosterone)Variable; anxiety prominent
            ContextVascular risk factors, ageYounger, stress, relationship factors

        Use a validated tool (IIEF-5 / SHIM) to grade severity and track response. Review medications, alcohol and recreational drug use, mood, and the relationship. Examine: BP, BMI/waist, cardiovascular system, secondary sexual characteristics, and genitalia (testicular size, penile plaques of Peyronie's).

        #### Investigations
        
          **Fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipids** - diabetes and dyslipidaemia are common and modifiable
          **Morning total testosterone** (if libido low or fatigue) - if low, repeat with LH/FSH, prolactin, SHBG before treating
          **TSH** where thyroid disease is suspected
          **Cardiovascular risk stratification** in all organic ED: BP, 10-year ASCVD risk score (ACC/AHA calculator). Consider CAC score in borderline-risk men (5-7.5% 10-year risk)
          - PSA before initiating testosterone and per age/symptoms; do not treat isolated low testosterone without symptoms

      🏃First-line: lifestyle & reversible causes▾
      
        Lifestyle change is not a consolation prize - it is genuinely disease-modifying for vascular ED and improves response to medication.

          **Weight loss and regular aerobic exercise** - among the best-evidenced interventions for ED
          **Smoking cessation** and reducing excess alcohol
          **Optimise vascular risk:** blood pressure, lipids, glycaemic control
          **Review culprit medications** - switch a beta-blocker or thiazide, reconsider an SSRI, where feasible
          **Treat depression and address relationship factors**; offer psychosexual counselling, alone or alongside medication

      💊PDE5 inhibitors: efficacy, cardiovascular benefit & safety▾
      
        Phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors are first-line pharmacotherapy, effective in ~70% when used correctly. They enhance nitric-oxide signalling, which improves erectile function *and* has systemic vascular benefits. They **require sexual stimulation to work**—they are not aphrodisiacs.

        #### Cardiovascular outcomes: recent evidence
        2024 meta-analysis (13 RCTs, 1.26M subjects): PDE5i use associated with **22% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE)**, including MI, stroke and all-cause mortality. Mechanisms: improved endothelial function, reduced systemic inflammation, enhanced exercise capacity. Men with CVD and ED can often benefit from PDE5i treatment on CV grounds alone.

          Absolute contraindication: concurrent nitrates or nicorandil
          Can cause severe, refractory hypotension. Always ask about nitrate use (including patch, paste, spray). Caution with alpha-blockers (additive BP lowering), recent MI/stroke, unstable angina, severe hypotension, or uncontrolled arrhythmias.

        #### Optimising response: the "failed trial" pitfall
        
          **Adequate dosing:** Sildenafil 100 mg, tadalafil 20 mg. Do not settle for suboptimal doses.
          **Timing:** Sildenafil 30-60 min before activity on empty stomach; tadalafil flexible.
          **Sexual stimulation required** for effect; reassure patient this is normal.
          **Adequate trial:** 4-8 attempts at maximum tolerated dose before concluding "failure"—most "non-responders" were underdosed or poorly counselled.
          **Common side effects:** headache, facial flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion, transient blue-tinted vision (all usually mild and self-limiting).
          **Priapism warning:** An erection lasting >4 hours is a urological emergency; seek care immediately. Rare (~1:5000-10000), higher risk with alprostadil or second-line agents.
          **NAION:** Non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy; extremely rare. Counsel about sudden vision change.

          NZ access: Special Authority & pharmacist supply
          **Publicly funded:** Sildenafil via Special Authority for diabetes, prostate cancer/prostatectomy, spinal cord injury, MS, renal failure on dialysis (criteria vary; check current PHARMAC schedule). **Pharmacist protocol:** Sildenafil available from trained pharmacists (screening tool) for men aged 35-70 without CV contraindications; screening includes BP check, diabetes, cardiac history. Patients encouraged to notify GP. Most men buy PDE5i privately.

      🔧Testosterone replacement therapy (TRT)▾
      
        #### When to treat: biochemistry and symptoms
        **Only treat confirmed, symptomatic hypogonadism.** Diagnosis requires: (1) two morning total testosterone measurements (fasting, 8-10 am) both **Intracavernosal alprostadil (prostaglandin E1)** - effective (~70% response); requires injection training; risk of priapism and cavernosal fibrosis with repeated use
          **Intraurethral alprostadil** (pellet, MUSE) or topical preparations
          **Combination PDE5i + alprostadil** - for refractory cases; careful counselling on priapism risk
          **Vacuum erection devices** - non-pharmacological, safe, high acceptance rates in motivated patients; useful in CV contraindications
          **Penile prosthesis** - surgical, high satisfaction (>90%), reserved for refractory cases with adequate motivation and partner support
        
        #### When to refer to urology/andrology
        
          Young men (
          - Failure of first-line PDE5i despite optimal counselling and dosing
          - Contraindication to PDE5i or need for second-line pharmacotherapy
          - Peyronie's disease, penile anatomical abnormality, or prior penile trauma
          - Endocrine complexity: treatment-resistant hypogonadism, suspected hyperprolactinaemia, or pituitary pathology
          - Training for intracavernosal injection, device assessment, or prosthesis evaluation

      💡Clinical pearls & pitfalls▾
      
        #### Pearls
        
          ✦**ED is an independent CVD risk factor.** Recent meta-analyses confirm it predicts MI, stroke, and mortality. Consider CAC scoring in borderline-risk men (EAU 2025, Princeton IV).
          ✦**PDE5i reduce MACE by 22%.** Men with both ED and CVD benefit on CV grounds alone, not just sexual function.
          ✦**Morning erections are a discriminator.** Preserved → psychogenic; absent → organic. Highly reliable.
          ✦**Nitrates are an absolute contraindication.** Ask about all nitrate forms (patch, paste, spray, sublingual). Combine with PDE5i → severe hypotension.
          ✦**Most "non-responders" were underdosed.** Sildenafil 100 mg, tadalafil 20 mg. Trial 4-8 times at max dose on empty stomach with stimulation before switching.
          ✦**Only treat confirmed, symptomatic hypogonadism.** Two fasting morning testosterone tests, both 
          ✦**Review medication list first.** Thiazides, beta-blockers, SSRIs, and finasteride are common, often reversible causes.
          ✦**Priapism is an emergency.** Erection >4 hours: cavernous aspiration, adrenergic injection. Warn patients; especially with second-line agents.
        
        #### Pitfalls
        
          **Overlooking ED as an independent CVD risk factor.** Treat it as a cardiac flag, not just a sexual complaint.
          **Co-prescribing PDE5i with nitrates or nicorandil** - check medication list, ask directly, and educate on the danger.
          **Accepting a single, non-fasting, afternoon testosterone level as diagnostic.** Require two fasting morning samples before diagnosing hypogonadism.
          **Starting TRT in asymptomatic men** with incidentally low testosterone.
          **Declaring PDE5i failure prematurely** (after one under-dosed trial). Recheck dose, timing, stimulation, meal status, and perform an adequate 4-8-attempt trial.
          **Treating isolated hypogonadism without exploring causes.** Is it primary (testis), secondary (pituitary/hypothalamic), or medication-induced (opioids, glucocorticoids)?
          **Ignoring psychological factors, mood, and relationship dynamics.** Psychogenic and organic ED often coexist; mental health support or couples counselling may be as important as medication.
          **Forgetting priapism counselling** - especially with injections or combination therapy. Patients must know: >4 hours = emergency.
