#### By MCV
          
            Microcytic (MCV  Iron deficiency, thalassaemia, anaemia of chronic disease, sideroblastic.
            **Normocytic (MCV 80–100):** Haemolysis, acute bleeding, bone marrow failure, renal disease (EPO deficiency), hypothyroidism.
            **Macrocytic (MCV >100):** B12 deficiency, folate deficiency, alcohol, reticulocytosis, antiretrovirals, myelodysplasia.
          
          #### Iron deficiency anaemia (IDA)
          
            **Causes:** Blood loss (GI bleed, heavy menstruation), poor intake (vegetarian diet), malabsorption (coeliac, post-gastrectomy).
            **Labs:** Low ferritin (
            **Treatment:** Oral iron (ferrous sulfate 200 mg daily) or IV (if intolerant or severe); treat underlying cause.
          
          #### B12 deficiency
          
            **Causes:** Pernicious anaemia (autoimmune), gastrectomy, fish tapeworm, strict vegan diet, metformin use.
            **Labs:** Low B12, elevated methylmalonic acid and homocysteine (if true deficiency).
            **Neurological complications:** Paresthesias, posterior column myelopathy, cognitive decline if untreated.
            **Treatment:** IM B12 injections (hydroxocobalamin 1000 micrograms monthly or every 2 months); oral supplementation less effective.

            **Treatment:** Oral folic acid 5 mg daily; correct within 2–4 weeks.

        🔴Haemolysis▾
        
          #### Intrinsic defects (RBC membrane, haemoglobin, metabolism)
          
            **Hereditary spherocytosis:** Osmotic fragility test positive; manage with folic acid, transfusions as needed, splenectomy if severe.
            **G6PD deficiency:** Triggered by fava beans, infections, sulfonamides; haemolytic episodes; manage triggers.
            **Sickle cell disease:** Vasoocclusive crisis, acute chest syndrome, stroke risk; hydroxyurea, transfusions, pain management.
          
          #### Extrinsic (immune, microangiopathic)
          
            **Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (AIHA):** Warm antibodies (IgG) typical; positive DAT (Coombs); corticosteroids first-line.
            **Microangiopathic (MAHA):** Mechanical fragmentation of RBCs; causes: TTP, HUS, DIC, prosthetic valve.
          
          #### Labs confirming haemolysis
          
            - High reticulocyte count (>100 × 10⁹/L).
            - Elevated LDH, low haptoglobin, elevated indirect bilirubin.
            - DAT (Direct Antiglobulin Test / Coombs) positive in immune haemolysis.

        ❓Symptoms and when to investigate▾
        
          #### Symptoms
          
            - Dyspnoea, fatigue, palpitations, chest pain, syncope, pallor, jaundice (haemolysis).
            - Neurological (B12): paresthesias, ataxia, cognitive changes.
