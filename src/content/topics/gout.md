#### Diagnosis
      
        - Clinical: acute onset severe joint pain (often nocturnal), warmth, erythema, swelling  -  first MTP joint (podagra) most classic
        - Joints: first MTP, ankle, knee, wrist, finger joints, olecranon bursa
        - MSU crystals in synovial fluid (needle-shaped, negatively birefringent under polarised light)  -  gold standard
        - Dual-energy CT or ultrasound (double-contour sign) when diagnosis uncertain
        - Serum urate: do not use during acute attack to diagnose  -  often normal or low during flare
      
      #### Treatment of acute attack  -  use one agent; add if needed
      
        #### NSAIDs  -  first-line
          Naproxen 500 mg BD or indomethacin 50 mg TDS
          Start within hours of attack; continue 5 - 7 days
          Avoid in CKD, peptic ulcer, HF, anticoagulation
        
        #### Colchicine  -  first-line (equal to NSAIDs)
          1 mg immediately, then 0.5 mg 1 hour later (NZ regimen)
          Then 0.5 mg BD for 5 - 7 days
          Dose-reduce in CKD; diarrhoea is common dose-limiting effect
        
        #### Corticosteroids  -  when NSAIDs/colchicine contraindicated
          Prednisolone 30 - 40 mg/day for 3 - 5 days then taper
          Or intra-articular triamcinolone if single joint accessible
          Useful in CKD, anticoagulated patients, post-op
        
        #### Ice + rest
          Ice applied 20 - 30 min several times daily  -  clinically effective adjunct
          Rest the affected joint
          Hydration  -  adequate fluid intake reduces crystallisation

      Do not start or stop allopurinol during an acute attackStarting, stopping or dose-changing ULT during an acute attack mobilises urate crystals and prolongs or worsens the flare. Wait until the attack has fully resolved (≥2 - 4 weeks) before initiating or changing ULT dose.

    💊Urate-lowering therapy (ULT)▾
    
      #### When to start ULT
      
        - ≥2 attacks per year
        - Tophi (subcutaneous urate deposits)
        - Radiographic damage from gout
        - CKD stage 2+ with gout
        - Gout + urolithiasis
        - Consider after first attack if young (<40), very high urate (>0.54 mmol/L) or strong family history
      
      #### Allopurinol  -  first-line ULT
      
        Start at **50 - 100 mg/day** (low dose reduces flare risk on initiation)
        - Titrate up by 50 - 100 mg every 4 weeks, checking serum urate
        - Target urate <0.36 mmol/L (or <0.30 in tophaceous gout)
        - Many patients require 300 - 600 mg/day  -  do not stop titrating at 300 mg if target not reached
        **Dose reduce in CKD:** start at 50 mg/day; max dose based on eGFR
        - HLA-B*5801 testing before starting if: Thai, Han Chinese, Korean or Korean ancestry  -  risk of severe allopurinol hypersensitivity (SJS/TEN)
      
      #### Alternatives
      
        **Febuxostat** 80 - 120 mg/day  -  non-purine XO inhibitor; use if allopurinol contraindicated or intolerant; cardiovascular risk in ischaemic heart disease (use with caution)
        **Probenecid**  -  uricosuric; avoid in CKD, kidney stones; less commonly used
        **Benzbromarone**  -  available in some countries; not NZ-funded
      
      #### Flare prophylaxis when starting ULT
      
        Colchicine 0.5 mg BD (or daily if CKD) for **3 - 6 months** after starting ULT
        - Prevents mobilisation flares  -  essential; omission is a common error
        - If colchicine not tolerated: low-dose NSAID or prednisolone

    🥗Diet, lifestyle & secondary causes▾
    
      #### Diet modification (modest benefit  -  <0.06 mmol/L urate reduction)
      
        - Reduce: red meat, organ meat, shellfish, high-fructose corn syrup (soft drinks)
        - Limit alcohol  -  beer and spirits most potent; wine less so
        - Increase: water intake, low-fat dairy, coffee (mildly uricosuric), vitamin C (modest effect)
        - Cherry consumption  -  some evidence for flare prevention
        - Weight loss reduces serum urate significantly  -  most effective lifestyle intervention
      
      #### Medications that raise urate
      
        **Diuretics**  -  thiazides and loop diuretics; switch if possible (furosemide most impactful)
        **Aspirin (low-dose):** raises urate; do not stop for CV indications  -  manage gout with ULT instead
        **Ciclosporin, tacrolimus**  -  transplant patients; high gout risk
        **Losartan**  -  mildly uricosuric; prefer as antihypertensive in gout patients
        **SGLT2 inhibitors**  -  reduce urate; favourable in gout + diabetes/CKD

    📈Monitoring & pearls▾
    
      #### Monitoring on ULT
      
        - Serum urate 4 - 6 weeks after each dose change  -  target <0.36 mmol/L
        - Once at target: check urate and renal function every 6 months
        - LFTs at baseline and periodically on allopurinol
        - Flares in first 6 - 12 months of ULT are normal  -  do not stop ULT; treat the flare
      
      #### Pearls
      
        - Titrate allopurinol to the serum urate target  -  most patients are underdosed at 300 mg
        - Colchicine prophylaxis for 3 - 6 months prevents initiation flares  -  always prescribe when starting ULT
        - Serum urate is unreliable during an acute attack
        - Losartan and SGLT2 inhibitors have mild uricosuric properties  -  useful in gout + HTN/diabetes
        - Gout in Māori and Pacific peoples often presents younger, more severe and more polyarticular  -  screen aggressively and treat early
        - Do not wait for ≥2 attacks before starting ULT in young patients with very high urate or tophaceous disease
