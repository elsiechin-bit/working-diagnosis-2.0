#### Iron
                Routine

                RoleO₂ transport, energy metabolism. The commonest micronutrient deficiency worldwide.
                At riskMenstruating and pregnant women, GI blood loss, coeliac/IBD, vegetarians, frequent donors.
                DeficiencyFatigue, dyspnoea, pallor, pica, restless legs; microcytic anaemia when advanced.
                Test call**Ferritin + iron studies.** Ferritin is the best stores marker but is an acute-phase reactant - pair with CRP. Iron deficiency without anaemia is real and worth treating. New iron deficiency in a man or postmenopausal woman mandates a GI source workup.

                #### Calcium
                On indication

                RoleBone, neuromuscular and cardiac function; tightly regulated by PTH and vitamin D.
                DisorderedCKD, hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency, malabsorption, malignancy (hypercalcaemia).
                FeaturesLow: paraesthesiae, tetany, Chvostek/Trousseau. High: stones, bones, groans, psychiatric moans.
                Test call**Always correct for albumin** (or ionised Ca). Serum reflects regulation, not dietary intake or bone stores - it is not a screen for "low calcium diet". Test for symptoms, CKD, or abnormal bone profile.

                #### Magnesium
                On indication

                RoleCofactor in >300 reactions; needed for PTH action and potassium handling.
                At riskPPIs (long-term), loop/thiazide diuretics, alcohol, diarrhoea, refeeding.
                FeaturesOften silent; cramps, weakness, arrhythmia; refractory hypokalaemia and hypocalcaemia.
                Test callWorth checking with the at-risk drugs and in **treatment-resistant low K⁺ or Ca²⁺** - you often cannot correct those until magnesium is replaced. Serum underestimates total body stores.

                #### Zinc
                On indication

                RoleImmune function, wound healing, taste, growth.
                At riskMalabsorption, high-output stomas/fistulae, alcohol, restrictive diets, bariatric surgery.
                FeaturesPoor wound healing, acro-orificial dermatitis, alopecia, diarrhoea, taste change.
                Test callTest only with a **matching clinical picture or clear risk**. Levels fall in any acute illness (negative acute-phase) and with low albumin, so interpret with CRP. Not a fatigue screen.

                #### Iodine & selenium
                Rarely

                RoleIodine: thyroid hormone synthesis. Selenium: antioxidant, thyroid metabolism.
                ContextNZ soils are low in both; bread is iodine-fortified. Pregnancy raises iodine demand.
                Test callIndividual testing is rarely useful - both are population/intake issues. **Pregnant and breastfeeding women should take iodine supplements** per Ministry of Health advice rather than be tested. Don't order serum selenium routinely.

          💧Water-soluble vitaminsB12 · folate · thiamine · B6 · C▾

                #### Vitamin B12 (cobalamin)
                Routine

                RoleDNA synthesis, myelination, haematopoiesis.
                At riskOlder adults, vegans, metformin and long-term PPI use, pernicious anaemia, ileal/gastric surgery.
                DeficiencyMacrocytic anaemia, glossitis, neuropathy, subacute combined degeneration, cognitive change.
                Test callUseful with macrocytosis, neuropathy, or risk factors. **Treat neurological features even with low-normal levels** - deficiency can precede anaemia. If borderline, MMA/homocysteine clarify. Check folate alongside.

                #### Folate
                On indication

                RoleDNA synthesis; neural tube closure in early pregnancy.
                At riskPregnancy, alcohol, coeliac, methotrexate/anticonvulsants, poor diet.
                Test callCheck with macrocytic anaemia (always with B12). **Pre-conception folate is given, not tested.** RBC folate reflects stores better than serum but is rarely needed.

                #### Thiamine (B1)
                Treat, don't wait

                RoleCarbohydrate metabolism; critical for neuronal energy.
                At riskAlcohol dependence, hyperemesis, refeeding, bariatric surgery, chronic vomiting.
                DeficiencyWernicke's (confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia), beriberi, refeeding decompensation.
                Test call**Do not wait for a level - treat empirically.** Give parenteral thiamine before glucose in any at-risk patient; the assay is slow and Wernicke's is a clinical, reversible-if-treated emergency.

                #### Vitamin C & B6
                Rarely

                ContextVitamin C deficiency (scurvy) is uncommon but reappears in very restricted diets, alcohol and isolation - perifollicular haemorrhage, gum disease, poor healing.
                Test callDiagnose scurvy **clinically and treat** rather than assay. B6 testing is rarely indicated; toxicity (sensory neuropathy) from high-dose supplements matters more than deficiency.

          🧈Fat-soluble vitaminsA · D · E · K▾

                #### Vitamin D
                On indication

                RoleCalcium/phosphate homeostasis, bone mineralisation, neuromuscular function.
                At riskLimited sun exposure, dark skin, veiling, malabsorption, the housebound, exclusively breastfed infants.
                DeficiencyMostly asymptomatic; severe causes osteomalacia, bone pain, proximal myopathy; rickets in children.
                Test call**25-OH-D**, and only with a risk factor or relevant bone/calcium problem - it is explicitly not recommended as a population screen in well adults. In clear deficiency with limited sun, many simply replace. Avoid repeat testing of stable normals.

                #### Vitamins A, E & K
                Rarely

                ContextDeficiency in NZ is essentially confined to **fat malabsorption** - cystic fibrosis, cholestatic liver disease, short bowel, severe pancreatic insufficiency.
                FeaturesA: night blindness, xerophthalmia. E: neuropathy/ataxia. K: bleeding, raised INR.
                Test callTest only in established malabsorption (often specialist-led). For vitamin K, the relevant test is usually the **INR**, not a vitamin level. Beware vitamin A toxicity from supplements.

        Pitfalls

        ## Two traps and one rule
        
          Treat thiamine, don't wait for it
          In any alcohol-dependent, hyperemetic or refeeding patient, give parenteral thiamine before carbohydrate. The level comes back too late and Wernicke's encephalopathy is reversible only if treated early.

          The acute-phase trap
          Ferritin rises and iron, zinc and selenium fall during any inflammatory illness. A "normal" ferritin in an unwell patient can mask iron deficiency; a "low" zinc may simply reflect the illness. Always read these against CRP, or wait until recovery.

          The one rule
          Order a micronutrient test when a risk factor or syndrome makes deficiency plausible and the result would change management. Skip the broad "vitamin and mineral panel" in well people - it mostly buys borderline results, cost and patient anxiety.
