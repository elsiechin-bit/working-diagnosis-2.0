### Psychotherapy is the treatment

        The evidence is unambiguous: structured psychotherapy is the first-line treatment for BPD. Several modalities have randomized trial support:

          **Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT).** The best-evidenced. Combines weekly individual therapy, group skills training (mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, interpersonal effectiveness), and phone coaching. Reduces self-harm, suicide attempts, and hospitalization.
          **Mentalization-based treatment (MBT).** Focuses on restoring mentalizing under emotional arousal. Strong trial evidence, including long-term follow-up.
          **Transference-focused psychotherapy (TFP)** and **schema therapy.** Both have positive trials.
          **Good psychiatric management (GPM).** Importantly for generalists: structured, principles-based generalist care performs comparably to specialist therapies in trials. The active ingredients seem to be: a coherent framework, a reliable relationship, a focus on life outside therapy, and clinician steadiness.

        The GPM finding matters for GPs: you don't need to deliver DBT to be therapeutic. Consistency, validation, structure, and realistic expectations - delivered by a GP over years - are themselves treatment.

        ### Medication: adjunct, not cure

        No medication is approved for BPD, and guidelines (NICE, RANZCP) are clear that drugs should not be the primary treatment. The honest summary:

          - Antidepressants help comorbid major depression but do little for core BPD features.
          - Mood stabilizers and low-dose antipsychotics have modest evidence for impulsivity and anger, but effect sizes are small.
          - The real-world problem is accumulation: the average BPD patient ends up on 2 - 4 psychotropics, each added in crisis, none reviewed. Polypharmacy with weight gain, sedation, and metabolic harm is common. Regular medication review and deprescribing are core GP work here.
          - Benzodiazepines deserve special caution: they can disinhibit, are habit-forming in a population prone to substance misuse, and are dangerous in overdose combinations.

        ### Managing crises

        Crisis management principles:

          **Stay calm and validate first.** "This sounds unbearable" lands better than immediate problem-solving. Escalation usually reflects the feeling of not being heard.
          **Distinguish chronic from acute risk.** Many patients live with chronic suicidal ideation for years. The question in crisis is: what's changed? Acute-on-chronic risk (new loss, new plan, new intent, intoxication) needs urgent response; chronic ideation at baseline usually doesn't need admission.
          **Be cautious with hospitalization.** Brief admission can be life-saving in acute crisis, but prolonged or repeated admissions often worsen BPD - reinforcing crisis as the route to care and eroding outside-world functioning. Most guidelines favour brief, goal-defined admissions only.
          **Safety planning beats no-suicide contracts.** A concrete, collaboratively written plan: warning signs, self-soothing strategies, people to contact, crisis numbers, means restriction.


## Prognosis


The deepest misconception about BPD is that it's permanent. The longitudinal data say otherwise:

          - In the McLean Study of Adult Development, 85% of patients achieved remission (no longer meeting criteria) within 10 years; recurrence was uncommon.
          - The Collaborative Longitudinal Personality Disorders Study found similar trajectories: symptomatic improvement is the norm, not the exception.
          - Acute symptoms (self-harm, suicidality, impulsivity) remit fastest. Temperamental features (emotional sensitivity, abandonment fears, emptiness) improve more slowly.
          - The lag is functional recovery: work, relationships, and life-building recover more slowly than symptoms, and need active support.

        BPD has a better long-term symptomatic prognosis than bipolar disorder or recurrent depression. Tell your patients this. Hope is clinically active.
