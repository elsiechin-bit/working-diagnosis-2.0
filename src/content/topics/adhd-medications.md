#### Mechanism of action
        Stimulants increase dopamine and noradrenaline in the prefrontal cortex and striatum by blocking their reuptake. This enhances executive function, attention, impulse control, and motivation.

        #### Why they work in ADHD
        ADHD brains have reduced dopamine and noradrenaline signalling. Stimulants restore neurotransmitter levels to normalcy, allowing executive networks to function properly.

      🔬Methylphenidate▾
      
        #### Overview
        Selective dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor. First-line stimulant in many countries (NZ, UK, Australia).

        #### Formulations
        
          **Immediate-release (Ritalin, generic):** Onset 20-30 min, peak 1-2 hours, duration 4-6 hours. Useful for dosing flexibility; requires 2-3 doses/day.
          **Extended-release (Concerta, Equasym XL):** Onset 30-60 min, duration 8-12 hours. Convenient once-daily dosing; releases drug gradually.
        
        #### Dosing
        
          **Children:** Start 5-10 mg once or twice daily; increase by 5-10 mg weekly; typical 20-60 mg/day (up to 2 mg/kg/day).
          **Adults:** Start 5-10 mg; increase by 5-10 mg; typical 20-60 mg/day divided into doses.
        
        #### Side effects
        
          **Common:** Appetite suppression, insomnia, headache, increased HR/BP, nervousness.
          **Uncommon:** Growth delay (monitor height/weight in children), tics (rare exacerbation), psychosis (rare, high doses).
        
        #### Monitoring
        
          - Baseline: BP, HR, weight, height (children), medical history for cardiac risk.
          - At each dose increase and 1-3 monthly: BP, HR, weight, appetite, sleep, mood.
          - Annual: full cardiovascular and physical exam.

      🔬Amphetamine▾
      
        #### Overview
        Releases dopamine and noradrenaline, in addition to blocking reuptake. Slightly longer-acting and potentially more potent than methylphenidate.

        #### Formulations
        
          **Mixed amphetamine salts (Adderall):** Immediate and extended-release available.
          **Lisdexamphetamine (Vyvanse):** Prodrug; converted to d-amphetamine in body; onset 1-2 hours, duration 10-13 hours. Harder to abuse (must be swallowed, not snorted/injected).
        
        #### Dosing
        
          **Children:** Start 2.5-5 mg daily; increase by 5 mg weekly; typical 5-40 mg/day.
          **Adults:** Start 5-10 mg; titrate to 20-40 mg/day (max 70 mg/day).
        
        #### Advantages over methylphenidate
        
          - Longer duration of action; once-daily dosing often sufficient.
          - Some patients respond better; may try if methylphenidate ineffective.
        
        #### Side effects and monitoring
        
          - Similar to methylphenidate; may be slightly more stimulating.
          - Same baseline and periodic monitoring required.

      💊Non-stimulants▾
      
        #### Atomoxetine (Strattera)
        
          **Mechanism:** Selective noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor.
          **Onset:** Slower (2-4 weeks for full effect); not acute.
          **Dosing:** Start 40 mg daily; increase to 80-100 mg daily (max 120 mg).
          **Advantages:** Lower abuse potential; useful if stimulant intolerance, tics, or anxiety; better for mood symptoms.
          **Disadvantages:** Slower onset; less efficacy than stimulants for core ADHD symptoms in some patients.
        
        #### Guanfacine (Intuniv) and Clonidine (Kapvay)
        
          **Mechanism:** Alpha-2 adrenergic agonists; enhance prefrontal noradrenaline.
          **Onset:** Weeks; not acute.
          **Best for:** Hyperactivity, impulsivity, emotional regulation; less effective for inattention.
          **Side effects:** Sedation, hypotension, bradycardia; monitor BP and HR.
        
        #### Bupropion
        
          **Mechanism:** Dopamine and noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor; also antidepressant.
          **Useful if:** Comorbid depression, anxiety, or substance use risk.
          **Dosing:** 150-300 mg daily (divided); max 450 mg/day.

      ⚠️Safety and contraindications▾
      
        #### Absolute contraindications
        
          - Uncontrolled hypertension or severe cardiovascular disease (recent MI, arrhythmia, structural heart disease).
          - Monoamine oxidase inhibitor use (or within 14 days).
          - Hypersensitivity to the drug.
        
        #### Relative contraindications (use with caution)
        
          - Personal or family history of sudden cardiac death.
          - Uncontrolled tics or Tourette syndrome (stimulants may exacerbate).
          - Psychosis, bipolar disorder (may worsen mood symptoms).
          - Substance use disorder (stimulant abuse risk).
          - Severe anxiety (may worsen).
        
        #### Drug interactions
        
          - MAOIs: absolute contraindication.
          - Other stimulants, sympathomimetics: additive effects; avoid.
          - Tricyclic antidepressants: increased effect; monitor.
