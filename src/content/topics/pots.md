#### What is POTS?
        **Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS)** is a form of dysautonomia — dysfunction of the autonomic nervous system. On standing, the body fails to regulate heart rate and blood pressure adequately, causing excessive tachycardia and orthostatic symptoms (dizziness, syncope, presyncope).

        #### Normal orthostatic response
        
          - Gravity pools blood in lower extremities on standing
          - Baroreceptors detect drop in blood pressure
          - Sympathetic nervous system activated: heart rate increases by ~10-15 bpm, vasoconstriction maintains BP
          - Result: BP stays stable; minimal heart rate rise

        #### POTS pathophysiology
        
          **Problem:** Exaggerated heart rate response to standing (>120 bpm within 10 minutes) without proportional blood pressure drop (unlike true orthostatic hypotension)
          **Mechanisms (heterogeneous):**
          - Neuropathic: loss of sympathetic innervation in legs; reduced peripheral vasoconstriction
          - Hyperadrenergic: excessive sympathetic activation; high noradrenaline levels
          - Hypovolemic: low blood volume; reduced cardiac preload
          - Autoimmune/post-viral: antibodies against autonomic receptors; follow-up to infection (COVID-19, EBV, Lyme disease)

        #### Classification
        
          **Neuropathic POTS:** ~60%; distal small fibre neuropathy on skin biopsy; more fixed tachycardia
          **Hyperadrenergic POTS:** ~10-15%; high plasma noradrenaline; hypertension on standing; tremor, palpitations
          **Hypovolemic POTS:** ~15-20%; low plasma volume; diuretic use, deconditioning common
          **Unclear mechanism:** ~10-15%; no clear abnormality on testing

        #### Associated conditions
        
          - Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (connective tissue laxity)
          - Mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS)
          - Long COVID (post-viral dysautonomia)
          - ME/CFS (myalgic encephalomyelitis)
          - Fibromyalgia
          - Autoimmune thyroiditis

      📋Clinical presentation and diagnosis▾
      
        #### Typical symptoms
        
          **On standing (orthostatic phase):** Dizziness, lightheadedness, blurred vision, brain fog, weakness
          **Syncope or presyncope:** Fainting or near-fainting; often abrupt
          **Palpitations:** Awareness of rapid heartbeat; often prominent
          **Tremor, anxiety:** Hyperadrenergic symptoms
          **Heat intolerance:** Symptoms worse in warm environments, after meals, with exercise
          **Fatigue, post-exertional malaise:** Disproportionate tiredness after minimal activity
          **Cognitive symptoms:** Brain fog, difficulty concentrating (orthostatic intolerance during mental tasks)

        #### Diagnostic criteria (Consensus Criteria for POTS diagnosis)
        
          - Symptoms of orthostatic intolerance (dizziness, presyncope, syncope, palpitations, tremor, blurred vision)
          - Heart rate increase ≥120 bpm within 10 minutes of standing (or during 80° head-up tilt)
          **AND** absence of orthostatic hypotension (systolic BP drop 
          - Symptoms that improve with recumbency
          - Symptoms lasting ≥6 months

        #### Differentials to exclude
        
          **Orthostatic hypotension:** BP drops significantly on standing; different mechanism
          **Sinus tachycardia (secondary):** Infection, anaemia, hyperthyroidism, deconditioning, anxiety
          **Cardiac arrhythmia:** SVT, atrial tachycardia; ECG and Holter distinguish
          **Anxiety disorder:** Can coexist with POTS; symptoms similar but no HR spike on standing in pure anxiety
          **Conversion disorder:** Functional symptoms; diagnosis of exclusion

      🔍Assessment and investigations▾
      
        #### History and physical exam
        
          **Onset:** Gradual vs sudden? Viral prodrome? Trauma? Medications?
          **Triggers:** Heat, dehydration, prolonged standing, meals, exercise, menses
          **Associated symptoms:** Fever, weight loss, rashes (autoimmune screen); joint hypermobility (EDS); flushing (MCAS)
          **Orthostatic vitals:** Lie down 5 minutes, then stand 3 and 10 minutes; measure HR and BP at each point
          **Red flags:** Syncope with exertion, family history of sudden death (suggests cardiac); severe neurological symptoms (central autonomic disorder)

        #### Investigations (stepwise)
        
          **Full blood count:** Anaemia can worsen symptoms
          **TSH, B12, glucose:** Rule out secondary causes
          **ECG:** Assess for cardiac arrhythmia; usually normal in POTS
          **Holter monitor or event recorder:** If syncope with exertion; rule out arrhythmia
          **Echocardiogram:** If structural heart disease suspected; mostly reassuring in POTS
          **Supine and standing plasma catecholamines:** Optional; helps classify (high noradrenaline = hyperadrenergic)
          **Tilt table test:** Gold standard if diagnosis uncertain; reproduces symptoms and confirms excessive HR response
          **Autonomic testing battery (Advanced):** Includes Valsalva manoeuvre, deep breathing, cold pressor test; quantifies autonomic dysfunction
          **Skin biopsy:** Small fibre neuropathy assessment (neuropathic variant)

        #### When to refer for specialist assessment
        
          - Syncope with exertion (rule out cardiac)
          - Family history of sudden cardiac death
          - Diagnostic uncertainty
          - Refractory to first-line treatment
          - Complex presentation with systemic features (autoimmune, EDS)

      💊Management and treatment▾
      
        #### Non-pharmacological (first-line)
        
          **Fluid intake:** 2-3 litres daily; increase electrolytes (salt tablets or electrolyte drinks)
          **Compression garments:** Waist-to-toe compression stockings (30-40 mmHg) reduce pooling
          **Recumbent activities:** Lie down when possible; avoid prolonged standing
          **Slow positional changes:** Sit up slowly; dangle legs before standing; avoid jumping out of bed
          **Avoid triggers:** Heat exposure, large meals, alcohol, prolonged standing, excessive exercise
          **Graded exercise (cautiously):** Recumbent exercise (cycling, swimming) often tolerated; progressive return to upright activity
          **Sleep hygiene and stress:** Fatigue and anxiety worsen symptoms
          **Avoid rapid fluid loss:** Reduce diuretics if possible; manage any concurrent diarrhoea/vomiting promptly

        #### Combination therapy
        
          - Often more effective: midodrine + fludrocortisone, or midodrine + compression stockings
          - Tailored to POTS subtype (neuropathic, hyperadrenergic, hypovolemic)

        #### Prognosis and long-term outlook
        
          **Variable:** Some patients improve or remit; others have chronic symptoms
          **Post-viral POTS:** May improve with time; supportive care paramount
          **Neuropathic POTS:** Often progressive but stable on treatment
          **Hyperadrenergic:** Variable; may respond well to targeted therapy
          **Psychological impact significant:** Activity limitation, social isolation, anxiety; counselling and support groups important

      ⚠️Red flags and complications▾
      
        #### When to refer urgently
        
          - Syncope with exertion (cardiac event risk)
          - Family history of sudden cardiac death or arrhythmias
          - Chest pain or dyspnoea
          - Signs of structural heart disease on examination
          - Recurrent unexplained syncope despite treatment
          - Severe systemic symptoms (fever, rash, weight loss) suggesting autoimmune/infectious cause

        #### Complications of POTS
        
          **Deconditioning:** Avoidance of upright activity worsens function; cycle of deterioration
          **Injury from syncope:** Falls, head trauma from fainting
          **Medication side effects:** Hypertension from midodrine, hypokalaemia from fludrocortisone
          **Psychological comorbidities:** Anxiety, depression, PTSD from recurrent syncope
          **Thromboembolic risk:** Increased in some POTS cohorts; individualise anticoagulation assessment

        #### Driving safety
        
          - Syncope while driving is a safety risk; advise against driving until stabilised on treatment
          - Consider road safety assessment; notify NZTA if recurrent syncope
