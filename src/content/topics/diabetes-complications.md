#### Mechanisms of hyperglycaemic damage
          
            **Advanced glycation end products (AGEs):** Cross-link proteins; activate RAGE receptors; promote inflammation.
            **Polyol pathway:** Glucose converted to sorbitol; accumulates in tissues causing osmotic stress.
            **Protein kinase C (PKC):** Hyperglycaemia activates PKC; dysregulates gene expression, causes oxidative stress.
            **NADPH oxidase:** Generates reactive oxygen species (ROS); mitochondrial dysfunction.
          
          #### Duration and control
          
            - Complication risk increases with longer diabetes duration and poorer glycaemic control (HbA1c).
            - "Metabolic memory": Early poor control increases long-term complication risk even after improved control (DCCT legacy study).

        👁️Diabetic retinopathy▾
        
          #### Screening and grading
          
            **Non-proliferative (NPDR):** Microaneurysms, haemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton-wool spots; no neovascularization.
            **Proliferative (PDR):** Neovascularization of disc or periphery; risk of vitreous haemorrhage and retinal detachment.
            **Diabetic macular edema (DME):** Thickening of macula; vision threatening.
          
          #### Management
          
            **Prevention:** Tight glycaemic control (HbA1c 
            **NPDR:** Annual screening; intensify BP and glycaemic control.
            **PDR/DME:** Laser photocoagulation, intravitreal anti-VEGF (bevacizumab, ranibizumab), intravitreal corticosteroids, vitrectomy if vitreous haemorrhage.

        🫘Diabetic nephropathy and CKD▾
        
          #### Stages of nephropathy
          
            **Stage 1 (Hyperfiltration):** Elevated GFR; normal albuminuria; reversible with tight control.
            **Stage 2 (Silent):** Persistent microalbuminuria (30–300 mg/day); declining GFR begins.
            **Stage 3 (Overt):** Macroalbuminuria (>300 mg/day); progressive GFR decline; hypertension develops.
            **Stage 4 (ESRD):** GFR 
          
          #### Management
          
            **ACE-I or ARB:** Slows progression by 20–50%; first-line.
            **SGLT2 inhibitors:** (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) reduce GFR decline and albuminuria; now recommended for all with CKD.
            Tight BP ( Reduces albuminuria and slows GFR decline.
            **GLP-1 RA:** Modest renoprotection.

        ⚡Diabetic neuropathy▾
        
          #### Types
          
            **Distal symmetrical sensorimotor:** Most common; affects feet and hands; "glove and stocking" distribution.
            **Focal/mononeuropathy:** Single nerve (CN III palsy, median nerve, foot drop).
            **Autonomic:** Gastroparesis, diarrhoea, orthostatic hypotension, erectile dysfunction, hypoglycaemia unawareness.
          
          #### Management
          
            **Prevention:** Tight glycaemic control.
            **Pain relief:** Gabapentin, pregabalin, or duloxetine (antidepressant); topical capsaicin.
            **Foot care:** Inspection, proper footwear, early treatment of ulcers; prevent amputations.

        ❤️Macrovascular complications▾
        
          #### Overview
          Diabetes accelerates atherosclerosis; 2–4x higher risk of MI and stroke; similar risk as non-diabetic with prior MI (DCCT/UKPDS legacy).

          #### Management
          
            **Aspirin:** Primary prevention debated; secondary prevention standard.
            **Lipid management:** High-intensity statin; target LDL-C 
            **BP control:** 
            **GLP-1 RA:** (especially semaglutide, liraglutide with proven CV benefits) reduces MI and stroke in established CVD.
            **SGLT2 inhibitors:** Reduce heart failure risk; cardioprotective.
