#### Core work-up
          
            Symptom history and **IPSS**; a bladder diary if storage symptoms or nocturia predominate
            Examination including abdomen (palpable bladder) and a **digital rectal examination** of the prostate
            **Urinalysis** to exclude infection and haematuria
            - Renal function if chronic retention suspected; post-void residual where available
            **PSA** - discuss the pros and cons first; interpret in the context of prostate size and DRE

        Red flags - referVisible or persistent microscopic haematuria, abnormal or suspicious DRE, significantly raised PSA, palpable bladder or acute/chronic retention, renal impairment, recurrent UTI, or a hard irregular prostate. These suggest malignancy, obstruction or upper-tract risk and need urological assessment.

        🔁Causes and mimics▾

            **Overactive bladder** - storage symptoms without obstruction
            **Polyuria** from diabetes, diuretics or high fluid/caffeine intake; nocturnal polyuria
            **UTI or prostatitis** - dysuria, pain, systemic features
            **Prostate cancer** - abnormal DRE or raised PSA
            **Neurological** causes and medication effects (e.g. anticholinergics, opioids)

        💊Management▾
        
          #### Conservative
          
            - Reassurance and watchful waiting for mild or non-bothersome symptoms
            - Fluid timing, reduce evening fluids/caffeine/alcohol, bladder training, treat constipation, review contributing drugs
          
          #### Medical
          
            **Alpha-blockers** (tamsulosin, doxazosin) - first-line for bothersome voiding symptoms; rapid effect. Warn re postural hypotension and that they can complicate later cataract surgery (intra-operative floppy iris syndrome - tell the ophthalmologist).
            **5-alpha-reductase inhibitors** (finasteride, dutasteride) - for larger prostates; shrink the gland over months and reduce progression/retention. They **roughly halve PSA** - double the measured value when interpreting.
            **Combination** alpha-blocker + 5-ARI for larger prostates with significant symptoms
            **Antimuscarinic or beta-3 agonist** (mirabegron) for predominant storage/overactive bladder symptoms
            **PDE5 inhibitor** (tadalafil) where LUTS coexist with erectile dysfunction
          
          #### Surgical referral
          
            - Refractory symptoms, recurrent retention, stones, recurrent UTI or upper-tract changes - options include TURP and newer minimally invasive procedures

      Acute urinary retentionPainful inability to void with a palpable bladder needs prompt catheterisation. Start an alpha-blocker before a trial without catheter, and arrange urology follow-up.
