#### C. difficile infection
                Causal
              
              The cleanest cause-and-effect story in the field. Antibiotics deplete diversity, **C. difficile** overgrows and produces toxins. The proof of causation is therapeutic: **faecal microbiota transplant** restores diversity and cures roughly 85-90% of recurrent cases.

                #### Recurrent vulvovaginal & gut Candida, BV
                Probable
              
              Antibiotic-driven loss of protective **Lactobacillus** and gut commensals permits overgrowth of yeast and anaerobes - the everyday clinical echo of the same colonisation-resistance principle.

          🧱Gut barrier & bowel diseaseGI tract▾

                #### Inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's, UC)
                Probable
              
              Reduced diversity, fewer butyrate producers (e.g. **Faecalibacterium prausnitzii**) and a leakier barrier are consistent findings. Dysbiosis is part of the disease loop, though IBD is driven by host genetics and immune dysregulation too - the microbiome is a contributor, not the sole cause.

                #### Irritable bowel syndrome
                Probable
              
              Post-infectious IBS and the response of IBS-D to gut-selective **rifaximin** and of symptoms to the low-FODMAP diet point to a real microbial component within a multifactorial, brain-gut disorder.

                #### Colorectal cancer
                Probable
              
              Specific organisms - **Fusobacterium nucleatum**, colibactin-producing **E. coli**, enterotoxigenic **Bacteroides fragilis** - are enriched in tumours and promote carcinogenesis in models. Low-fibre, low-SCFA diets compound the risk.

          🦠Immune education: allergy & autoimmunityimmune▾

                #### Atopy: eczema, asthma, food allergy
                Probable
              
              Early-life diversity shapes immune tolerance. Caesarean birth, formula feeding and infant antibiotics - all of which alter early colonisation - track with higher atopy risk, supporting the **"old friends"** hypothesis.

                #### Type 1 diabetes & other autoimmunity
                Association
              
              Altered infant microbiota precede islet autoimmunity in cohort studies, and similar signatures appear in rheumatoid arthritis and MS. Plausible and actively studied, but human causation is not established.

          🔁Metabolic & liver axismetabolic▾

                #### Obesity & type 2 diabetes
                Probable
              
              Microbiota influence energy harvest, bile-acid signalling and metabolic endotoxaemia. Transferring an obese phenotype between mice via faeces is striking - but human FMT for metabolic disease gives only modest, transient effects, so the human contribution is real yet partial.

                #### MASLD & hepatic encephalopathy
                Probable
              
              The gut-liver axis delivers microbial products via the portal vein. In **hepatic encephalopathy** the link is therapeutic and strong: lactulose and rifaximin lower ammonia-producing bacteria and reverse symptoms.

                #### Cardiovascular disease (TMAO)
                Association
              
              Microbial metabolism of dietary choline and carnitine yields **TMAO**, associated with atherosclerosis and adverse CV events. Mechanistically attractive, but a clinically actionable causal role is unproven.

          🧠Gut-brain axisneuro / mental▾

                #### Mood & anxiety
                Association
              
              Microbes make and modulate neuroactive compounds (serotonin precursors, GABA, SCFAs) and signal via the vagus nerve. Animal data are compelling and small "psychobiotic" trials show signals, but human evidence remains early and easily overstated.

                #### Parkinson's disease
                Association
              
              Constipation often precedes motor symptoms by years and a distinct faecal signature is seen; alpha-synuclein pathology may begin in the enteric nervous system. Intriguing, but observational.

          Correlation is not causation
          Most microbiome-disease links are cross-sectional associations. Dysbiosis can be a cause, a consequence, or a bystander of illness, diet and medication. The strongest causal claims rest on intervention evidence - FMT curing C. difficile, rifaximin in hepatic encephalopathy - not on a "different-looking" stool sample. Treat direct-to-consumer microbiome tests as not yet clinically validated.

        In practice

        ## What this means at the bench
        
          Protect what is there
          The single most evidence-based microbiome intervention is antibiotic stewardship - narrowest effective spectrum, shortest effective course. Diet is the most powerful day-to-day modulator: fibre diversity and fermented foods build SCFA-producing diversity.

          Match the tool to the goal
          For the conditions with causal evidence, targeted therapies exist (FMT and live biotherapeutics for recurrent C. difficile; rifaximin for hepatic encephalopathy and IBS-D). Elsewhere, support diversity through diet rather than reaching for unvalidated supplements. See the companion explainer on microbiome-altering treatments.
