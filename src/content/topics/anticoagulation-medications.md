#### The cascade simplified
        Tissue factor activates factor VII. Factor X is activated (intrinsic and extrinsic pathways converge). Factor X activates prothrombin to thrombin. Thrombin converts fibrinogen to fibrin; fibrin forms the clot.

        #### Key intervention points
        
          **Heparins:** Enhance antithrombin; inhibit factors IIa and Xa.
          **Fondaparinux:** Enhances antithrombin; inhibits factor Xa only.
          **Warfarin:** Inhibits vitamin K synthesis; reduces factors II, VII, IX, X.
          **DOACs:** Direct inhibition of factor IIa (dabigatran) or factor Xa (apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban).

        #### Unfractionated heparin (UFH)

          **Onset:** Immediate (minutes).
          **Reversal:** Protamine sulfate (1 mg per 100 units heparin).

        #### Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH)
        
          **Agents:** Enoxaparin, dalteparin, tinzaparin.
          **Dosing:** Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC BD (treatment) or 40 mg OD (prophylaxis); weight-based or fixed-dose.
          **Monitoring:** Anti-Xa level (optional); no routine aPTT monitoring.
          **Onset:** 3-4 hours.
          **Elimination:** Renal; use with caution if CrCl 
          **Reversal:** Protamine (less effective than for UFH; gives 60% reversal); only if major bleed.

      💊Warfarin and DOACs▾
      
        #### Warfarin
        
          **Target INR:** 2-3 for most indications (AF, VTE, mechanical valve).
          **Onset:** Slow (3-5 days); bridge with LMWH or UFH.
          **Monitoring:** INR weekly then every 4 weeks when stable; many drug and dietary interactions.
          **Reversal:** Vitamin K (slow, 12-24 hours) or FFP/PCC (rapid, for major bleed).
          **Advantages:** Cheap; well-understood; reversible.
          **Disadvantages:** Slow onset; narrow therapeutic window; interactions; needs monitoring.
        
        #### Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs)
        
          **Factor Xa inhibitors:** Apixaban 5 mg BD, rivaroxaban 20 mg OD, edoxaban 60 mg OD.
          **Factor IIa inhibitor:** Dabigatran 110-150 mg BD.
          **Advantages:** Rapid onset (1-3 hours); no monitoring; fewer interactions; better side-effect profile.
          **Disadvantages:** Irreversible (except newer agents); contraindicated in severe renal impairment; more expensive than warfarin.
          **Reversal:** Dabigatran: idarucizumab (rapid, complete); Factor Xa inhibitors: andexanet alfa (partial, slower availability).

      🏥Thromboprophylaxis▾
      
        #### Surgical patients
        
          **Low risk (minor surgery, no VTE risk factors):** Early mobilisation only.
          **Moderate risk (general surgery, mild VTE risk):** LMWH (enoxaparin 40 mg OD) or UFH prophylaxis for 7-10 days.
          **High risk (major surgery, cancer, orthopaedic):** LMWH or fondaparinux for 10-35 days; DOACs option in orthopaedic (e.g. apixaban post-hip replacement).
        
        #### Medical patients (hospitalised)
        
          - LMWH 40 mg OD for duration of hospitalisation if immobile or acute illness.
        
        #### Mechanical valves
        
          - Warfarin only (INR 2-3 or higher for mitral valve); DOACs contraindicated.

      ⚠️Bleeding and reversal▾
      
        #### Major bleed management
        
          **Warfarin:** Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) 15 ml/kg or Prothrombin Complex Concentrate (PCC) 25 units/kg plus IV vitamin K 10 mg.
          **LMWH:** Protamine 1 mg per 100 units enoxaparin (max 50 mg); repeat if needed; only if recent dose.
          **Dabigatran:** Idarucizumab 5 g IV (2 x 2.5 g 15 min apart); complete and rapid reversal.
          **Factor Xa inhibitors:** Andexanet alfa (expensive, not widely available); PCC as temporary measure.
        
        #### Minor bleeding
        
          - Hold anticoagulant; local measures; restart when safe.
          - No need for reversal agents.
