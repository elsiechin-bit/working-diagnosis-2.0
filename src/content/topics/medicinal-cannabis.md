#### THC (Delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol)
        
          **Mechanism:** Partial agonist at CB1 (brain) and CB2 (immune) receptors.
          **Effects:** Psychoactive, analgesic, antiemetic, appetite stimulation, sedation.
          **Onset:** Oral 1-2 hours; smoked/vaporised 15-30 minutes.
          **Peak:** 2-4 hours (oral); 30 minutes (inhaled).
          **Half-life:** 25-36 hours (THC stored in fat; elimination slow).
          **Adverse effects:** Dizziness, dry mouth, tachycardia, anxiety, paranoia, impaired cognition, dependence risk (~9% users; higher if adolescent onset).
        
        #### CBD (Cannabidiol)
        
          **Mechanism:** Non-intoxicating; affects multiple pathways (5-HT, TRPV1, adenosine, GPR55).
          **Effects:** Anxiolytic, anticonvulsant, anti-inflammatory, analgesic (modest).
          **Onset:** Slower than THC; 30 minutes to 2 hours.
          **Half-life:** 18-32 hours.
          **Adverse effects:** Minimal; fatigue, diarrhoea (rare). No abuse potential.
          **Note:** May reduce THC effects (modulates CB1 signalling).

      📋Evidence and approved indications▾
      
        #### Strong evidence
        
          **Chronic pain (non-cancer):** Modest analgesic effect; NNT ~6-8 for 30% pain reduction. Often used as adjunct to conventional analgesia.
          **Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV):** THC effective (oral dronabinol approved in many countries). Less effective than modern 5-HT3 antagonists alone, but useful adjunct.
          **Multiple sclerosis spasticity:** Modest efficacy; nabiximols (Sativex, 1:1 THC:CBD) approved in some countries.
          **Epilepsy (specific):** CBD-rich formulations approved for Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes (severe paediatric epilepsies); significant seizure reduction.
        
        #### Moderate evidence (promising but not conclusive)
        
          - PTSD symptoms (primarily THC + CBT).
          - Anxiety disorders (CBD primarily).
          - Insomnia (THC + CBD; limited RCT data).
          - Inflammatory bowel disease (modest anti-inflammatory effect; evidence mixed).
        
        #### Weak or no evidence
        
          - Cancer (as primary treatment); no evidence for direct anti-tumour effect.
          - Psychosis (THC may worsen; CBD possibly neutral or protective).
          - Alzheimer's disease, other neurodegenerative conditions.
          - Most other conditions (acne, migraines, etc.) lack RCT evidence.

      🇳🇿NZ regulatory pathway and prescribing▾
      
        #### Legal status in Aotearoa New Zealand
        Cannabis is a controlled drug (Schedule 2). Medicinal cannabis products (Epidiolex for epilepsy, Sativex for MS spasticity) can be prescribed on the Provisional Product List through specialist and DHB approval.

        #### Prescribing requirements
        
          - Specialist initiation (usually neurology, oncology, pain specialist, or rheumatology).
          - GP can continue prescription once initiated and stabilised.
          - DHBNZ approval required; application process variable by DHB.
          - Patient consent and discussion of risks/benefits essential.
          - Baseline assessment: cognitive function, mood, driving ability, cannabis use history.
        
        #### Available products (in NZ)
        
          **Epidiolex:** CBD-only (98%+); for Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut epilepsy. Tablets or liquid.
          **Sativex:** 1:1 THC:CBD nabiximols oromucosal spray; for MS spasticity.
          - Other products available through compassionate access or specialist arrangements (e.g., dried flower for pain).

      ⚠️Adverse effects and monitoring▾
      
        #### Common adverse effects
        
          - Dizziness, drowsiness, dry mouth, impaired concentration.
          - Tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension.
          - Anxiety, paranoia, hallucinations (especially with high-THC products).
        
        #### Serious risks
        
          **Cannabis use disorder:** ~9% of users; higher if adolescent onset or heavy use.
          **Psychosis:** THC increases risk in vulnerable individuals (family history of psychosis, schizophrenia spectrum). CBD may be protective.
          **Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome:** Paradoxical severe nausea and vomiting with chronic high-THC use; resolves on cessation.
          **Impaired cognition and driving:** THC impairs reaction time and coordination. Advise against driving or operating machinery, especially during dose titration.
        
        #### Monitoring on treatment
        
          - Cognitive assessment at baseline and 3-6 months (Montreal Cognitive Assessment or similar).
          - Mood screening (PHQ-9, GAD-7) at baseline and regular intervals.
          - Driving and workplace safety discussion; document advice.
          - Reassess indication and efficacy every 6-12 months.

      💊Drug interactions and contraindications▾
      
        #### Cytochrome P450 interactions
        
          - THC and CBD are metabolised by CYP3A4 and CYP2C9.
          - Co-administration with inducers (phenytoin, rifampicin) may reduce cannabis levels.
          - Co-administration with inhibitors (ketoconazole, erythromycin) may increase cannabis levels and side effects.
          - Additive CNS depression with opioids, benzodiazepines, alcohol.
        
        #### Contraindications
        
          - Personal or family history of psychosis or schizophrenia (especially high-THC products).
          - Active substance use disorder or high-risk use pattern.
          - Severe liver or renal disease (reduce dose or avoid).
          - Pregnancy or breastfeeding (THC and CBD cross placenta; teratogenicity data limited).
          - Uncontrolled cardiac disease, arrhythmias (THC causes tachycardia).

      ❓Common questions▾
      
        #### Is medicinal cannabis safe?
        Relative safety depends on THC:CBD ratio, duration of use and individual factors. CBD-only products (Epidiolex) are safest. High-THC products carry more risk of psychosis, dependence and cognitive impairment. Use should be reserved for evidence-based indications and monitored by clinicians.

        #### Can patients drive on medicinal cannabis?
        THC impairs driving ability; patients should not drive during dose titration or if experiencing dizziness/drowsiness. CBD alone does not significantly impair driving. Advise patients to assess their individual tolerance and legal responsibilities (NZ impairment testing not standardised for cannabis).

        #### How quickly does it work?
        Onset varies: inhalation 15-30 minutes, oral 1-2 hours. Full therapeutic effect may take weeks to months as pain pathways adapt. Dose titration is gradual to minimise adverse effects.

        #### What about cannabis from other sources (non-prescription)?
        Illegal in NZ outside approved medical products. Street cannabis has highly variable THC and CBD content, unknown contaminants, and no quality control. Advise patients to use only prescribed, regulated products.
