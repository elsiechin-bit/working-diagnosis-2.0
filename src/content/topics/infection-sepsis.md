#### Sepsis (Sepsis-3, 2016)
          Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysregulated host response to infection.

        💊Acute management (Sepsis bundles)▾
        
          #### First hour ("Golden Hour")
          
            **Lactate measurement:** Elevated lactate indicates tissue hypoperfusion.
            **Blood cultures:** Before antibiotics (2 sets); culture any indwelling device.
            **Broad-spectrum antibiotics:** Within 1 hour; empirically cover likely pathogens based on source (see below).
            **Fluid resuscitation:** 30 mL/kg crystalloid (e.g. Hartmann's) over first 3 hours if hypotensive or lactate >2.
            **Source identification:** Imaging (CXR, CT abdomen, ultrasound), examination, history.
          
          #### Early hours (3-6)
          
            **Reassess perfusion:** Lactate clearance, urine output (>0.5 mL/kg/hr target), vital signs.
            **Vasopressors (noradrenaline first-line):** If SBP remains 
            **Source control:** Drainage of abscess, debridement, removal of infected device, appendectomy, etc.
            **Antibiotic de-escalation:** Once culture results available, switch to narrower spectrum if possible.

        🦠Empiric antibiotic choice by source▾
        
          #### Community-acquired pneumonia
          Ceftriaxone 2g IV Q12H + azithromycin 500mg IV daily (or respiratory fluoroquinolone alone).

          #### UTI / urosepsis
          Ceftriaxone or fluoroquinolone (ciprofloxacin 400mg IV Q8–12H) or gentamicin + ampicillin if enterococcus suspected.

          #### Intra-abdominal
          Ceftriaxone + metronidazole (or piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV Q6H covers anaerobes).

          #### Skin and soft tissue
          Flucloxacillin 2g IV Q6H (if Staph aureus likely); vancomycin if MRSA suspected; add clindamycin if necrotizing.

          #### Meningitis
          Ceftriaxone 2g IV Q12H + vancomycin + dexamethasone (reduces mortality).

        ❓Post-sepsis syndrome▾
        
          #### Long-term sequelae
          
            - Fatigue, weakness, cognitive impairment ("brain fog"), mood disturbance (depression, PTSD).
            - Organ dysfunction (renal, hepatic, cardiac) may persist weeks to months.
            - Immunosuppression increases re-infection risk.
          
          #### Management
          
            - Rehabilitation, PT/OT, cognitive support.
            - Monitor for secondary infection, VTE (DVT/PE common post-sepsis).
            - Psychological support; screen for depression and PTSD.
