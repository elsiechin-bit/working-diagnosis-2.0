## Treatment


**Psychotherapy is the first-line treatment.** Medication has very limited evidence for personality disorders themselves, though it may help comorbid conditions (depression, anxiety, ADHD). Specific approaches with evidence include:

          **Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT):** Especially for BPD. It combines individual therapy, skills training, phone coaching, and therapist consultation meetings. The structure is intense and requires commitment from both therapist and patient, but it has strong evidence for reducing self-harm and improving interpersonal functioning.4
          **Mentalization-based therapy (MBT):** Helps patients understand their own and others' mental states. Evidence supports it for BPD and other Cluster B disorders.5
          **Transference-focused psychotherapy (TFP):** Works with the relationship between therapist and patient as the vehicle for change. Evidence for BPD and narcissistic personality disorder.
          **Cognitive-behavioural approaches:** For specific traits (e.g., cognitive therapy for paranoid traits, behavioural activation for avoidant traits).
        
        **What doesn't work:** labelling the patient "untreatable," rotating through multiple therapists, setting punitive boundaries, or expecting medication to resolve core personality issues. Change is slow and requires sustained therapeutic relationship and effort.
