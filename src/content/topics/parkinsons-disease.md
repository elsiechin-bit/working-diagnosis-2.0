## Overview


Parkinson’s disease is a clinical diagnosis requiring **bradykinesia** plus rest tremor and/or rigidity, characteristically **asymmetric**. A clear, sustained response to levodopa supports it.

  #### Red flags suggesting an alternative (Parkinson-plus / secondary)
  
   **Symmetry, early falls, rapid progression**, poor levodopa response
   **Early autonomic failure** (MSA), **early dementia/visual hallucinations** (DLB)
   **Vertical gaze palsy** (PSP), **drug history** (antipsychotics, metoclopramide → drug-induced parkinsonism)
  
  Refer untreated where possibleGuidelines favour specialist (neurology) confirmation before starting therapy; DaT imaging only helps when essential tremor or drug-induced parkinsonism is in doubt.

      🚶Motor & non-motor features▾
      
   #### MotorRest tremor (pill-rolling), bradykinesia, cogwheel rigidity, hypomimia, micrographia, shuffling/festinant gait, reduced arm swing, postural instability (later)
   #### Non-motor (often the most disabling)Constipation, hyposmia, REM sleep behaviour disorder, depression/anxiety, cognitive impairment, orthostatic hypotension, urinary urgency, fatigue, pain
  
  Several non-motor features (constipation, anosmia, RBD, depression) commonly **predate the motor diagnosis by years** and are part of ongoing management, not incidental.

      💊Principles of treatment▾
      Treatment is symptomatic - no agent yet proven to slow progression. Start when symptoms affect function/quality of life. See the Parkinson’s Medications explainer for detail.

   **Levodopa** (with a decarboxylase inhibitor) - most effective for motor symptoms; first-line for most, especially older patients.
   **Dopamine agonists / MAO-B inhibitors** - alternatives or adjuncts; agonists carry impulse-control disorder risk (gambling, hypersexuality) - warn and monitor.
   **Never stop dopaminergic therapy abruptly** - risk of akinetic crisis / neuroleptic malignant-like syndrome. Maintain timing in hospital ("Parkinson’s meds on time, every time").
   - Multidisciplinary input: PD nurse specialist, physiotherapy, OT, SALT.

      ⏳Long-term complications▾
      
   **Motor fluctuations** ("wearing off", on-off) and **levodopa-induced dyskinesia** emerge over years.
   **PD dementia** and psychosis (visual hallucinations) - avoid typical antipsychotics; quetiapine/clozapine if essential; rivastigmine for cognition.
   **Falls, dysphagia, autonomic and mood** problems dominate later disease.
   **Advanced therapies** (apomorphine, intestinal levodopa gel, deep brain stimulation) for selected patients.

   ✦**Asymmetric + levodopa-responsive** = the typical picture.
   ✦**Never miss a dose:** in-hospital timing is a safety issue.
   ✦**Ask about impulse-control disorders** on dopamine agonists.
   ✦**Avoid dopamine blockers** (metoclopramide, prochlorperazine, typical antipsychotics) - use domperidone for nausea.
