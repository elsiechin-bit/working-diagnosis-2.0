#### Onychocryptosis (ingrown toenail) classification
        
          **Stage 1 (erythema):** Mild inflammation, no drainage
          **Stage 2 (abscess):** Drainage, swelling, pain; infection present
          **Stage 3 (hypertrophic):** Granulation tissue formation, chronic drainage, significant pain

        #### Indications for surgical intervention
        
          - Stage 2 or 3 despite conservative management (nail care, soaking, antibiotics)
          - Recurrent episodes (>2) of infection
          - Pain significantly impacting function or quality of life
          - Patient preference after failed conservative care

        #### Conservative measures (first-line, 4-6 week trial)
        
          - Trim nail straight across; avoid cutting below skin line
          - Soak foot in warm salt water 15-20 min, 2-3x daily
          - Wear loose-fitting shoes; avoid tight toe box
          - Oral antibiotics if infected (amoxicillin-clavulanate or first-generation cephalosporin) for 7-10 days
          - Topical antibiotics (mupirocin) to prevent secondary infection
          - Nail bracing or taping: adhesive strips under nail lateral edge to lift it away from skin (limited evidence)

        #### Contraindications to surgery
        
          - Active infection with systemic signs (fever, lymphangitis) — treat with antibiotics first; defer surgery 1-2 weeks
          - Severe peripheral vascular disease or critical limb ischaemia (relative; assess healing potential)
          - Severe immunocompromise or uncontrolled diabetes (relative; higher infection risk)
          - Patient refusal or inability to comply with aftercare

        #### Pre-operative assessment
        
          - Inspect both feet; assess for bilateral involvement (10-15% bilateral)
          - Palpate for fluctuance (abscess); check for lymphadenopathy or systemic signs
          - Examine peripheral pulses and capillary refill (assess vascular status)
          - Document nail appearance, location of ingrowing edge (medial vs lateral), degree of inflammation

        #### Consent
        
          - Explain procedure: removal of nail edge, chemical or physical destruction of nail matrix to prevent regrowth
          - Warn: temporary drainage post-op (may persist 1-2 weeks); slight chance of nail deformity if incomplete matrix destruction; small risk of infection
          - Advise: healing 4-6 weeks; modified footwear during healing; soaking and dressing changes necessary

      🩹Anaesthesia: digital block technique▾
      
        Ring block (digital block) is preferred for toe anaesthesia; allows complete tourniquet application without ischaemia concern.

          Digital nerve block for hallux (great toe)
          **Local anaesthetic:** 1% lignocaine without adrenaline; 5-10 mL total
          **Needle:** 25G, 1-inch
          
          **Technique:**
          
            1
            Patient supine or seated with foot elevated on table

            2
            Identify metatarsal head at base of hallux; inject 2-3 mL on medial (tibial) side of toe at MTP joint level

            3
            Inject lateral (fibular) side with same volume

            4
            Optional: inject 1-2 mL at dorsal midline (dorsal branch) to ensure complete block

            5
            Wait 5-10 minutes; test with pinprick before proceeding

          **Duration:** 45-60 minutes (adequate for procedure and initial post-op period)
          **Caution:** NEVER use adrenaline in digital blocks (risk of vasoconstriction, tissue necrosis)

          Tourniquet application
          **Purpose:** Reduce bleeding and improve visualization
          **Method:** Wrap elastic bandage (Elastoplast or Esmarch) from toes toward ankle, creating proximal obstruction to arterial flow
          **Duration:** Safe up to 20 minutes in lower limb at room temperature; release if exceeds 20 min or patient reports pain
          **Alternative:** Finger tourniquet (isolated, local ischaemia) applied at toe base; simpler, effective for minor toe work

      ✂️Partial nail avulsion (PNA) technique▾
      
        Most common procedure for ingrown toenail in primary care. Removes only the ingrowing lateral or medial nail edge, preserving central nail.

          Surgical setup
          **Sterile field:** Prep foot with chlorhexidine or iodine; drape with sterile towels
          **Equipment:** Nail nipper or scissors, periosteal elevator or nail elevator, phenol container (85%), cotton wool balls, forceps, specimen pot
          **Assistant:** Helpful for retraction and haemostasis; may not be essential in primary care setting

          Step-by-step PNA procedure
          
          **Step 1: Identify nail edge to be removed**
          
            1
            Assess which edge is ingrowing (usually medial for hallux)

            2
            Plan to remove 3-4 mm (or width of nail impinging on skin)

          **Step 2: Lift nail edge**
          
            1
            Use periosteal elevator or nail elevator to separate lateral nail edge from nail bed

            2
            Gently lift and retract nail; do not tear remaining nail

          **Step 3: Excise nail edge**
          
            1
            Using nail nipper or scissors, cut nail longitudinally along line to be removed

            2
            Use scissors or nipper to cut from distal to proximal; direct blade at ~45° angle to follow nail growth direction

            3
            Remove entire nail edge from distal to nail matrix (where nail grows at base)

            4
            Place removed segment in specimen pot for histology if desired

          **Step 4: Matricectomy (matrix destruction)**
          Either phenol chemical matricectomy (preferred) or surgical matricectomy required to prevent nail regrowth
          
          **Phenol matricectomy (chemical):**
          
            1
            Soak 2-3 cotton wool balls in 85% phenol solution

            2
            Place phenol-soaked swabs into nail matrix area where nail was removed; keep in place for 2-3 minutes

            3
            Repeat phenol application 1-2 more times (total 3 applications) at 2-minute intervals for enhanced effect

            4
            Rinse thoroughly with saline or alcohol to neutralize phenol (prevents chemical burns)

          **Surgical matricectomy (alternative):**
          
            1
            Identify nail matrix (pinkish tissue at base of nail bed)

            2
            Using scalpel or curette, carefully excise 2-3 mm of matrix tissue where nail was removed

            3
            Cauterize matrix with diathermy or phenol to prevent regrowth

            4
            More invasive; higher tissue trauma; similar recurrence rates to phenol

        #### Haemostasis
        
          - Most bleeding stops with tourniquet release and pressure dressing
          - If brisk bleeding: apply gauze pressure x 5 min or use diathermy/cautery
          - Avoid suturing nail bed (risk of infection, poor healing)

      🩹Dressing and post-operative care▾
      
        #### Initial dressing (in clinic)
        
          - After tourniquet release, clean site thoroughly with saline
          - Apply sterile non-adherent dressing (paraffin gauze or silicone) to nail bed
          - Cover with sterile gauze pad
          - Wrap with elastic bandage (Elastoplast) to provide gentle compression; not too tight
          - Advise elevation for first 24 hours to reduce swelling

        #### Post-operative instructions (written and verbal)
        
          **First dressing change:** 2-3 days post-op; patient-led or clinic review
          **Soaking:** Once dressing removed, soak foot in warm salt water (1 teaspoon salt per litre) 15-20 min, twice daily until healed
          **Redressing:** Apply fresh non-adherent dressing and gauze after soaking; change daily or every other day
          **Footwear:** Wear loose-fitting, open-toed shoes (sandals) for first 2 weeks; avoid tight toe box
          **Activity:** Avoid sports and strenuous activity for 2 weeks; normal walking OK after 5-7 days
          **Pain management:** Paracetamol or NSAIDs as needed; usually minimal pain after first 24 hours
          **Antibiotics:** Routine prophylaxis not needed if sterile technique used; prescribe only if signs of infection develop

        #### Expected healing timeline
        
          **Days 1-3:** Swelling, mild serous drainage (normal)
          **Days 4-7:** Drainage decreases; dressing changes reduce in frequency
          **Weeks 2-4:** Epithelialization of nail bed; reduced need for dressing
          **Weeks 4-6:** Full healing; skin closure over nail bed; may have mild scarring

        #### Red flags and when to review
        
          **Increasing pain after day 3:** Suggests infection; review and consider antibiotics
          **Purulent drainage, fever, or cellulitis:** Sign of infection; urgent review and IV antibiotics if systemically unwell
          **Excessive bleeding not controlled by pressure:** Rare; check haemostasis, consider cautery or sutures
          **Nail regrowth into lateral fold:** Suggests incomplete matricectomy; consider repeat procedure or referral to podiatrist/surgeon

        #### Routine follow-up
        
          - First review: 3-5 days post-op (wound check, redressing)
          - Second review: 2 weeks post-op (assess healing, reduce dressing frequency)
          - Final review: 6 weeks post-op (confirm healed, check for recurrence)

      ⚠️Complications and troubleshooting▾
      
        #### Common complications
        
          **Serous or mild serosanguinous drainage (weeks 1-2):** Normal inflammatory response; manage with daily dressing changes; no antibiotics needed unless purulent
          **Transient nail bed tenderness:** Expected; improves with local anaesthetic in soaks or topical lidocaine cream
          **Mild nail bed scarring or slight deformity:** Usually cosmetic only; rarely symptomatic

        #### Infection (1-2% if sterile technique)
        
          **Signs:** Increasing pain after day 3, purulent drainage, erythema extending beyond surgical site, fever, lymphadenopathy
          **Management:** Culture drainage (if available); start oral antibiotics (amoxicillin-clavulanate 500 mg TDS or cephalexin 500 mg QID); review in 3-5 days
          **If systemically unwell:** IV antibiotics (IV cefazolin + flucloxacillin); consider hospitalization; urgent microbiological review

        #### Inadequate matricectomy (recurrence 3-5% if phenol used correctly)
        
          **Signs:** New nail growth into lateral nail fold 4-8 weeks post-op; return of pain and inflammation
          **Prevention:** Ensure thorough phenol application (3 times, 2-3 min each); visualize entire matrix
          **Management:** Repeat PNA with matricectomy; consider referral to surgeon if recurrent

        #### Phenol burn (if not neutralized)
        
          **Signs:** Severe pain, white or brown discoloration of tissue, tissue necrosis
          **Prevention:** Rinse thoroughly with saline or 70% alcohol immediately after phenol application
          **Management:** Continue rinsing; allow healing; monitor for infection; may require more frequent dressing changes

        #### Excessive bleeding
        
          **Prevention:** Proper tourniquet application; avoid cutting nail bed during removal
          **Management:** Direct pressure with gauze x 5-10 minutes; if bleeding persists, apply diathermy to nail bed or matrix; consider adrenaline-soaked swab (1:1000) for brief haemostasis

        #### Nail bed ulceration or slow healing
        
          - More common in diabetic or vascular patients
          - Manage with frequent dressing changes, salt water soaks, topical negative pressure therapy if available
          - Consider vascular assessment if healing delayed beyond 6-8 weeks

      📚Equipment and references▾
      
        #### Surgical instruments (basic tray)
        
          - Nail nipper or strong scissors (curved or straight)
          - Periosteal elevator or nail elevator
          - Scalpel (No. 15 blade, optional for surgical matricectomy)
          - Forceps (standard surgical)
          - Gauze swabs, non-adherent paraffin gauze or silicone pads
          - Elastic tourniquet bandage (Elastoplast or Esmarch)
          - 85% Phenol solution in container
          - Sterile gloves, drapes, cleaning solution (chlorhexidine or iodine)
          - Specimen pot (if saving nail segment for histology)

        #### Tips for success in primary care
        
          - Practice digital block technique on foot models or cadavers before live patient
          - Phenol matricectomy simpler and as effective as surgical; preferred for primary care setting
          - Ensure adequate tourniquet time (up to 20 minutes safe; improves haemostasis and visualization)
          - Triple phenol application (3x, 2-3 minutes each) reduces recurrence
          - Thorough saline/alcohol rinse essential to prevent phenol burn complications
          - Clear post-operative instructions and scheduled follow-up essential for good outcomes
