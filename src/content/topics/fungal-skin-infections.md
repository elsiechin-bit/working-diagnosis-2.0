## Overview


Most superficial fungal infections are dermatophyte (tinea), Candida, or Malassezia. Diagnosis is usually clinical but **skin scrapings for microscopy/culture** are worthwhile before oral therapy or when uncertain.

  InfectionClues
   Tinea corporisAnnular, scaly, active raised edge, central clearing
   Tinea pedisInterdigital maceration/scaling; a reservoir for cellulitis
   Tinea crurisGroin, advancing edge, spares scrotum (vs Candida)
   Tinea capitisScaly patches + hair loss; children - needs oral Rx
   OnychomycosisThick, discoloured, crumbly nail - confirm before oral Rx
   CandidiasisMoist flexures, satellite pustules, beefy red
   Pityriasis versicolorTrunk macules, fail to tan / hypo- or hyperpigmented, fine scale

      💊Treatment▾
      
   **Localised tinea / candida:** topical azole (clotrimazole, miconazole) or terbinafine, continued 1-2 weeks past clearance.
   **Extensive, recurrent, or hair/nail involvement:** oral terbinafine (dermatophytes) or itraconazole/fluconazole - onychomycosis needs weeks (fingernails) to months (toenails).
   **Tinea capitis:** always oral (terbinafine or griseofulvin) plus antifungal shampoo to reduce spread; examine/treat household contacts.
   **Pityriasis versicolor:** topical ketoconazole shampoo (applied to skin) or selenium sulfide; oral for extensive disease. Warn pigment takes months to normalise.
   **Candidal intertrigo:** topical azole + keep the area dry; treat predisposing factors (diabetes, occlusion, moisture).
  
  Beware tinea incognitoA topical steroid (or steroid-antifungal combo) applied to undiagnosed tinea suppresses the inflammation, erases the classic ring, and lets the fungus spread. Confirm the diagnosis before prescribing any steroid for an "eczema" that isn’t settling.

   ✦**Scrape the active edge** - that’s where the hyphae are.
   ✦**Confirm nails before oral therapy** - months of treatment shouldn’t be empirical.
   ✦**Treat tinea pedis** to prevent recurrent cellulitis.
   ✦**No steroids on undiagnosed rings.**
