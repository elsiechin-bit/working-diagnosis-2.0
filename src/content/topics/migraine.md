#### ICHD-3 diagnostic criteria (migraine without aura  -  5 attacks required)
      
        - Headache lasting 4 - 72 hours
        - At least 2 of: unilateral, pulsating, moderate-severe, worsened by routine activity
        - At least 1 of: nausea/vomiting, photophobia AND phonophobia
      
      #### Aura criteria
      
        - Visual: fortification spectra, scintillating scotoma, hemianopia
        - Sensory: unilateral tingling/numbness spreading over minutes
        - Speech: dysarthria or dysphasia
        - Motor: hemiplegic migraine (rare)  -  specialist referral
        - Aura symptoms spread over ≥5 minutes; each lasts 5 - 60 minutes; headache follows within 60 minutes
      
      Red flags  -  secondary headache must be excluded**SNOOP4:** Systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss), Neurological signs, Onset sudden/thunderclap, Older age (>50 first migraine), Positional, Papilloedema, Progressive/changed pattern, Prior history (new pattern in known migraineur). Any of these  -  investigate with CT/MRI urgently.

      #### When to image
      
        - Thunderclap headache  -  CT head (subarachnoid haemorrhage)
        - New headache in HIV or cancer  -  MRI with gadolinium
        - Hemiplegic migraine  -  MRI to exclude structural cause
        - Typical stable migraine without red flags  -  imaging not required

    💊Acute treatment▾
    
      #### Stepwise approach
      
        **Step 1:** NSAIDs (ibuprofen 400 - 600 mg, naproxen 500 mg) or aspirin 900 mg + metoclopramide 10 mg for mild-moderate attacks
        **Step 2:** Triptan + NSAID (combination more effective than either alone) for moderate-severe attacks
        **Step 3:** Try a different triptan if first fails; switch class
      
      #### Triptans
      
        TriptanOnsetNotes
        
          Sumatriptan 50 - 100 mg oral30 - 60 minMost evidence; also SC 6 mg for rapid/reliable effect; nasal spray 10 - 20 mg
          Rizatriptan 10 mg ODT30 - 45 minWafer useful when nausea impairs swallowing
          Zolmitriptan 2.5 - 5 mg30 - 60 minAlso nasal spray; good for menstrual migraine
          Eletriptan 40 - 80 mg30 - 60 minHigher efficacy in some patients; longer duration

      Triptan contraindications: ischaemic heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension, stroke/TIA history, basilar/hemiplegic migraine, concurrent MAOI, pregnancy (use with caution in 2nd - 3rd trimester).

      #### Newer acute options
      
        **Gepants** (rimegepant, ubrogepant)  -  CGRP receptor antagonists; no vasoconstriction; safe in cardiovascular disease; can also be used as preventive
        **Lasmiditan**  -  5-HT1F agonist; no vasoconstriction; dizziness/sedation; do not drive for 8 hours; not yet available in NZ

    🛡️Preventive therapy▾
    
      #### When to start prevention
      
        - ≥4 migraine days/month OR significantly impaired quality of life regardless of frequency
        - Chronic migraine (≥15 days/month)
        - Contraindication to or failure of acute therapies
        - Hemiplegic migraine, migraine with prolonged aura
      
      #### First-line oral preventives
      
        DrugDoseNotes
        
          Propranolol40 - 160 mg/dayMost evidence; avoid in asthma, depression, Raynaud's; useful if HTN or anxiety
          Amitriptyline10 - 75 mg nocteGood if sleep disruption or depression; start 10 mg; titrate slowly
          Topiramate25 - 100 mg/dayCognitive side effects ("dopamax"); weight loss; teratogenic  -  contraception required
          Valproate400 - 600 mg BDEffective; highly teratogenic  -  avoid in women of childbearing potential unless no alternatives
          Candesartan8 - 16 mg/dayGood evidence; fewer side effects; useful in HTN

      #### Anti-CGRP monoclonal antibodies (specialist)
      
        - Erenumab, fremanezumab, galcanezumab  -  monthly SC injections
        - 50 - 60% achieve ≥50% reduction in monthly migraine days
        - Well tolerated; few significant side effects
        - Funded in NZ for chronic migraine after failure of ≥2 preventives
      
      #### Non-pharmacological prevention
      
        - Identify and manage triggers: sleep disruption, fasting, dehydration, stress, hormonal changes
        - Regular sleep schedule, aerobic exercise, stress management
        - Migraine diary (app-based) to track triggers and treatment response
        - Acupuncture  -  evidence comparable to preventive medication

    ⚠️Medication overuse headache & special populations▾
    
      #### Medication overuse headache (MOH)
      Occurs when analgesics or triptans are used >10 - 15 days/month for >3 months. Results in daily or near-daily headache that loses its migrainous character.

        - Risk: triptans and opioids >10 days/month; simple analgesics >15 days/month
        - Management: abrupt or gradual withdrawal of the overused medication; start preventive therapy
        - Prednisolone 60 mg for 5 days can ease withdrawal; expect worsening for 1 - 2 weeks before improvement
        - Full recovery may take 2 - 3 months; relapse is common  -  ongoing monitoring essential
      
      #### Migraine and oral contraceptives
      
        - Migraine with aura + combined OCP = significantly increased stroke risk  -  avoid combined OCP
        - Progestogen-only methods (POP, implant, DMPA, Mirena) are safe in all migraineurs
        - Migraine without aura: combined OCP may be used cautiously with no other stroke risk factors
      
      #### Migraine in pregnancy
      
        - Migraine often improves (especially in 2nd - 3rd trimester), particularly without aura
        - Acute: paracetamol; sumatriptan where benefit outweighs risk (2nd - 3rd trimester)
        - Preventive: avoid valproate, topiramate; low-dose amitriptyline or propranolol with obstetric input
        - Avoid NSAIDs from 20 weeks; avoid opioids
