#### Symptoms
      Weight loss despite increased appetite, palpitations, tremor, heat intolerance, sweating, diarrhoea, anxiety, insomnia, menstrual irregularity, muscle weakness, lid lag, tachycardia, AF.

      #### Biochemical confirmation
      
        **TSH suppressed** (<0.1 mIU/L) is the most sensitive finding
        **fT4 and fT3 elevated**  -  fT3 may be disproportionately high in T3 toxicosis
        **Subclinical hyperthyroidism:** TSH suppressed with normal fT4 and fT3
      
      #### Differentiating the cause
      
        **TRAb (TSH receptor antibody):** positive in Graves' (>95% sensitivity)  -  key diagnostic test; also predicts relapse risk
        **TPO antibodies:** positive in Graves' and Hashimoto's  -  less specific
        **Thyroid ultrasound:** diffuse vs nodular; vascularity (high flow = Graves' or toxic MNG)
        **Thyroid radionuclide scan (Tc-99m):** diffuse uptake (Graves'), hot nodule (toxic adenoma), absent uptake (thyroiditis, iodine excess)  -  if diagnosis uncertain after above
        **ESR/CRP:** elevated in subacute thyroiditis

    💊Antithyroid drugs (ATD)▾
    
      #### Carbimazole (first-line in NZ)
      
        Initial dose: **15 - 40 mg/day** (higher for severe biochemical hyperthyroidism)
        - Titration: reduce to maintenance 5 - 15 mg/day once fT4 normalises (usually 4 - 8 weeks)
        - Block-and-replace: continue higher dose + add levothyroxine  -  avoids dose adjustments
        Duration in Graves': **12 - 18 months** then trial of withdrawal; remission ~50%
        **Agranulocytosis** (rare, ~0.3%): warn patients  -  stop immediately if fever/sore throat and check WCC urgently. Do not rechallenge.
        - Other SE: rash (switch to PTU or proceed to definitive therapy), hepatitis (rare)
      
      #### Propylthiouracil (PTU)
      
        Preferred in **first trimester pregnancy** (carbimazole associated with aplasia cutis/choanal atresia)
        - Switch to carbimazole from 2nd trimester
        - Also used in thyroid storm (blocks T4→T3 conversion) and amiodarone-induced type 1
        - Higher risk of severe hepatotoxicity than carbimazole  -  not first-line for non-pregnant adults
      
      #### Beta-blockers  -  symptomatic control
      
        - Propranolol 40 - 80 mg TDS or atenolol 25 - 50 mg daily  -  controls palpitations, tremor, anxiety while awaiting ATD effect
        - Not antithyroid  -  purely symptomatic
        - Wean off once euthyroid

    ☢️Definitive treatment  -  RAI & surgery▾
    
      #### Radioiodine (RAI)  -  ¹³¹I
      
        - Safe, effective, definitive  -  most common definitive treatment for Graves' and toxic MNG
        - Hypothyroidism develops in >80% within 6 - 12 months  -  plan levothyroxine
        **Contraindications:** pregnancy (absolute), breastfeeding, active moderate-severe Graves' ophthalmopathy (may worsen)
        - Avoid pregnancy for 6 months after RAI
        - Stop ATD 5 - 7 days before RAI; restart if needed for 6 weeks post-treatment until euthyroid
      
      #### Thyroidectomy (total or near-total)
      
        - Preferred when: large goitre with compressive symptoms, coexisting thyroid cancer, severe ophthalmopathy, RAI contraindicated, patient preference
        - Render euthyroid with ATD before surgery
        - Complications: hypoparathyroidism, recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy  -  requires experienced surgeon
        - Lifelong levothyroxine replacement after total thyroidectomy
      
      #### Graves' ophthalmopathy
      
        - 20 - 30% of Graves' disease; 3 - 5% severe
        - Features: proptosis, lid retraction, chemosis, diplopia, corneal exposure, visual loss (optic nerve compression)
        - Refer ophthalmology for all moderate-severe GO; urgent if visual threat
        - IV methylprednisolone pulse for moderate-severe active GO; teprotumumab (anti-IGF-1R) for severe/chronic
        - Smoking significantly worsens GO  -  smoking cessation is therapeutic

    🚨Thyroid storm & amiodarone▾
    
      Thyroid storm  -  life-threatening emergencyExtreme thyrotoxicosis with systemic decompensation: fever >38.5°C, HR >140, arrhythmia, HF, confusion/agitation, GI symptoms. Burch-Wartofsky score ≥45 = thyroid storm. Management: PTU 600 mg loading then 200 - 250 mg 4-hourly, potassium iodide 1 hour AFTER PTU, IV hydrocortisone 100 mg 8-hourly, propranolol, paracetamol, ICU support.

      #### Amiodarone-induced thyrotoxicosis (AIT)
      
        **Type 1** (excess iodine): underlying thyroid disease; carbimazole + potassium perchlorate
        **Type 2** (destructive thyroiditis): prednisolone 40 mg/day for 4 - 6 weeks; usually self-limiting
        - Differentiating: ultrasound vascularity (high in type 1, low in type 2); thyroid scan (low uptake in type 2)
        - Cardiologist and endocrinologist co-management; amiodarone continuation assessed case-by-case
      
      #### Subclinical hyperthyroidism
      
        - TSH <0.1 with normal fT4/fT3  -  investigate cause
        - Treat if: age >65, osteoporosis, AF, cardiac disease, postmenopausal women
        - Observe if: TSH 0.1 - 0.4, young, asymptomatic, no risk factors
