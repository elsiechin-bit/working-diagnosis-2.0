#### Recurrent UTI (rUTI)
          
            - ≥2 UTIs in 6 months or ≥3 in 12 months.
            - Affects 25–40% of women post-first UTI; rare in men without structural or functional abnormality.
          
          #### Recurrence vs relapse
          
            **Recurrence:** New infection (different organism or time interval); usual presentation.
            **Relapse:** Same organism within 2 weeks; suggests inadequate treatment or resistant focus (prostate, stones).

        🔬Risk factors and pathophysiology▾
        
          #### Anatomical and functional
          
            - Female urethra (short, close to anus); sexual activity (trauma, ascent of bacteria).
            - Incomplete bladder emptying (post-void residual >50 mL suggests issue); urinary obstruction; neurogenic bladder.
            - Vesicoureteric reflux; renal/ureteric stones; indwelling catheter.
          
          #### Host factors
          
            - Oestrogen deficiency (post-menopausal; reduces lactobacilli, increases pathogenic bacteria adherence).
            - Immunosuppression (diabetes, immunosuppressive drugs).
            - Genetics: ABO blood group, secretor status, P blood group antigen expression affect adherence of uropathogenic E. coli.
          
          #### Microbial factors
          
            - UPEC (uropathogenic E. coli) with P fimbriae and type 1 fimbriae adhere to uroepithelium; biofilm formation protects from antibiotics.

        💊Prophylaxis and prevention▾
        
          #### Non-pharmacological
          
            - Hydration (increase urine output and frequency).
            - Post-coital voiding (reduces bacterial ascent).
            - Avoid spermicide, diaphragm (increase UTI risk).
            - Cranberry juice/extracts: mixed evidence; may reduce adherence but not strongly proven.
            - Vaginal oestrogen (post-menopausal women): restores lactobacilli; modest benefit.
          
          #### Antibiotic prophylaxis
          
            **Post-coital:** TMP-SMX single dose or cephalexin after sexual intercourse; prevents pregnancy-related UTI if used consistently.
            **Continuous (long-term):** TMP-SMX 40/200 mg daily, or nitrofurantoin 100 mg daily; 3–6 months; reduces UTI by ~50%.
            **Caution:** Long-term prophylaxis increases resistance risk; reserve for frequent symptomatic rUTI (>3 per year) impacting quality of life.
          
          #### Immunological approaches
          
            **D-mannose:** May reduce UPEC adherence; limited evidence; OTC, low risk.
            **Lactobacilli (probiotics):** Restore vaginal flora; modest benefit in some studies.

        ⚠️Antibiotic resistance and stewardship▾
        
          #### Resistance patterns
          
            - TMP-SMX resistance rising globally (40–50% in some regions); fluoroquinolone resistance increasing.
            - ESBL-producing E. coli in 10–15% of community rUTI; cephalosporin-resistant.
          
          #### Stewardship
          
            - Reserve fluoroquinolones for serious infection or resistant cases; use narrow-spectrum agents first (amoxicillin if susceptible).
            - Culture-guided therapy; avoid empiric broad-spectrum in uncomplicated cystitis.

      📚Key references▾

          #### Key guidelines & evidence
          
            - IDSA (Infectious Diseases Society of America) Recurrent UTI Guidelines.
            - NICE UTI management recommendations.
            - Cranberry products have limited evidence for prevention — counselling on hydration and voiding habits preferred.
            - Prophylactic antibiotics (nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim) reduce recurrence in >2 infections/year.
            - Post-coital prophylaxis effective in sexually-triggered recurrent UTI.
            - Urological imaging (ultrasound, cystoscopy) indicated if >4 infections/year in women or any recurrence in men.
            - NZ Urology Society resources.
