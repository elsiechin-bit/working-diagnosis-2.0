#### IgE-mediated (Type I hypersensitivity)
        
          - Allergen binds IgE on mast cells and basophils; cross-linking triggers immediate degranulation.
          - Release of histamine, tryptase, leukotrienes, prostaglandins.
          - Onset: seconds to minutes; symptoms: urticaria, angioedema, pruritus, rhinitis, bronchospasm, anaphylaxis.
        
        #### Non-IgE-mediated
        
          **Food protein-induced enterocolitis (FPIES):** T-cell mediated; delayed (1-4 hours); vomiting, diarrhoea.
          **Food protein-induced allergic colitis:** Infants; bloody stools; resolves with allergen avoidance.
          **Oral allergy syndrome (OAS):** Cross-reactivity between pollen and raw fruit/vegetable proteins; local mouth itching only; heat-labile proteins destroyed by cooking.

      🍽️Food allergies▾
      
        #### Common allergens (90% of cases)
        
          - Peanuts, tree nuts, milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, soy, wheat, sesame.
        
        #### Presentation
        
          **Mild:** Oral pruritus, lip swelling (OAS or localised IgE reaction).
          **Moderate:** Urticaria, angioedema, GI symptoms (vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea), rhinitis.
          **Severe:** Anaphylaxis (hypotension, loss of consciousness, respiratory distress).
        
        #### Natural history
        
          - Milk, egg, wheat, soy: 80-90% outgrow by age 5.
          - Peanut, tree nut, fish, shellfish: 10-20% outgrow (persist into adulthood).

      💨Inhalant allergies and anaphylaxis▾
      
        #### Common inhalant allergens
        
          - Pollen, dust mites, pet dander, moulds.
          - Symptoms: rhinitis, conjunctivitis, asthma (bronchoconstriction).
        
        #### Anaphylaxis
        
          **Definition:** Severe, life-threatening systemic allergic reaction; rapid onset.
          **Triggers:** Food (peanuts, shellfish most common), medications (penicillins, NSAIDs), insect venom, latex.
          **Symptoms:** Cardiovascular collapse (hypotension, syncope), respiratory compromise (stridor, wheezing), angioedema, urticaria, GI symptoms.
          **Management:** IM epinephrine 0.3-0.5 mg (adrenaline) immediately; IV access; antihistamines, corticosteroids, oxygen; observe 4-6+ hours for biphasic reaction.

      🧪Diagnostic testing▾
      
        #### Skin prick test (SPT)
        
          **How:** Small amount of allergen extract pricked into skin; read at 15 minutes.
          **Interpretation:** Wheal >3 mm larger than control = positive (indicates IgE sensitisation).
          **Sensitivity/specificity:** ~85% / ~95%; rapid, inexpensive.
          **Note:** Positive does not always mean clinical allergy; negative rules out IgE-mediated allergy.
        
        #### Specific IgE blood test (serology)
        
          **How:** Measures IgE antibodies to specific allergens.
          **Advantages:** No antihistamines needed; can test if skin disease; less operator-dependent.
          **Disadvantages:** Slower, more expensive; results take days.
        
        #### Oral food challenge
        
          - Gold standard for diagnosis; open, single-blind, or double-blind placebo-controlled formats.
          - Performed in hospital setting with epinephrine available.
          - Reserve for diagnostic uncertainty after history and testing.
        
        #### Total IgE
        
          - Less useful; many atopic individuals have elevated total IgE; not specific.

      💊Management and prevention▾
      
        #### Allergen avoidance
        First-line; strict avoidance of known allergens. Education on hidden sources, cross-contamination, restaurant safety.

        #### Medications
        
          **Antihistamines:** H1-blockers (cetirizine, loratadine) for mild reactions; second-generation less sedating.
          **Corticosteroids:** Topical (nasal, inhaled) for rhinitis, asthma; oral for severe reactions.
          **Epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen):** For anyone with history of anaphylaxis or risk of severe reaction; two-dose injector recommended (may need repeat dose).
        
        #### Immunotherapy
        
          **Allergen immunotherapy (desensitisation):** Gradual exposure to increasing allergen doses; induces tolerance. Takes 3-5 years; 60-70% effective.
          **Indications:** Moderate to severe inhalant allergies, insect venom allergy (very effective), food allergy (oral immunotherapy emerging).
        
        #### Biologic therapy
        
          **Omalizumab (anti-IgE):** For moderate to severe allergic asthma, chronic urticaria.
          **Dupilumab (anti-IL-4R):** For atopic dermatitis, eosinophilic oesophagitis, asthma.
