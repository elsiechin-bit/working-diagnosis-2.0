#### Goals
        
          - Suppress endogenous sex hormone production (via gonadal or adrenal sources).
          - Introduce exogenous hormones matched to gender identity.
          - Achieve physiological levels comparable to cisgender peers.
          - Support psychological well-being and social functioning.
        
        #### Timeline of changes
        
          **Early (1-3 months):** Mood changes, skin texture, energy levels, libido shifts.
          **Intermediate (3-12 months):** Body fat redistribution, muscle changes, genital changes (atrophy or enlargement), voice changes (testosterone only).
          **Late (1-2+ years):** Breast development (oestrogen), facial hair growth (testosterone), pattern of secondary sexual characteristics.
        
        #### Key principles
        
          - Informed consent: discussion of expected changes, reversibility, monitoring, side effects.
          - Individualised dosing: start low, titrate to effect and tolerance.
          - Regular monitoring: hormone levels, metabolic parameters, psychological adjustment.
          - Reversibility: most changes reverse upon hormone cessation (except voice deepening and some facial hair with testosterone).

      ♀️Oestrogen-based therapy (for transgender women and transfeminine people)▾
      
        #### Oestrogen sources
        
          **Oral 17-beta-oestradiol:** 2-6 mg daily (divided); most commonly used. Variable absorption; hepatic first-pass effect.
          **Transdermal patches (17-beta-oestradiol):** 0.1-0.4 mg twice weekly; bypasses liver; steadier levels; useful if clotting risk.
          **IM injections (oestradiol valerate, oestradiol cypionate):** 5-10 mg weekly or biweekly; higher peak levels; less commonly used now.
          **Avoid conjugated oestrogens and ethinylestradiol:** Higher thrombotic risk; not recommended.
        
        #### Antiandrogens (to suppress testosterone)
        
          **Spironolactone:** Aldosterone antagonist; also blocks androgen receptor. Dose 100-400 mg daily. Side effects: hyperkalaemia, polyuria, gynecomastia.
          **Finasteride (5-alpha reductase inhibitor):** Blocks conversion of testosterone to DHT; does not lower testosterone. Useful for hair loss. 1-5 mg daily.
          **GnRH agonists (leuprolide, goserelin):** Suppress LH and FSH; reduce testosterone to near-castrate levels. Reserved for resistant cases or those with contraindications to spironolactone. Expensive.
        
        #### Expected effects
        
          - Breast development (takes 1-2+ years; final size often less than natal females).
          - Softening of skin; redistribution of body fat (hips, thighs, breasts).
          - Decreased muscle mass and strength; slower metabolism.
          - Genital atrophy; decreased libido and erectile function.
          - Mood changes; emotional sensitivity.
          - Hair texture changes; possible mild male-pattern baldness reversal.
        
        #### Monitoring
        
          - Baseline and 3-monthly (then 6-monthly): oestradiol, testosterone, LH, FSH, prolactin.
          Target: oestradiol 100-200 pg/mL; testosterone 
          - Baseline: BP, weight, lipids, glucose, clotting screen (if risk factors).
          - Annual: BP, weight, lipids, glucose; breast exam at 1-2 years (monitor for cancer risk, though low).
        
        #### Safety considerations
        
          **Thromboembolic risk:** Increased with oestrogen, especially oral; transdermal lower risk. Avoid in personal/family Hx of VTE or AF.
          **Cardiovascular:** Monitor BP; oestrogen may increase triglycerides and affect lipid profile.
          **Breast cancer:** Small increased risk with long-term oestrogen; screening guidelines evolving.
          **Liver effects:** Monitor LFTs; avoid if severe liver disease.

      ♂️Testosterone-based therapy (for transgender men and transmasculine people)▾
      
        #### Testosterone formulations
        
          **IM injections (testosterone enanthate or cypionate):** 50-100 mg weekly or 100-200 mg biweekly. First-line in many settings. Variable absorption; requires needle.
          **Transdermal gel or cream:** 50-100 mg daily; steady levels; easy dose adjustment. More expensive; skin irritation risk.
          **Transdermal patches:** 2-6 mg daily; less commonly used now due to skin irritation.
          **Oral testosterone undecanoate (Nebido, Reandron):** 40-160 mg daily; absorbed via lymphatics; bypasses liver. Not widely available; variable efficacy.
          **Subcutaneous injections:** Growing use; lower volume; less painful than IM; similar dosing to IM.
        
        #### Expected effects
        
          - Increased muscle mass and strength; increased metabolic rate; appetite increase.
          - Voice deepening (usually permanent even with cessation); deepens within first 3-6 months.
          - Facial and body hair growth (gradually over 1-2+ years); male-pattern baldness (if genetically predisposed).
          - Clitoral enlargement (1-3 cm); increased libido and erectile changes.
          - Menstrual suppression (usually within 3 months); amenorrhea expected.
          - Mood changes; increased aggression or assertiveness (mild).
          - Fat redistribution; cheek prominence; jaw widening (slow changes).
        
        #### Monitoring
        
          - Baseline and 3-monthly (then 6-monthly): total and free testosterone, LH, FSH, haemoglobin, haematocrit.
          - Target: testosterone in typical male range (300-1000 ng/dL); avoid supraphysiological levels (increased cardiovascular and polycythaemia risk).
          - Baseline: BP, weight, lipids, glucose, haemoglobin; pelvic exam if internal reproductive organs present.
          - Annual: BP, weight, lipids, glucose, haemoglobin.
        
        #### Safety considerations
        
          **Cardiovascular:** Testosterone increases BP and lipids; monitor. Stroke and MI risk increased with supraphysiological doses.
          **Polycythaemia:** Increased RBC production; monitor haemoglobin. Phlebotomy may be needed if Hb >18 g/dL.
          **Endometrial and ovarian cancer:** Long-term data limited; amenorrhoea protective but ovaries still present. Individual risk assessment needed.
          **Liver:** Oral formulations metabolised hepatically; monitor LFTs.
          **Virilisation:** Some changes permanent (voice, facial hair); patient counselling essential on reversibility timeline.

      ⏸️Puberty blockers (GnRH agonists and antagonists)▾
      
        #### Use in adolescents
        
          - Pause puberty in gender-dysphoric youth whilst they explore identity and prepare for gender-affirming decisions.
          - Reversible; allows time for psychological assessment and social transition.
          - Typically used in Tanner stage 2-3; not first-line in early childhood.
        
        #### Agents
        
          **GnRH agonists:** Leuprolide (Lupron), goserelin (Zoladex), triptorelin. Require regular injections (monthly to every 3 months).
          **GnRH antagonist:** Elagolix (newer, oral option; not widely available for this indication yet).
        
        #### Effects
        
          - Pauses pubertal development; suppresses gonadal hormone production.
          - Reversible upon cessation; puberty resumes within months.
          - Minimal side effects in short-term use; long-term data still accumulating.
        
        #### Monitoring
        
          - Baseline and periodic: height, weight, Tanner staging, bone age (X-rays), LH, FSH, testosterone/oestradiol.
          - Bone health: DXA scan if prolonged use (>2-3 years); calcium and vitamin D supplementation recommended.
          - Psychological support essential; regular assessment for persistent gender dysphoria.

      ❓Common questions and considerations▾
      
        #### How long until I see results?
        Varies by agent and individual. Mood changes within weeks; skin changes 1-3 months; body composition 3-6 months; secondary sexual characteristics 1-2+ years. Voice deepening (testosterone) fastest; breast development (oestrogen) slowest.

        #### Can I stop hormone therapy and reverse changes?
        Most changes reverse within 6-12 months (breast tissue may persist, voice stays deepened with testosterone, some facial hair permanent). Discuss reversibility timeline with clinician.

        #### Are there any long-term health risks?
        Oestrogen: increased clotting and cardiovascular risk (especially oral high-dose). Testosterone: cardiovascular and polycythaemia risk, especially at supraphysiological doses. Puberty blockers: potential effects on bone health if prolonged. Regular monitoring essential.

        #### Do I need monitoring whilst on therapy?
        Yes. Hormone levels, metabolic parameters (BP, lipids, glucose), blood counts (testosterone users), and psychological well-being require regular assessment (every 3-6 months initially, then annually).

        #### Can I use gender-affirming hormones if I have medical comorbidities?
        Depends on the condition. Oestrogen contraindicated in clotting disorders, AF, uncontrolled HTN. Testosterone requires caution in CVD. Individual risk assessment essential; many comorbidities do not preclude use but require monitoring.
