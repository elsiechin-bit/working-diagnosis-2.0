#### Three factors in thrombosis
        
          **Stasis (blood flow):** Immobility (surgery, long flights, bed rest), atrial fibrillation, mechanical obstruction.
          **Endothelial injury (vessel wall):** Trauma, central lines, surgery, malignancy, recent MI.
          **Hypercoagulability (blood):** Inherited (Factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A), acquired (cancer, antiphospholipid syndrome, pregnancy, OCP, surgery).
        
        #### Interaction
        DVT and PE form when two or three factors converge. Complete understanding of risk is important for stratification and duration of anticoagulation.

      👁️Clinical presentation and diagnosis▾
      
        #### DVT symptoms
        
          - Unilateral leg swelling, pain, warmth, erythema, palpable cord.
          - May be asymptomatic (incidental on imaging).
        
        #### PE symptoms
        
          - Acute dyspnoea, chest pain, tachycardia, tachypnoea, syncope (massive PE).
          - Can present as sudden death if extensive.
        
        #### Diagnostic approach
        
          **DVT:** Compression ultrasound (95% sensitive/specific for proximal DVT); if negative but high suspicion, repeat at 1 week or proceed to MRI.
          **PE:** CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA) is gold standard; Wells score or PERC rule for pretest probability; D-dimer if low risk and negative imaging excludes PE.

      📊Risk stratification for recurrence▾
      
        #### Provoked vs unprovoked
        
          **Provoked (reversible risk factor):** Surgery, trauma, immobility, recent long flight. Recurrence 2-6% per year off anticoagulation.
          **Unprovoked (no identifiable cause):** Recurrence 7-10% per year off anticoagulation.
        
        #### High-risk features for recurrence
        
          - Unprovoked PE (vs DVT: higher recurrence).
          - Proximal DVT (vs distal: higher risk).
          - Active cancer.
          - Thrombophilia (Factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation, antithrombin deficiency).
          - Antiphospholipid syndrome.
          - Male sex (slightly higher recurrence).
        
        #### Duration of anticoagulation
        
          **3 months:** Provoked VTE (surgery, trauma, immobility).
          **Indefinite:** Unprovoked VTE (first episode or recurrence); cancer-associated; antiphospholipid syndrome.

      💊Anticoagulation treatment▾
      
        #### Initial acute phase (5-7 days)
        
          **Unfractionated heparin (UFH):** IV bolus then infusion; rapid onset, reversible with protamine; used in renal failure, obesity, pregnancy.
          **Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH):** Enoxaparin 1 mg/kg BD or 1.5 mg/kg OD; SC injection; preferred for outpatient DVT/PE (PESI score PE severity).
          **Fondaparinux:** Factor Xa inhibitor; SC once daily; alternative if HIT concern.
        
        #### Transition to long-term anticoagulation
        
          **Warfarin (INR 2-3):** Start overlapping with LMWH/UFH for 5-7 days; slower onset; requires monitoring; drug interactions.
          **DOAC (direct oral anticoagulant):** Apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban, dabigatran; faster onset; no monitoring required; preferred for most patients.
        
        #### DOAC specifics for VTE
        
          **Apixaban:** 5 mg BD x5 days then 5 mg BD long-term; can start without bridging.
          **Rivaroxaban:** 15 mg BD x3 weeks then 20 mg OD; can start without bridging.
          **Edoxaban:** Must bridge with LMWH/UFH x5 days then 60 mg OD.

      ⚠️Complications and special situations▾
      
        #### Post-thrombotic syndrome
        Chronic venous insufficiency after DVT; pain, swelling, skin changes. Incidence ~30%. Early mobilisation and compression reduce risk.

        #### Massive PE
        
          - Haemodynamic instability (shock); consider thrombolysis or embolectomy.
        
        #### Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)
        
          - Immune reaction to heparin; paradoxical thrombosis and thrombocytopenia. Switch to fondaparinux or argatroban; avoid heparin products.
        
        #### Cancer-associated thrombosis
        
          - Higher recurrence; LMWH preferred over DOAC or warfarin (though DOACs emerging as option).
