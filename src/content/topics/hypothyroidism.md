#### Symptoms
      Often insidious  -  fatigue, cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, dry skin/hair, bradycardia, depression, cognitive slowing, menstrual irregularity, myalgia.

      #### Causes
      
        **Hashimoto's thyroiditis**  -  most common; autoimmune; TPO antibodies in >90%
        - Post-thyroidectomy or radioiodine
        - Iodine deficiency (globally most common)
        - Drug-induced: amiodarone, lithium, interferon-alpha, checkpoint inhibitors, tyrosine kinase inhibitors
        - Central hypothyroidism: pituitary/hypothalamic disease  -  suspect if TSH low/normal with low fT4
        - Transient: postpartum thyroiditis, subacute thyroiditis (de Quervain's)
      
      #### Investigation
      
        - TSH + fT4  -  always together if symptoms suggest thyroid disease
        - TPO antibodies  -  confirms autoimmune cause; predicts progression from subclinical to overt
        - Thyroid ultrasound  -  if goitre, nodule, or diagnostic uncertainty (not routine)
        - Lipids, glucose, CK (hypothyroidism causes dyslipidaemia, myopathy)
        - FBC  -  macrocytic anaemia (associated pernicious anaemia in Hashimoto's)

    💊Treatment  -  levothyroxine▾
    
      #### Starting dose
      
        **Young, healthy adults:** start at full replacement dose  -  1.6 µg/kg/day (lean body weight)
        **Elderly or cardiac disease:** start 25 µg/day; increase by 25 µg every 6 - 8 weeks
        - Typical maintenance: 50 - 150 µg/day
      
      #### Administration
      
        - Take on an empty stomach, 30 - 60 minutes before food (or 4 hours after calcium/iron supplements)
        - Consistent timing  -  same time each day
        - Interactions: calcium, iron, antacids, PPIs, cholestyramine, rifampicin all reduce absorption
      
      #### Titration
      
        - Recheck TSH 6 weeks after starting or any dose change
        - Adjust dose by 12.5 - 25 µg increments
        - Target TSH 0.5 - 2.5 mIU/L for most; 0.1 - 1.0 mIU/L in pregnancy
        - Once stable: annual TSH monitoring
        - Symptom-TSH discordance: if TSH normal but symptomatic, consider other causes; rarely add T3 (liothyronine)  -  specialist decision
      
      Overtreatment risksSuppressed TSH on levothyroxine therapy increases risk of atrial fibrillation and osteoporosis. Avoid TSH <0.1 mIU/L unless treating thyroid cancer. Reassess dose in elderly  -  requirements fall with age.

    📊Subclinical hypothyroidism▾
    
      #### Definition
      TSH elevated above upper limit (usually >4.5 mIU/L), fT4 normal, with or without symptoms.

      #### When to treat
      
        **Definitely treat:** TSH >10 mIU/L (high risk of progression)
        **Consider treating:** TSH 4.5 - 10 with symptoms, positive TPO antibodies, pregnancy or planning pregnancy, dyslipidaemia, heart failure
        **Monitor only:** TSH 4.5 - 10, asymptomatic, TPO negative, elderly (>70 yrs)  -  high prevalence of mildly elevated TSH without benefit from treatment
      
      #### Monitoring without treatment
      
        - Recheck TSH + fT4 in 3 - 6 months initially; then annually if stable
        - TPO antibody positive: ~5% per year progression to overt hypothyroidism
      
      #### Hypothyroidism in pregnancy
      
        - TSH target in pregnancy: trimester-specific ranges  -  generally <2.5 mIU/L in 1st trimester
        - Increase levothyroxine dose by 25 - 30% as soon as pregnancy confirmed
        - Check TSH every 4 - 6 weeks in first half of pregnancy; 6-weekly thereafter
        - Subclinical hypothyroidism with positive TPO antibodies: treat in pregnancy

    🚨Myxoedema coma & pearls▾
    
      Myxoedema coma  -  emergencyRare but life-threatening  -  severe hypothyroidism + precipitant (cold, infection, sedatives, trauma). Features: hypothermia, bradycardia, hypotension, hyponatraemia, hypoventilation, altered consciousness. IV T4 (and often T3) + IV hydrocortisone + supportive ICU care. Mortality 30 - 60% even with treatment.

      #### Pearls
      
        - Do not recheck TSH sooner than 6 weeks  -  leads to over-adjustment and instability
        - Calcium, iron and PPIs significantly reduce levothyroxine absorption  -  timing matters
        - Amiodarone can cause both hypo- and hyperthyroidism  -  check TFTs before starting and every 6 months
        - Unexplained dyslipidaemia, macrocytosis, CK elevation  -  check TFTs
        - Central hypothyroidism (low TSH + low fT4) does not respond to normal feedback  -  treat to fT4 target in upper half of normal range
        - Hashimoto's has increased risk of pernicious anaemia and coeliac disease  -  screen if clinically indicated
