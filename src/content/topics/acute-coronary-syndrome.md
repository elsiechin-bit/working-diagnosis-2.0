#### ACS spectrum
          
            **Unstable angina (UA):** Ischaemic chest pain; no myocardial necrosis (troponin negative).
            **NSTEMI:** Myocardial infarction without ST elevation; troponin elevated.
            **STEMI:** MI with ST elevation on ECG; transmural infarction; high-risk, requires urgent reperfusion.
          
          #### Typical presentation
          
            - Chest pain or pressure (often radiating to arm, jaw, back); may be atypical in women, elderly, or diabetics (dyspnoea, nausea, fatigue).
            - Duration typically >20 minutes; not relieved by rest or GTN alone.

        🔬Diagnosis and risk stratification▾
        
          #### 12-lead ECG
          
            **STEMI:** ST elevation ≥1 mm (≥2 mm in precordial leads) in ≥2 contiguous leads.
            **NSTEMI/UA:** ST depression, T wave inversion, or normal ECG.
            - Posterior MI may show ST depression V1-V3.
          
          #### Troponin (cTn)
          
            - Elevated in MI; rises within 2–3 hours, peaks at 24–48 hours, falls over 7–14 days.
            - High-sensitivity troponin allows earlier rule-out or rule-in.
          
          #### Risk scores
          
            **TIMI score:** Predicts 30-day outcome in UA/NSTEMI (0–7 points).
            **GRACE score:** Prognostic in ACS; includes age, HR, BP, Killip class, creatinine, troponin, ST deviation.

        💊Acute management▾
        
          #### Reperfusion strategy
          
            **STEMI:** Primary PCI (door-to-balloon 
            **NSTEMI/UA:** Invasive angiography within 24–72 hours for high-risk; early invasive if haemodynamic instability, arrhythmia, or cardiogenic shock.
          
          #### Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT)
          
            **Aspirin:** 300–600 mg loading, then 75–100 mg daily indefinitely.
            **P2Y12 inhibitor:** Clopidogrel 600 mg loading (or prasugrel 60 mg, ticagrelor 180 mg if PCI planned); then daily for 12 months (or longer if high risk).
          
          #### Anticoagulation
          
            - Unfractionated heparin or LMWH or fondaparinux during acute phase.
          
          #### Acute symptom relief
          
            Oxygen (if SpO2 
            - Nitrates (GTN) for pain; caution if RV infarct or low BP.
            - Opioids (morphine) for pain and anxiety.
            - Beta-blockers (if no contraindication) to reduce HR and myocardial workload.

        🛡️Secondary prevention▾
        
          #### Long-term medications (post-ACS)
          
            **Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor:** 12 months standard; consider longer if high thrombotic or low bleeding risk.
            **Statin:** High-intensity (atorvastatin 80 mg or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg); target LDL-C 
            **ACE inhibitor:** Reduces remodelling and recurrence; especially if LV dysfunction.
            **Beta-blocker:** Reduces arrhythmias and remodelling; continue indefinitely unless contraindicated.
          
          #### Cardiac rehabilitation
          
            - Early ambulation, exercise testing, structured exercise program (Phase II–III).
            - Psychological support (depression common post-MI; screen and treat).
