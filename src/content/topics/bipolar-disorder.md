#### Bipolar I disorder
          
            - ≥1 manic episode (≥7 days, marked impairment in function or hospitalization required).
            - Often preceded or followed by depressive or hypomanic episodes.
            - Manic episode: elevated/expansive mood, increased goal-directed activity, decreased need for sleep, racing thoughts, pressured speech, distractibility, increased risky behaviour (spending, sexual, substance use).
          
          #### Bipolar II disorder
          
            - ≥1 hypomanic episode (≥4 days; similar to mania but less severe, no psychosis, no hospitalization).
            - ≥1 major depressive episode.
            - Never a full manic episode.
          
          #### Cyclothymia
          
            - Chronic mood instability with multiple brief hypomania and depression periods, but not meeting full criteria for episodes.
            - ≥2 years duration (1 year in children/adolescents).

        💊Pharmacological management▾
        
          #### Lithium - gold standard
          
            - Reduces relapse risk in both manic and depressive episodes; anti-suicidal effects.
            - Narrow therapeutic window (0.6–1.2 mmol/L); requires monitoring (lithium level, renal function, TSH every 6–12 months).
            - Side effects: tremor, polyuria/polydipsia, weight gain, hypothyroidism, renal impairment (long-term).
            - Drug interactions: NSAIDs, diuretics, ACE inhibitors increase lithium levels.
          
          #### Valproate / divalproex
          
            - Effective for mania; may be superior to lithium in some patients.
            - Side effects: tremor, sedation, weight gain, teratogenicity (avoid in pregnancy), hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis (rare).
            - Monitor LFTs, platelets, weight.
          
          #### Lamotrigine
          
            - Effective for bipolar depression; less effective for mania.
            - Side effects: rash (Stevens-Johnson syndrome in ~1:1,000; slow titration reduces risk), diplopia, ataxia.
            - Requires slow titration (especially if on valproate, which increases levels).
          
          #### Atypical antipsychotics
          
            **Quetiapine, olanzapine, aripiprazole, risperidone:** Effective for acute mania and maintenance; some also help bipolar depression.
            - Side effects: weight gain, metabolic syndrome, extrapyramidal symptoms (especially risperidone), tardive dyskinesia (long-term).
            - Monitor metabolic parameters (glucose, lipids, weight).

        🗣️Psychosocial interventions▾
        
          #### Psychoeducation
          
            - Critical for adherence; teach about triggers, early warning signs, medication importance.
          
          #### Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT)
          
            - Targets automatic thoughts, behaviour patterns, sleep and routine disruption.
          
          #### Interpersonal and social rhythm therapy (IPSRT)
          
            - Stabilizes sleep-wake cycle and social routines; effective in reducing relapse.
          
          #### Family-focused therapy
          
            - Improves communication, reduces expressed emotion, and enhances support.

      📚Key references▾

          #### Key guidelines & evidence
          
            - DSM-5 Bipolar I and II diagnostic criteria.
            - NICE Bipolar Disorder guidelines (2014, updated).
            - Lithium remains gold-standard mood stabiliser — narrow therapeutic window, requires monitoring.
            - Valproate, lamotrigine, quetiapine, olanzapine, aripiprazole all evidence-based.
            - Acute mania: antipsychotic + benzodiazepine vs lithium + antipsychotic.
            - Maintenance: lithium reduces suicide risk in bipolar disorder.
            - NZ Mental Health Foundation bipolar resources.
