## Overview


**Weight loss pharmacotherapy is adjunctive, not monotherapy.** All effective agents require concurrent lifestyle modification (reduced-calorie diet, exercise, behavioural support). Discontinuation of medication almost universally leads to weight regain. Realistic expectations: 5–22% weight loss over 52 weeks depending on agent; partial weight regain on cessation; long-term maintenance therapy required for sustained benefit.

        #### NZ landscape (as of March 2026)
        
          **Oral medications:** Phentermine (Duromine; sympathomimetic amine), Orlistat (Xenical; lipase inhibitor), Naltrexone/bupropion (Contrave; limited use)
          **Injectable GLP-1 agonists:** Semaglutide (Wegovy), Liraglutide (Saxenda), Tirzepatide (Mounjaro) — approved by Medsafe; NOT PHARMAC-funded for weight loss
          **Oral semaglutide (Rybelsus):** Not approved in NZ for weight loss; approved overseas for diabetes only (lower doses, lower efficacy for weight loss)
          **Oral semaglutide 25 mg (Wegovy pill):** FDA approved December 2025; not yet approved in NZ; pipeline candidate

          PHARMAC funding decision imminent (expected 2026)
          In December 2025, the Obesity Treatments Advisory Group (OTAG) gave provisional high-priority recommendation for PHARMAC to fund semaglutide for weight loss in people with BMI ≥30 + comorbidities. Full decision & eligibility criteria expected by mid-2026.

      💊GLP-1 receptor agonists (injectable)▾
      
        GLP-1 agonists enhance satiety, slow gastric emptying, and suppress appetite via central and peripheral mechanisms. **2024–2025 paradigm shift:** evidence now shows cardiovascular & heart failure benefits independent of weight loss.

        #### Efficacy & cardiovascular evidence
        
          AgentWeight loss (% at 52–72 wks)Key trial / CV outcome
          
            **Semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly (Wegovy)**14.9–15%SELECT trial: 20% ↓ MACE in non-diabetic obese adults with CVD. FDA approval for CV risk reduction 2024.
            **Tirzepatide 15 mg weekly (Mounjaro)**20.2–22.5%SUMMIT trial: 38% ↓ HF worsening events in HFpEF + obesity. SURMOUNT-5: superior to semaglutide in weight loss (head-to-head).
            **Liraglutide 3.0 mg daily (Saxenda)**8–10%SCALE trial: modest CV benefit; less effective than semaglutide/tirzepatide but older, more established.

        #### Mechanisms: beyond weight loss
        
          **Cardiovascular:** Reduced MACE by ~20% independent of diabetes status. Endothelial function improved; vascular inflammation reduced.
          **Heart failure (HFpEF):** Tirzepatide & semaglutide reduce HF worsening events; improved exercise tolerance, symptom burden, and quality of life. Reduced LV mass & cardiac adipose tissue.
          **Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA):** Tirzepatide now FDA-approved for moderate-severe OSA in obesity (December 2024).
          **Kidney protection:** Semaglutide approved for CKD progression reduction in T2DM (2025).
          **Metabolic:** Improved HbA1c, cholesterol, inflammation markers.
        
        #### Dosing & NZ availability
        
          **Semaglutide (Wegovy):** 2.4 mg subcutaneous once weekly. Start 0.25 mg weekly × 4 weeks, titrate by 0.25 mg weekly to 2.4 mg maintenance. Approved Medsafe June 2025. Private cost ~$400–500/month NZ.
          **Tirzepatide (Mounjaro):** 5–15 mg subcutaneous once weekly. Start 2.5 mg weekly × 4 weeks, titrate by 2.5 mg to 15 mg. Approved Medsafe; private cost ~$600/month NZ.
          **Liraglutide (Saxenda):** 3.0 mg subcutaneous once daily. Less commonly used in NZ due to lower efficacy & daily injections vs weekly.
        
        #### Safety & adverse effects
        
          **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation (most common, usually transient, weeks 1–4). Mild-to-moderate; rarely discontinuation-limiting.
          **Pancreatitis:** Rare (~0.1%); counsel on acute abdominal pain. Absolute history of pancreatitis = contraindication.
          **Gallbladder disease:** Increased risk of cholelithiasis & cholecystitis with rapid weight loss (~3–5% over 12 weeks). Screen for gallstones if symptomatic.
          **Dehydration:** GI side effects + reduced fluid intake; monitor for volume depletion.
          **Hyperglycaemia in non-diabetics:** Rare; monitor glucose at baseline.
          **Injection site reactions:** Tirzepatide more common (~2–5%) than semaglutide/liraglutide.
          **Long-term safety:** Studies 40–120 weeks show sustained benefit & tolerable safety. Longer-term (>2 years) outcomes still emerging.
        
        #### Contraindications & caution
        
          **Absolute:** Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma; history of multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN2).
          **Caution:** History of pancreatitis; diabetic retinopathy (may transiently worsen with rapid glucose improvement); HFrEF (limited data; monitor closely); dehydration risk.
          **Pregnancy:** Not recommended; counsel contraception during treatment.

          Weight regain & discontinuation
          Most trials show rapid weight regain (~50% of loss) within 12 months of stopping GLP-1 therapy. Long-term efficacy requires ongoing treatment; patient counselling on this expectation is essential upfront.

      💊Phentermine (Duromine)▾
      
        Sympathomimetic amine; CNS stimulant; appetite suppressant. **Short-term use only** (12 weeks or less in most guidelines, though some data supports longer with ongoing weight loss). Available orally in NZ.

        #### Efficacy & practical aspects
        
          **Weight loss:** ~5–10% over 12–16 weeks. Modest compared to GLP-1 agents; most effective as adjunct with lifestyle modification.
          **Dosing:** 15–30 mg once daily, usually morning (to avoid insomnia).
          **Onset:** 3–5 days; effects diminish if continued beyond 12 weeks (tolerance).
          **Cost:** NZ retail $40–80/month (typically lower than GLP-1 agents); some private insurance may cover.
        
        #### Side effects
        
          **Common:** Dry mouth, insomnia, anxiety, tremor, increased HR & BP, headache.
          **Cardiovascular caution:** Increases HR and BP; baseline ECG recommended for CV risk. Contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension, arrhythmias, unstable angina.
          **Addiction potential:** Schedule agent; monitor for misuse in substance-use history.
        
        #### When to use
        
          - Cost-conscious patients unable to afford GLP-1 therapy.
          - Short-term weight loss before bariatric surgery.
          - Patients who need early appetite suppression (GLP-1 agents require 4+ weeks to titrate).
          - Inability to tolerate GLP-1 GI effects or inject (though often swapped to GLP-1 once phentermine tolerance develops).

          Limited long-term data & tolerance
          Phentermine is **not** a long-term solution. Efficacy plateaus at ~12 weeks due to tolerance. Not recommended for chronic weight maintenance. If prescribed beyond 12 weeks, ongoing weight loss & appropriate BP monitoring essential.

      💊Orlistat (Xenical)▾
      
        Pancreatic & gastric lipase inhibitor; reduces dietary fat absorption by ~30%. **Minimal systemic absorption;** works locally in GI tract. Lowest efficacy among modern weight-loss drugs but safest profile.

        #### Efficacy & practical aspects
        
          **Weight loss:** ~3–5% above placebo over 52 weeks. Often 2–4 kg total. Least effective among available agents.
          **Dosing:** 120 mg three times daily with meals (must contain fat for efficacy). Can be purchased over-counter at lower doses (60 mg); 120 mg is Rx-only NZ.
          **Cost:** ~$20–40/month NZ (least expensive option); often accessible over-counter.
          **Long-term use:** Approved for chronic use, including adolescents & pregnancy (minimal systemic absorption).
        
        #### Side effects (GI focused)
        
          **Common & significant:** Oily stools, faecal urgency, incontinence, flatulence, cramping, defecation urgency (worst in first 2–4 weeks then often improve).
          **Fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption:** Risk of deficiency in A, D, E, K; recommend multivitamin + fat-soluble vitamins 2 hours before/after orlistat.
          **Adherence:** ~50% discontinuation at 12 months due to GI side effects.
          **Minimal systemic toxicity:** Very safe; no cardiovascular or metabolic adverse effects.
        
        #### When to use
        
          - Very cost-conscious or uninsured patients.
          - Low-risk profile needed (pregnancy, elderly, renal/hepatic impairment, cardiovascular disease).
          - Patients unable to tolerate injections or CNS stimulation.
          - Patients with low-fat diets (compliance easier; less GI upset).

          Modest efficacy; high discontinuation
          Orlistat is rarely first-line in modern practice due to low efficacy & significant GI burden. Often used when cost is prohibitive or GLP-1 contraindicated. Not suitable for long-term weight maintenance in most patients.

      🛣️Treatment algorithm & prescribing pathway▾
      
        #### Eligibility criteria (NZ guidance)
        
          **Pharmacotherapy indicated if:** BMI ≥30 + failed lifestyle intervention ≥6 months, OR BMI ≥27 + weight-related comorbidity (diabetes, hypertension, sleep apnoea, CVD, dyslipidaemia)
          **Assessment required:** Baseline weight, waist circumference, CV risk factors, metabolic panel (glucose, lipids, liver/renal function), BP.
        
        #### Preferred prescribing sequence
        **First-line (if accessible):** GLP-1 agonist (semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide)

          - Superior efficacy (15–22% weight loss); cardiovascular & HF benefit; reasonable tolerability.
          - Current barrier: PHARMAC funding pending; cost ~$400–600/month private.
          - If approved by PHARMAC, likely recommend semaglutide (most evidence in non-diabetics; tirzepatide if HFpEF or moderate-severe OSA).
        
        **Second-line (if GLP-1 unavailable/contraindicated/unaffordable):** Phentermine

          - Oral, affordable (~$40–80/month); faster onset; reasonable short-term efficacy.
          - Limit to 12 weeks; unsuitable for long-term maintenance.
          - Screen CV risk before prescribing.
        
        **Third-line (if GLP-1 & phentermine contraindicated):** Orlistat

          - Safest; minimal systemic absorption; very affordable.
          - Modest efficacy & poor tolerability limit use; patient education on realistic outcomes essential.
        
        #### Monitoring & follow-up
        
          **Baseline:** Weight, waist circumference, BP, glucose, lipids, liver & renal function, CV assessment.
          **4 weeks:** Side effect review; efficacy check (minimum 3–5% weight loss expected by week 12 for continuation).
          **12 weeks:** Assess ≥5% weight loss; if achieved, continue or consider increasing dose/agent. If 
          **6–12 monthly:** Reassess weight, comorbidities, medication tolerance, adherence to lifestyle changes.
          **Weight regain:** If >2 kg regain, consider dose increase or addition of second agent; counsel on dietary/exercise drift.
        
        #### Transitioning between agents
        
          **Phentermine → GLP-1:** Stop phentermine, begin GLP-1 at lowest dose. Wait 7–10 days to minimise stimulant rebound appetite. Do not overlap.
          **GLP-1 → different GLP-1:** Can switch within class without washout; titrate new agent from minimum dose to minimise GI upset.
          **Orlistat → GLP-1 or phentermine:** Can overlap briefly; no washout needed.

      💡Clinical pearls & pitfalls▾
      
        #### Pearls
        
          ✦**Weight-loss meds are adjunctive only.** All require concurrent lifestyle modification (diet, exercise, behaviour). No medication works without lifestyle support.
          ✦**GLP-1 agonists are game-changing for CVD & HF.** 2024–2025 data show 20% ↓ MACE, HF benefits beyond weight loss alone. Consider CV comorbidities when choosing agent.
          ✦**Tirzepatide > semaglutide in head-to-head weight loss (SURMOUNT-5).** But semaglutide has more CV data & lower injection site reactions. Choice individualised.
          ✦**GLP-1 agents require 6+ months assessment.** Titration takes 4–6 weeks; full metabolic effect at 12+ weeks. Do not declare failure before 6 months on stable dose.
          ✦**Weight regain is the rule, not exception, on cessation.** Most men regain ~50% of weight loss within 12 months of stopping. Set realistic long-term expectations upfront.
          ✦**PHARMAC funding likely soon (2026).** Semaglutide given provisional high-priority recommendation Dec 2025. Cost barrier will lift if approved.
          ✦**Phentermine is 12-week maximum.** Tolerance develops; not a long-term agent. Use for short-term weight loss before surgery or transition to GLP-1.
          ✦**Orlistat is lowest efficacy but safest.** ~3–5% loss; significant GI burden; minimal systemic toxicity. Use when cost/contraindications prohibit GLP-1/phentermine.
          ✦**HFpEF + obesity? Consider tirzepatide.** SUMMIT trial showed 38% ↓ HF worsening; now preferred over other agents in this phenotype.
        
        #### Pitfalls
        
          **Prescribing medication without lifestyle support.** Weight-loss drugs fail if diet & exercise unchanged. Multidisciplinary team (GP, dietitian, psychologist) improves adherence & outcomes.
          **Declaring GLP-1 failure before 6 months.** Many patients need 12+ weeks to achieve maximum effect; reassess at stable dose before switching.
          **Continuing phentermine beyond 12 weeks.** Tolerance develops; efficacy plateaus. If using >12 weeks, verify ongoing weight loss & appropriate BP monitoring.
          **Not screening CV risk before phentermine.** Sympathomimetics raise HR & BP; baseline ECG advised in CV disease.
          **Not counselling on weight regain risk.** Patients expect permanent weight loss; realistic expectation-setting prevents dissatisfaction & improves long-term engagement.
          **Overlooking comorbidities when selecting agent.** CVD/HF? Prioritise GLP-1 (CV benefit). OSA? Tirzepatide (FDA approval). Renal disease? Semaglutide (CKD benefit).
          **Forgetting GI side effect management in GLP-1 therapy.** Nausea/diarrhoea often resolve with time; do not abandon too early. Small, frequent meals, hydration, ginger may help.
          **Skipping baseline & monitoring labs.** Ensure no contraindications; track CV risk, metabolic markers, renal function.
