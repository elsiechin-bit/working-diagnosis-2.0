## Investigations


The purpose of investigation in primary care is twofold: to exclude contributors that are treatable, and to inform whether and where to refer. A standard panel:2,3

          First-line work-up for suspected dementia
          TestWhy
          
            FBC, ESR/CRPAnaemia, infection, inflammation as contributors to confusion
            Electrolytes, urea, creatinine, calciumHyponatraemia, renal impairment, hypercalcaemia
            Glucose / HbA1cDiabetes, hypo- and hyperglycaemia
            TSHHypothyroidism (and hyperthyroidism)
            Vitamin B12 and folateDeficiency causing reversible cognitive impairment
            LFTsHepatic encephalopathy, alcohol-related harm
            Medication reviewAnticholinergic burden, sedatives, opioids - common and reversible
            Consider: HIV, syphilis serologyWhere history or epidemiology warrants

        Structural neuroimaging - CT head as a minimum, MRI where available and tolerated - is recommended to exclude a structural lesion (subdural haematoma, tumour, normal-pressure hydrocephalus) and to characterise vascular burden and atrophy patterns.3 Delirium must be actively excluded: an acute or fluctuating change in cognition with altered attention is delirium until proven otherwise, and it is a medical emergency, not dementia.

          ### When to refer beyond primary care
          Diagnostic uncertainty, young-onset presentation (under 65), rapid progression, prominent or early behavioural change, atypical features (early parkinsonism, hallucinations, focal signs), or where specialist confirmation is needed for treatment, family, or medicolegal reasons. Memory services, geriatric medicine, old-age psychiatry, and neurology share this work regionally; many areas now run nurse-led memory clinics with GP-shared follow-up.
