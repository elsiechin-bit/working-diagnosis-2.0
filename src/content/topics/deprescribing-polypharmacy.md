## Overview


Deprescribing is the planned, supervised process of dose reduction or stopping of medicines that may be causing harm or no longer providing benefit. It is a positive, proactive part of good prescribing - not "giving up".

  Trigger a medicines review when there is:

   **Polypharmacy** (often ≥5 regular medicines), especially in frailty or limited life expectancy
   A **fall, confusion, or hospital admission**
   A possible **prescribing cascade** (a new drug treating an old drug’s side effect)
   **Changed goals of care** - shifting from prevention to comfort/function

      🧰A structured approach▾
      
   **Reconcile** the full list (including OTC, supplements) and confirm what is actually taken.
   **Apply explicit tools** - STOPP/START and Beers criteria to flag potentially inappropriate medicines and under-prescribing.
   **Assess each drug:** ongoing indication, benefit vs harm in *this* patient, time-to-benefit vs life expectancy, and patient priorities.
   **Prioritise** the highest-risk/lowest-benefit drugs to stop first.
   **Agree a plan** with the patient/whānau, taper where needed, and **monitor** for withdrawal effects and return of symptoms.
  
  #### Common targets in the older adult
  
   **Anticholinergics and sedatives** (the "Z-drugs", benzodiazepines, sedating antihistamines, oxybutynin, tricyclics) - falls and cognition.
   **PPIs** continued without indication; **long-term NSAIDs**.
   **Antipsychotics** for BPSD beyond review periods.
   **Tight glycaemic/BP control** in frailty (hypoglycaemia, falls).
   **Bisphosphonates** beyond a drug-holiday review point; preventive statins near end of life.

      📉Tapering & safety▾
      
   **Taper drugs with withdrawal/rebound risk:** benzodiazepines, opioids, SSRIs/SNRIs, beta-blockers, gabapentinoids, PPIs, corticosteroids.
   **Stop one drug at a time** where possible, so cause and effect are clear.
   **Safety-net:** tell the patient what to watch for and how to restart/seek help.
   **Document the rationale** and review - deprescribing is iterative, not one-and-done.

   ✦**Total the anticholinergic burden** - small contributions add up to real harm.
   ✦**Look for cascades** - is a drug treating another drug’s side effect?
   ✦**Match treatment to time-to-benefit** - preventive drugs need years the patient may not have.
   ✦**Taper the dependence-forming ones;** stop one at a time.
