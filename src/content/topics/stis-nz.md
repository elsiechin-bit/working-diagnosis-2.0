#### Chlamydia trachomatis
          
            **Most common reportable STI in NZ;** asymptomatic in 70–80% women, 50% men.
            **Diagnosis:** NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test) on first-catch urine or urethral/cervical swab; most sensitive.
            **Treatment:** Azithromycin 1g single dose (or doxycycline 100 mg BD x7 days if pregnant: use azithromycin).
            **Complications:** PID (pelvic inflammatory disease), ectopic pregnancy, infertility; in men: urethritis, epididymitis, proctitis.
          
          #### Neisseria gonorrhoeae
          
            - Often symptomatic; urethritis, urogenital discharge, dysuria; PID, prostatitis, disseminated gonococcal infection (arthritis, skin lesions) if untreated.
            **Diagnosis:** NAAT (urine, urethral, cervical, rectal, pharyngeal).
            **Treatment:** Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM single dose PLUS azithromycin 2g PO (or doxycycline x7 days). Resistance rising; higher doses may be needed soon.
            **Resistance alert:** Cefalosporin-resistant gonorrhoea emerging; monitor susceptibility.
          
          #### Treponema pallidum (syphilis)
          
            **Primary:** Chancre (painless ulcer) at inoculation site; regional lymphadenopathy.
            **Secondary:** Rash (including palms/soles), systemic symptoms, condyloma lata.
            **Tertiary:** Neurosyphilis, tabes dorsalis, gummas (rare in modern era).
            **Diagnosis:** RPR/VDRL (non-specific, titre tracks treatment response); treponemal test (FTA-abs, TP-PA, EIA) for confirmation.
            **Treatment:** Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 MU IM weekly x3 (early syphilis); neurosyphilis requires IV aqueous penicillin.
          
          #### Herpes simplex virus (HSV)
          
            - Vesicular lesions, pain, systemic symptoms; recurrent in 40% (usually milder).
            **Diagnosis:** PCR most sensitive; culture older/less reliable.
            **Treatment:** Acyclovir 400 mg 5x daily x10 days (primary) or 5 days (recurrence); daily suppression if >6 recurrences/year.
          
          #### HPV and cervical cancer
          
            - 16, 18: high-risk; responsible for >99% cervical cancers.
            **Prevention:** Gardasil-9 vaccine covers 9 types; funded for school-age (Year 7–8) and catch-up to age 26 in NZ.
            **Screening:** Cervical smears (liquid cytology) 3-yearly from age 25–69 in NZ.

        🔬Screening and partner notification▾
        
          #### NZ screening recommendations
          
            All sexually active persons 
            - Older adults (25+): if new partner, >1 partner, or symptoms.
            - Pregnancy: syphilis, chlamydia, gonorrhoea screening at booking; repeat 3rd trimester if risk.
          
          #### Partner notification
          
            - Chlamydia, gonorrhoea: trace and treat recent partners (last 3 months or last partner if >3 months since last contact).
            - Syphilis: contact tracing varies by stage; primary/secondary have infectious partners within weeks to months.
            - NZ uses partner-assisted/index-assisted notification; health provider can contact if patient unable/unwilling.

        📊NZ epidemiology and disparities▾
        
          #### Incidence trends
          
            - Chlamydia highest in young people 15–24 years; higher rates in Māori and Pacific populations.
            - Gonorrhoea: rising; concentrated in younger populations and Māori/Pacific (due to systemic barriers to care, healthcare access).
            - Syphilis: resurgent; historically controlled, now increasing in MSM populations and in some jurisdictions among heterosexuals.
          
          #### Health equity
          
            - Māori and Pacific peoples: higher incidence due to limited healthcare access, health literacy gaps, stigma.
            - MSM and transgender: higher risk; targeted health promotion and services critical.
