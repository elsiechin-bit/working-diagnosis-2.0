#### GERD (gastro-oesophageal reflux disease)
          
            - Chronic reflux of gastric contents into oesophagus; symptoms ≥2x/week or use of antireflux medication ≥1x/week.
            - Pathophysiology: Lower oesophageal sphincter (LES) dysfunction, delayed gastric emptying, increased intra-gastric pressure, impaired clearance.
          
          #### Typical symptoms
          
            - Heartburn (central chest/epigastric burning), regurgitation, nocturnal symptoms, dysphagia.
          
          #### Atypical presentations
          
            - Chronic cough, laryngitis, asthma exacerbation, dental erosion.
          
          #### Complications
          
            - Erosive oesophagitis, Barrett's oesophagus (metaplasia; increased dysplasia risk), oesophageal adenocarcinoma, strictures.

        🍎Lifestyle modifications▾
        
          #### Dietary triggers (individualised)
          
            - Fatty foods, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, spicy foods, citrus, tomato products.
            - Large meals; eat smaller, frequent meals.
          
          #### Behaviours
          
            - Avoid eating within 3 hours of bedtime.
            - Elevate head of bed (20–30°).
            - Weight loss if overweight (5–10% reduction often helps).
            - Quit smoking; reduce alcohol.
            - Avoid tight clothing, bending after meals.

        💊Pharmacotherapy▾
        
          #### Antacids and alginates
          
            **Antacids:** Calcium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide; rapid relief; frequent dosing needed.
            **Alginates:** Form protective raft on gastric contents; good for frequent symptoms.
          
          #### H2-receptor antagonists
          
            **Ranitidine, famotidine:** Reduce acid secretion; slower onset than antacids; once or twice daily; good for mild-moderate symptoms.
          
          #### Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) - first-line for moderate-severe
          
            **Omeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole:** Most potent acid suppressors; 80% acid reduction; daily or BID dosing.
            **Long-term use considerations:** Risk of hypomagnesaemia, B12 and calcium malabsorption (monitor), C. difficile, pneumonia (weak associations). Benefit usually outweighs risk if needed.
          
          #### Pro-motility agents
          
            **Domperidone:** For gastroparesis component; limited efficacy in pure GERD.

        🔬H. pylori testing and screening▾
        
          #### Indications for testing
          
            - Peptic ulcer disease (active or history).
            - Chronic gastritis, family history of gastric cancer, or in countries with high H. pylori prevalence before PPI use (NZ guidelines variable).
          
          #### Tests
          
            **Urea breath test (UBT):** Gold standard; non-invasive; 95% sensitivity.
            **Stool antigen:** Cheaper, non-invasive; sensitivity ~90%.
            **Serology:** Detects antibody; cannot distinguish active from past infection; less useful.
            **Endoscopy + biopsy:** If upper GI symptoms warrant endoscopy anyway.
          
          #### Treatment
          
            **Triple therapy:** PPI + clarithromycin + amoxicillin x10–14 days; eradication ~80–90%.
            **Quadruple (bismuth):** For macrolide-resistant strains; ~95% eradication.

        ⚠️Alarm symptoms - when to refer▾

            - Age >60 at symptom onset.
            - Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), odynophagia (pain with swallowing).
            - Weight loss.
            - Persistent vomiting.
            - GI bleeding (haematemesis, melena).
            - Severe symptoms despite PPI therapy.
          
          Indication for upper endoscopy to exclude Barrett's, dysplasia, cancer.
