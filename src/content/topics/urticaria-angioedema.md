## Overview


**Urticaria** (weals/hives) is itchy, raised, transient and blanching; each lesion lasts <24 h. **Angioedema** is deeper dermal/subcutaneous swelling (lips, eyelids, tongue, hands). They often coexist.

  The airway is the emergencyTongue/throat swelling, stridor, or any features of anaphylaxis (wheeze, hypotension, gut symptoms with a clear trigger) need **IM adrenaline** and emergency care - not just antihistamines.

   **Acute** (<6 weeks): often post-viral, drug or food related; usually self-limiting.
   **Chronic** (&ge;6 weeks): spontaneous or inducible (cold, pressure, cholinergic, dermographism).
   **Isolated angioedema without weals** - consider **ACE-inhibitor-induced** (can occur years in) and hereditary/acquired C1-esterase inhibitor deficiency (antihistamine-unresponsive).

      💊Management▾
      
   **Stop the trigger** where identified (NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, opioids, the relevant allergen).
   **First-line: a non-sedating H1 antihistamine** (cetirizine, loratadine, fexofenadine), regularly. If not controlled, **up-titrate to up to 4× the standard dose** (off-label but guideline-endorsed) before adding anything.
   **Short oral steroid course** for severe acute flares only - not for maintenance.
   **Refractory chronic urticaria:** refer for omalizumab (anti-IgE) or ciclosporin.
   - Chronic spontaneous urticaria is usually self-limiting over months to a couple of years; reassure and avoid unnecessary allergy testing.

   ✦**Weal >24 h or bruising** → think urticarial vasculitis, biopsy.
   ✦**Angioedema without weals on an ACE inhibitor** - stop the drug.
   ✦**Up-titrate the antihistamine** before escalating.
   ✦**Chronic urticaria is usually not allergy** - skip the food panels.
