#### Reasonable baseline in typical IBS without red flags
          
            - FBC and CRP (or ESR)
            **Coeliac serology** (tissue transglutaminase) - coeliac disease commonly masquerades as IBS
            **Faecal calprotectin** in younger patients with diarrhoea to help exclude IBD
            - Consider TSH if other features suggest thyroid disease
          
          Don't over-investigateIn a young patient with typical symptoms, normal examination and no red flags, colonoscopy is not required. Excessive testing reinforces illness behaviour and rarely changes management.

        🥗Diet and lifestyle▾

            **First-line advice:** regular meals, adequate hydration, limit caffeine, alcohol, fizzy drinks and resistant starch; adjust fibre by subtype (soluble fibre such as ispaghula for IBS-C; reduce insoluble fibre if it worsens symptoms)
            **Low-FODMAP diet** is effective but should be dietitian-led, time-limited and followed by structured reintroduction - it is not an indefinite diet and depletes beneficial gut bacteria long-term
            - Physical activity and addressing stress and sleep help symptom burden

        💊Pharmacotherapy by symptom▾
        
          #### Symptom-targeted
          
            **Pain/spasm:** antispasmodics (mebeverine, hyoscine) and peppermint oil
            **IBS-C:** soluble fibre, an osmotic laxative such as macrogol (avoid lactulose - worsens bloating); linaclotide where available for refractory constipation with pain
            **IBS-D:** loperamide titrated to effect; consider a bile-acid sequestrant if bile-acid diarrhoea suspected
          
          #### Neuromodulators
          
            **Low-dose tricyclic** (amitriptyline) - good for IBS-D and pain; start low at night
            **SSRI** can help, particularly in IBS-C or with coexisting anxiety/depression

        🧠Gut-brain therapies▾
        
          IBS is a disorder of gut-brain interaction, and psychological therapies have a solid evidence base for refractory symptoms.

            **CBT** (including gut-directed) reduces symptom severity and improves quality of life
            **Gut-directed hypnotherapy** has good evidence for global symptom improvement
            - Frame this as treating the gut-brain axis, not as "it's all in your head" - explain the physiology to maintain the therapeutic alliance
