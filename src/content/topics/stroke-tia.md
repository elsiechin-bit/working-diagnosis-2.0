#### Ischaemic stroke (80–85%)
          
            **Large vessel atherosclerotic:** Thrombosis of carotid, middle cerebral, basilar arteries.
            **Cardioembolic:** Embolus from atrial fibrillation, cardiac thrombus, prosthetic valve.
            **Small vessel (lacunar):** Penetrating artery disease; hypertension related.
          
          #### Haemorrhagic stroke (15–20%)
          
            **Intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH):** Rupture of small penetrating arteries; hypertension most common cause.
            **Subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH):** Rupture of aneurysm or AVM; severe headache typical.
          
          #### Transient ischaemic attack (TIA)
          
            Focal neurological deficit 
            - 10–20% risk of stroke within 3 months if untreated.

        ⚡Acute assessment and thrombolysis▾
        
          #### NIHSS (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale)
          
            - 0–42 score; measures stroke severity.
            **0–4:** Minor stroke.
            **5–15:** Moderate.
            **16–20:** Moderate-severe.
            **21+:** Severe.
          
          #### Imaging
          
            **CT head (non-contrast):** Rule out haemorrhage; ischaemic changes subtle early on.
            **MRI:** More sensitive for acute ischaemia (DWI); preferred if available.
            **CT/MR angiography:** Identifies large vessel occlusion for thrombectomy.
          
          #### Thrombolysis (IV alteplase / tPA)
          
            **Window:** ≤4.5 hours from symptom onset (ideally 
            **Dose:** 0.9 mg/kg (max 90 mg); 10% as bolus, remainder over 60 min.
            **Efficacy:** 30% relative improvement in outcomes; higher benefit within 3 hours.
            **Complications:** Intracranial haemorrhage (6–7%); symptomatic ICH 2–3%.
          
          #### Mechanical thrombectomy
          
            - For large vessel occlusion (ICA, MCA M1); window ≤24 hours with advanced imaging.
            - Superior to thrombolysis alone; should be done within 12–24 hours if available.

        🛡️Secondary prevention▾
        
          #### Antiplatelet therapy
          
            **Aspirin:** 75–300 mg daily; first-line for non-cardioembolic stroke/TIA.
            **Clopidogrel + aspirin:** Short-term (10–21 days) for high-risk TIA or minor stroke (POINT trial).
            **Dipyridamole + aspirin:** Superior to aspirin alone in some trials.
          
          #### Anticoagulation
          
            **Atrial fibrillation:** DOAC (apixaban, rivaroxaban, etc.) or warfarin (INR 2–3); start within 2 weeks if ischaemic stroke, can delay 24 hours if large infarction.
            **Other indications:** LV thrombus, hypercoagulable state, prosthetic valve.

          #### Risk factor modification
          
            - Smoking cessation, diabetes control, weight loss, exercise, diet (Mediterranean).

        🏥Rehabilitation and recovery▾
        
          #### Early mobilization and therapy
          
            - Physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech pathology within 24–48 hours.
          
          #### Neuroplasticity and recovery
          
            - Greatest recovery 3–6 months; some improvement up to 1–2 years with intensive therapy.
          
          #### Mood and cognition
          
            - Screen for post-stroke depression (40% incidence); treat with antidepressants and therapy.

      📚Key references▾

          #### Key guidelines & evidence
          
            - ESC Stroke Guidelines (2021).
            - AHA/ASA Stroke Prevention and Management Guidelines.
            - Ischaemic stroke — thrombolysis (IV tPA) within 4.5 hours; thrombectomy within 24 hours in selected patients.
            - NIHSS score guides severity and prognosis.
            - TIA is medical emergency — risk of stroke within 48 hours is ~5%; ABCD2 score guides risk.
            - Antiplatelet (aspirin) therapy acute post-TIA; switch to clopidogrel if aspirin intolerant.
            - AF screening essential — if found, anticoagulation significantly reduces recurrent stroke.
