      ## About this calculator
      Pregnancy and the postpartum period are times of significantly elevated VTE risk. This calculator uses evidence-based risk stratification to guide decisions about thromboprophylaxis during pregnancy and up to 6 weeks postpartum.

      Risk assessment should be individualised and combined with clinical judgment. This tool is designed to support decision-making, not replace it.

        ### Risk Assessment Checklist

          Age and obesity

              Age > 35 years

              BMI > 30 kg/m²

          Obstetric factors

              Primigravida

              Multiple pregnancy

              Assisted conception

              Pre-eclampsia or gestational hypertension

              Previous IUGR or placental insufficiency

          Delivery-related factors

              Caesarean section (any indication)

              Emergency caesarean (particularly for failure to progress)

              Prolonged labour

              Postpartum haemorrhage requiring transfusion

              Immobility > 3 days (bed rest)

          Medical and surgical history

              Active cancer

              Personal history of VTE

              Family history of VTE

              Thrombophilia (high-risk or multiple clotting disorders)

              Significant cardiac disease

              Recent major surgery within 6 weeks

              Current or recent hormone replacement therapy

          Infection and systemic factors

              Systemic infection requiring hospitalisation (including COVID-19)

              Inflammatory bowel disease or connective tissue disease

              Nephrotic syndrome

      ## Risk Stratification and Management

          Risk Level
          Score
          Criteria
          Management

          **Low**
          0 - 1
          No significant risk factors
          Standard antenatal care; mobilisation; compression stockings if immobile; post-delivery VTE prophylaxis not routinely indicated unless other factors present

          **Intermediate**
          2 - 4
          One or more moderate risk factors
          Consider antenatal LMWH prophylaxis (especially if multiple factors); recommend early mobilisation; compression if prolonged immobility; post-delivery LMWH for 6 - 10 days minimum

          **High**
          5+
          Significant cumulative risk (e.g., previous VTE, thrombophilia, multiple factors)
          Antenatal LMWH prophylaxis strongly recommended; post-delivery LMWH for minimum 6 weeks; consider extending post-delivery anticoagulation if additional risk present at delivery

      ## Practical Guidance
      ### During pregnancy
      LMWH dosing: Standard dosing based on pre-pregnancy weight (e.g., enoxaparin 40 mg daily or 1 mg/kg daily for therapeutic dosing). Consider pharmacokinetic monitoring if extreme body weight.

      Transition: LMWH can usually be continued throughout pregnancy and delivery. Switch to IV UFH if labour imminent or if requiring urgent delivery.

      ### Postpartum
      Duration: Minimum 6 - 10 days after vaginal delivery; minimum 6 weeks after caesarean section or if complicated delivery.

      Extended prophylaxis: Consider extending to 6 weeks postpartum if high-risk features present at time of delivery (e.g., emergency caesarean, postpartum haemorrhage, infection).

      Breastfeeding: LMWH and fondaparinux are compatible with breastfeeding. Warfarin is also safe.

      ### When to withhold prophylaxis
      Antenatal prophylaxis should not routinely be given to women with a single intermediate risk factor. Individualise based on cumulative risk and baseline risk.

        ### Evidence Base
        This calculator is informed by:

          - Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists (RCOG) Green-top Guideline 37a: Venous thromboembolism: reducing the risk in pregnancy and the puerperium (2015)
          - American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) Practice Bulletin 196: Thromboembolism in Pregnancy (2018)
          - European Society of Cardiology Guidelines on cardiovascular disease in pregnancy (2018)
          - New Zealand Ministry of Health guidance on VTE risk in pregnancy

        ### Clinical Safety Note
        This calculator is a clinical support tool. Risk assessment should always be individualised and informed by clinical judgment. All pregnant women require assessment for VTE risk as part of routine antenatal care. Consider specialist haematology input in cases of previous VTE, thrombophilia, or cumulative high-risk factors.
