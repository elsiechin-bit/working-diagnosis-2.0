#### Who to test
      Perform spirometry in any adult ≥35 with dyspnoea, chronic cough, sputum production or wheeze, and any relevant exposure (smoking, biomass fuel, occupational dust).

      #### Spirometry interpretation
      
        - Post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC <0.70 = airflow obstruction (COPD criterion)
        - FVC <80% predicted with preserved ratio = restriction (consider ILD, obesity, neuromuscular)
        - Significant bronchodilator reversibility (>12% and >200 mL FEV1 increase)  -  consider asthma-COPD overlap
      
      Do not diagnose COPD without spirometryClinical diagnosis has poor accuracy. Pre-bronchodilator spirometry is used for screening only; post-bronchodilator values confirm diagnosis. Never diagnose on flow-volume loop alone.

      #### Initial investigations
      
        - CXR  -  exclude other pathology (malignancy, heart failure, TB), not sensitive for COPD
        - FBC  -  polycythaemia (chronic hypoxaemia), anaemia (worsens dyspnoea)
        - Alpha-1-antitrypsin level  -  in GOLD 3 - 4, early-onset COPD (<45 yrs), or family history of emphysema
        - Oximetry  -  SpO2 <92%: consider ABG to assess for LTOT candidacy
        - ECG and echo if cor pulmonale suspected

    💊Inhaler therapy▾
    
      #### Principles
      Inhaler therapy is titrated by symptom burden and exacerbation frequency, not by spirometry grade alone. Use validated tools: **mMRC dyspnoea score** and **CAT (COPD Assessment Test)**.

      #### Stepwise approach (GOLD 2024)
      
        #### Step 1  -  Mild (mMRC 0 - 1, CAT <10)
          **SABA PRN** (salbutamol)
          Short-acting anticholinergic PRN (ipratropium) as alternative
        
        #### Step 2  -  Moderate symptoms
          **LAMA** (tiotropium, umeclidinium)
          Or LABA (salmeterol, formoterol) if LAMA not tolerated
        
        #### Step 3  -  High symptoms / frequent exacerbations
          **LAMA + LABA** dual bronchodilation
          Preferred over ICS/LABA for exacerbation prevention
        
        #### Step 4  -  Triple therapy
          **ICS + LAMA + LABA**
          Reserved for: ≥2 exacerbations/year, eosinophils ≥300, or asthma-COPD overlap

      ICS  -  use selectively in COPDICS is not first-line in COPD. Add ICS (in triple therapy) only for frequent exacerbators or blood eosinophils ≥300. ICS increases pneumonia risk. Withdraw ICS if eosinophils <100 and no exacerbations on dual therapy.

      #### Device selection
      
        - Match device to patient ability: pMDI (with spacer) for those who can coordinate; DPI for those who cannot
        - Check technique at every visit  -  poor technique is extremely common and negates efficacy
        - Nebulisers rarely offer benefit over optimal inhaled therapy; reserve for severe cases

    🔥Exacerbations▾
    
      #### Definition
      Acute worsening of respiratory symptoms beyond normal day-to-day variation, requiring a change in therapy. Most are triggered by respiratory infections (bacterial or viral).

      #### Severity classification
      
        **Mild:** managed with increased SABA only
        **Moderate:** requires systemic steroids and/or antibiotics
        **Severe:** requires hospitalisation; consider ICU if respiratory failure
      
      #### Moderate exacerbation  -  GP management
      
        **Corticosteroids:** prednisolone 30 - 40 mg/day for 5 days (5-day equivalent to 14-day for outcomes)
        **Antibiotics:** if increased sputum purulence + increased volume + increased dyspnoea (Anthonisen criteria)  -  amoxicillin, doxycycline or azithromycin
        - Increase SABA frequency; consider adding ipratropium
        - Review maintenance inhalers  -  escalate if on step 1 - 2
      
      When to admitAdmit for: SpO2 <90%, RR >30, altered consciousness, cyanosis, new arrhythmia, failure to improve on initial treatment, inadequate home support. Target SpO2 88 - 92% with controlled oxygen (not high-flow).

      #### Prevention of future exacerbations
      
        - Optimise maintenance inhalers (escalate to LAMA+LABA or triple if needed)
        - Pulmonary rehabilitation after each exacerbation  -  reduces re-hospitalisation
        - Influenza and pneumococcal vaccination
        - Azithromycin 250 - 500 mg 3×/week (for frequent exacerbators  -  specialist decision; caution: QT, hearing, resistance)
        - Roflumilast (PDE-4 inhibitor)  -  in GOLD 3 - 4 with chronic bronchitis phenotype and frequent exacerbations

    🚭Smoking cessation & non-pharmacological▾
    
      #### Smoking cessation
      The most effective intervention at any COPD stage  -  slows decline in FEV1 and reduces exacerbation risk. The 5 A's framework: Ask, Advise, Assess, Assist, Arrange.

        **Pharmacotherapy:** varenicline (most effective, OR ~3×), combination NRT, bupropion
        - Combine behavioural support with pharmacotherapy  -  significantly improves quit rates
        - E-cigarettes: may help some smokers but not recommended as a first-line cessation tool
      
      #### Pulmonary rehabilitation (PR)
      Supervised exercise + education programme  -  the most effective intervention for improving dyspnoea and quality of life. Refer after any hospitalisation and for GOLD 2+ with significant symptoms.

      #### Long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT)
      
        - Indicated if resting PaO2 ≤55 mmHg (or ≤59 with polycythaemia/cor pulmonale)
        - Must use ≥15 hrs/day including during sleep to gain survival benefit
        - Assess via ABG after 4+ weeks of stable state
      
      #### Other
      
        - Annual influenza vaccination; pneumococcal and COVID-19 vaccination
        - Nutrition: weight loss and muscle wasting are poor prognostic factors  -  dietitian input
        - Advance care planning for GOLD 3 - 4

    📈Monitoring & follow-up▾

        ParameterFrequency / notes
        
          SpirometryAt diagnosis; annually or with significant clinical change
          CAT / mMRC scoreEach visit  -  tracks symptom burden and guides escalation
          Exacerbation historyNumber, severity and triggers  -  drives ICS decision
          Inhaler techniqueEvery visit  -  poor technique is the most correctable issue
          OximetryAnnually; more often GOLD 3 - 4 or if SpO2 <92%
          Blood eosinophilsAt baseline and when considering ICS withdrawal or addition
          BMI / nutritionLow BMI (<21) independently predicts mortality

      #### Pearls & pitfalls
      
        - Spirometry is mandatory  -  do not diagnose or stage COPD clinically
        - ICS is not first-line  -  use LAMA or LAMA+LABA first in most patients
        - Check eosinophils before adding ICS  -  ≥300 supports benefit; <100 suggests no benefit
        - 5-day prednisolone is as effective as 14-day for exacerbations
        - Refer for PR after hospitalisation  -  it is under-referred and highly effective
        - SpO2 88 - 92% is the target in exacerbation  -  high-flow oxygen causes hypercapnia
