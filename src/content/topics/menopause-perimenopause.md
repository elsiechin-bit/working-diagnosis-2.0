## Overview


**Over 45 with typical symptoms:** diagnose clinically, no FSH needed
            **FSH is useful** when the picture is unclear: women under 45 (and especially under 40 for suspected premature ovarian insufficiency), or where hysterectomy makes cycle history unavailable
            - FSH is unreliable while on combined hormonal contraception or in the fluctuating perimenopause

        💊MHT - what to prescribe▾
        
          #### Oestrogen
          
            **Transdermal oestrogen** (patch or gel) is preferred where there is VTE risk, migraine, higher BMI or cardiovascular risk - it avoids the first-pass VTE risk seen with oral oestrogen
          
          #### Progestogen (endometrial protection)
          
            Any woman **with a uterus** needs a progestogen to prevent endometrial hyperplasia; a levonorgestrel intrauterine system is an excellent option that also provides contraception
            - Women without a uterus need oestrogen alone
          
          #### Regimen
          
            - Cyclical progestogen in perimenopause (still having periods); continuous combined once postmenopausal

        ⚖️Benefit and risk▾

            **Benefits:** highly effective for vasomotor symptoms and genitourinary symptoms; improves quality of life and protects bone
            **Breast cancer:** a small increase with combined MHT, related to duration; oestrogen-only carries little or no increase. Put the absolute risk in context against lifestyle factors.
            **VTE:** increased with oral but not significantly with transdermal oestrogen
            **Cardiovascular:** starting MHT under 60 or within 10 years of menopause is favourable ("timing hypothesis"); MHT is not used solely for cardiovascular prevention
          
          IndividualiseThere is no arbitrary stop age. Review annually, use the lowest effective dose for symptom control, and base duration on the woman's symptoms and risk profile.

        🌸Genitourinary syndrome & non-hormonal options▾
        
          #### Genitourinary syndrome of menopause
          
            **Vaginal oestrogen** is safe, effective and can be used long-term, including alongside systemic MHT and (with specialist input) in many women with a history of breast cancer; vaginal moisturisers and lubricants help
          
          #### Non-hormonal for vasomotor symptoms
          
            **SSRIs/SNRIs** (e.g. venlafaxine, escitalopram), **gabapentin**, and **CBT** all reduce flushes when MHT is unsuitable or declined
            - Lifestyle: layered clothing, reduce triggers, weight management, exercise

      Premature ovarian insufficiencyMenopause before 40 needs investigation and, unless contraindicated, hormone replacement continued until at least the average age of menopause (~51) to protect bone, cardiovascular and brain health - this is replacement, not optional symptom relief.
