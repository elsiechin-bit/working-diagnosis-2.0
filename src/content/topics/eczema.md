#### Atopic dermatitis (AD)Chronic inflammatory skin disease; genetically predisposed with impaired skin barrier and immune dysregulation.
#### Emollients- Apply frequently after bathing; creams and ointments more occlusive than lotions.- Examples: Cetaphil, QV, E45 (gentle, fragrance-free).#### Topical corticosteroids- Mild potency for face (hydrocortisone 1%); mild-moderate for body (triamcinolone 0.1%).- Use 2-4 weeks during flares; taper and switch to emollients for maintenance.#### Systemic: Dupilumab- Anti-IL-4Ra monoclonal antibody; very effective; 2-week injections; improves itch within days.

          #### Key guidelines & evidence
          
            - BAD Atopic Dermatitis Management Guidelines.
            - AAD Eczema/Atopic Dermatitis Guidelines.
            - Emollients are foundation therapy — frequent application reduces flare frequency.
            - Topical corticosteroids and calcineurin inhibitors for inflammation.
            - Dupilumab (IL-4 receptor antagonist) highly effective for moderate-severe AD.
            - Other biologics (JAK inhibitors, phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitors) emerging.
            - Allergen identification and avoidance counselling.
