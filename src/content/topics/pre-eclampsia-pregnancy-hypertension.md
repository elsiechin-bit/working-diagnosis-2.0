## Overview


Hypertension in pregnancy is BP ≥140/90. The disorders form a spectrum:

   **Chronic hypertension:** present before pregnancy or before 20 weeks.
   **Gestational hypertension:** new hypertension after 20 weeks *without* features of pre-eclampsia.
   **Pre-eclampsia:** new hypertension after 20 weeks *with* proteinuria *or* maternal organ dysfunction (renal, hepatic, neurological, haematological) or uteroplacental dysfunction (fetal growth restriction). Proteinuria is no longer required if other organ involvement is present.
   **Eclampsia:** seizures; **HELLP:** haemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, low platelets.

      🛡Risk & aspirin prophylaxis▾
      Assess risk at booking. Offer **low-dose aspirin from 12-16 weeks until delivery** to women with one high or two moderate risk factors.

  Calcium tooIn women with low dietary calcium intake, supplementation further reduces pre-eclampsia risk.

      🚩Warning features - same-day assessment▾
      These symptoms in a pregnant woman beyond 20 weeks warrant urgent (same-day) obstetric assessment, even with only modestly raised BP:

   Severe or persistent **headache**
   **Visual disturbance** (flashing, blurring)
   **Epigastric / right upper quadrant pain** (hepatic involvement)
   Sudden **swelling** of face, hands or feet
   **Reduced fetal movements**
   - BP ≥160/110, or any proteinuria on dip
  
  Severe hypertension is an emergencyBP ≥160/110 needs urgent treatment to prevent maternal stroke. Refer acutely.

      💊Management▾
      
   **Antihypertensives:** labetalol (first-line), nifedipine, or methyldopa are pregnancy-appropriate. **Avoid ACE inhibitors/ARBs** (fetotoxic).
   **Magnesium sulfate** for seizure prophylaxis in severe pre-eclampsia and for treatment of eclampsia.
   **Surveillance:** BP, urine protein, bloods (FBC, LFTs, renal, urate), and fetal growth/wellbeing.
   **Delivery** is the definitive treatment - timing balances maternal risk against prematurity, decided in secondary care.
   **Postnatal:** hypertension can worsen for several days after delivery; continue monitoring. Flag the lifelong elevated cardiovascular risk for future care.

   ✦**Aspirin from 12-16 weeks** is the single best preventive lever - identify candidates at booking.
   ✦**Proteinuria is not required** to diagnose pre-eclampsia if other organ involvement is present.
   ✦**No ACE/ARB** in pregnancy.
   ✦**Pre-eclampsia predicts future CVD** - it is a lifelong risk marker.
