#### Primary targets
        
          - Slow GFR decline and delay progression to ESRD.
          - Reduce proteinuria (especially albuminuria in diabetes).
          Lower blood pressure to target (typically 
          - Reduce cardiovascular risk (MI, stroke, heart failure, death).
          - Manage secondary complications (anaemia, bone disease, acidosis).

      💊RAAS blockade (ACE-I / ARB)▾
      
        #### Mechanism
        
          **ACE inhibitors (e.g. enalapril, ramipril):** Block angiotensin II formation; reduce intraglomerular pressure and proteinuria.
          **Angiotensin receptor blockers (e.g. losartan, irbesartan):** Block AT1 receptor; similar renoprotective effect to ACE-I.
        
        #### Indications
        
          - CKD with albuminuria or proteinuria (any stage).
          - Diabetic nephropathy (standard first-line).
          - Hypertension with CKD.
        
        #### Monitoring
        
          - Baseline and 1-2 weeks after initiation or dose increase: check K, creatinine.
          - Expected GFR drop of 10-30% in first weeks is acceptable; decline >30% or rise in K >5.5 may warrant discontinuation.
          - Monitor BP, albuminuria, renal function 6-12 monthly.
        
        #### Contraindications
        
          Pregnancy, bilateral renal artery stenosis, K >5.5 mmol/L, eGFR 

      🩺SGLT2 inhibitors▾
      
        #### Mechanism
        Block sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 in proximal tubule; increase urinary glucose excretion, lower blood glucose, reduce intraglomerular pressure, improve metabolic health.

        #### Agents (NZ approved)
        
          **Empagliflozin:** 10 mg daily; EMPA-KIDNEY trial showed 25% reduction in CKD progression.
          **Dapagliflozin:** 10 mg daily; similar renoprotective benefit; also reduces HF hospitalisations.
          **Canagliflozin:** 100-300 mg daily; also approved for CKD progression reduction.
        
        #### Indications
        
          - CKD with albuminuria, any stage, any aetiolog (not just diabetes).
          - Recommended for all CKD stages III-IV with proteinuria (KDIGO 2022).
          - Type 2 diabetes with CKD (regardless of albuminuria).
        
        #### Benefits
        
          - 40-50% reduction in CKD progression or ESRD risk.
          - Cardiovascular protection: reduced MI, stroke, CV death.
          - Heart failure benefit: reduced HF hospitalisations.
          - Weight loss, improved glycaemic control (in diabetes).
        
        #### Monitoring
        
          - Check volume status (risk of DKA, euglycaemic DKA if concurrent GLP-1).
          - Genitourinary infections more common; counsel on symptoms.
          Use with caution if eGFR 

      💉GLP-1 receptor agonists▾
      
        #### Mechanism
        Incretin mimetics; increase insulin secretion, reduce glucagon, delay gastric emptying, reduce appetite, lower blood glucose and weight.

        #### Renal benefits
        
          - LEADER, SUSTAIN trials: reduce albuminuria and slow CKD progression in type 2 diabetes.
          - Weight loss improves metabolic health and BP control.
        
        #### Agents
        
          **Semaglutide (Ozempic):** Once-weekly; more potent for weight loss and glycaemic control.
          **Dulaglutide (Trulicity):** Once-weekly; good cardiorenal outcomes.
          **Liraglutide (Victoza):** Daily injection; lower cost but less convenient.
          **Tirzepatide (Mounjaro):** GLP-1/GIP agonist; superior weight loss and glucose control.
        
        #### Monitoring
        
          - Nausea common at start; dose slowly.
          - Risk of euglycaemic DKA if combined with SGLT2-I (counsel on sick days).
          - Medullary thyroid cancer contraindication (avoid if personal or family history).

      🩸Antihypertensives and other agents▾
      
        #### Beta-blockers and calcium-channel blockers
        
          - Second-line agents; less renoprotective than ACE-I/ARB but needed for BP control.
          - Amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil for additional BP lowering.
        
        #### Diuretics
        
          - Use sparingly in CKD; may worsen electrolytes and glucose.
          - Reserved for fluid overload, oedema, HF.
        
        #### Finerenone (non-steroidal MRA)
        
          - FIDELITY trial: 18% reduction in CKD progression and CV events in diabetic CKD.
          - Especially useful if cannot tolerate full-dose ACE-I/ARB or as add-on.
        
        #### Phosphate binders
        
          - Start when phosphate rises (typically stage IV CKD); calcium-based or non-calcium (sevelamer, lanthanum).
        
        #### Iron supplementation
        
          - If anaemic and iron-deficient; IV iron if intolerant of oral or stage IV-V CKD.
