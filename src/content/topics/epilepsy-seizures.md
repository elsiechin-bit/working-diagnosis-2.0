## Overview


Most people referred with "a seizure" need three questions answered: was it a seizure, was it provoked, and what is the recurrence risk?

   **Mimics to exclude:** syncope (especially convulsive syncope), non-epileptic attacks, cardiac arrhythmia, hypoglycaemia.
   **Provoked (acute symptomatic) seizures** - alcohol withdrawal, hyponatraemia, hypoglycaemia, drugs, infection - are treated by removing the cause, not lifelong antiepileptics.
   **Work-up after a first unprovoked seizure:** history (eyewitness account is gold), exam, ECG, bloods/glucose, then EEG and MRI brain via first-seizure/neurology services.
   - Diagnosis is clinical; EEG supports classification and risk but a normal EEG does not exclude epilepsy.
  
  Don’t usually treat a single provoked seizureAntiepileptic drugs are generally started after a second unprovoked seizure, or after the first if recurrence risk is high (structural lesion, epileptiform EEG).

      🧩Classification▾
      Classifying the seizure guides drug choice.

  TypeFeatures
   Focal awareConsciousness retained; localised motor/sensory/autonomic/psychic symptoms
   Focal impaired awarenessAltered awareness, automatisms; often temporal lobe
   Generalised tonic-clonicLoss of consciousness, stiffening then jerking, post-ictal confusion
   AbsenceBrief vacant spells, childhood; 3 Hz spike-wave
   Myoclonic / atonicBrief jerks / sudden loss of tone

      💊Drug choice & safety▾
      Match the drug to the seizure type (detail in the Antiepileptics explainer). Aim for monotherapy at the lowest effective dose.

   **Focal:** lamotrigine or levetiracetam first-line; carbamazepine/lacosamide alternatives.
   **Generalised:** sodium valproate is most effective but **contraindicated in those able to become pregnant** unless strict conditions are met (teratogenic, neurodevelopmental harm) - use lamotrigine/levetiracetam.
   **Avoid** carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, gabapentin in generalised/absence/myoclonic seizures - they can worsen them.
   Counsel on **driving** (NZTA rules - seizure-free periods), **preconception/folate**, contraception interactions (enzyme inducers), alcohol/sleep, and SUDEP.
  
  Valproate and pregnancyValproate carries the highest teratogenic and neurodevelopmental risk of the antiepileptics. Avoid in anyone who could become pregnant unless a pregnancy-prevention programme is in place.

      🚨Status epilepticus▾
      A convulsive seizure lasting **≥5 minutes**, or repeated seizures without recovery, is status epilepticus - a medical emergency with time-critical, stepwise treatment:

   **Benzodiazepine** first (IV lorazepam, or buccal midazolam / rectal diazepam in the community); repeat once.
   **Second-line IV agent** (levetiracetam, valproate or phenytoin) if seizures persist.
   **Refractory** status → anaesthesia/ICU.
   - Throughout: ABC, glucose, look for a cause.

   ✦**Eyewitness history** distinguishes seizure from syncope better than any test.
   ✦**Normal EEG doesn’t exclude epilepsy.**
   ✦**5 minutes = status.** Don’t wait.
   ✦**Discuss driving and pregnancy** at diagnosis - both have major consequences.
