#### Stages of ALD
          
            **Steatosis (fatty liver):** Accumulation of triglycerides in hepatocytes; reversible with abstinence.
            **Alcoholic hepatitis:** Inflammation, hepatocyte necrosis; can be acute, severe, or chronic; jaundice, RUQ pain, fever.
            **Cirrhosis:** Fibrosis, architectural distortion, portal hypertension; largely irreversible; high morbidity/mortality.
          
          #### Risk factors for progression
          
            - Quantity and duration of alcohol use (>40–60 g/day in men, >20–40 g/day in women increases risk).
            - Female sex (lower body weight, different metabolism, higher oestrogen increases hepatic injury).
            - HCV or HBV coinfection.
            - Obesity and metabolic syndrome.
            - Genetic factors (ADH, ALDH polymorphisms).

        🔬Diagnosis and screening▾
        
          #### Liver function tests
          
            **AST > ALT:** Typical in ALD (reverse in viral hepatitis, NAFLD).
            **GGT elevation:** Marker of hepatic injury and enzyme induction.
            **Albumin:** Decreased in advanced disease (synthetic dysfunction).
            **INR:** Prolonged in cirrhosis (impaired clotting factor synthesis).
            **Bilirubin:** Elevated in hepatitis and cholestasis.
          
          #### Biomarkers and non-invasive assessment
          
            **FIB-4 score:** (Age × AST) / (Platelet count × √ALT); helps identify cirrhosis risk.
            **APRI:** (AST / platelet) ratio; alternative to FIB-4.
            **Transient elastography (FibroScan):** Non-invasive measure of liver stiffness; >12.5 kPa suggests cirrhosis.
            **Liver biopsy:** Gold standard for staging but invasive; reserved for diagnostic uncertainty.

        💊Management▾
        
          #### Abstinence
          
            - Only proven intervention; can reverse steatosis and hepatitis; slows cirrhosis progression.
            - Counselling, brief interventions, medications (acamprosate, naltrexone, disulfiram).
          
          #### Nutritional support
          
            - Thiamine, folate, multivitamins (malnutrition common).
            - Protein balanced (not restricted) unless hepatic encephalopathy.
          
          #### Acute alcoholic hepatitis
          
            **Corticosteroids:** Prednisolone 40 mg daily for 4 weeks if Maddrey score ≥32 or Glasgow score ≥9; improves short-term survival.
            **Pentoxifylline:** May reduce mortality in some; used when steroids contraindicated.
            **Supportive care:** Fluid resuscitation, electrolyte correction, treatment of infection, lactulose for encephalopathy.
          
          #### Cirrhosis management
          
            **Variceal bleeding:** Beta-blockers (propranolol, carvedilol) for primary and secondary prevention; endoscopic banding if bleed.
            **Ascites:** Sodium restriction, diuretics (spironolactone, furosemide); paracentesis if tense.
            **Hepatic encephalopathy:** Lactulose, rifaxomicin.
            **Liver transplant:** For decompensated cirrhosis, acute liver failure; requires sustained abstinence (usually 6 months).
