## Overview


**NZ funds core childhood vaccines, plus select vaccines for adolescents, elderly, & at-risk groups.** Many vaccines remain unfunded, requiring private payment or employer sponsorship. Knowledge of non-funded options allows GPs to counsel patients on available protection beyond the funded schedule.

        #### PHARMAC funding categories

          **Partially funded (Special Authority or subset):** Varicella (chickenpox vaccine; funded for post-exposure prophylaxis, immunocompromised, healthcare workers), zoster (shingles; funded ≥65 years with Special Authority in some DHBs), RSV vaccine (select older adults/immunocompromised)
          **Not funded (private cost):** Typhoid, yellow fever, Japanese encephalitis, rabies, tick-borne encephalitis, dengue (when approved), varicella (self-pay except noted exceptions above), herpes zoster (for 

          PHARMAC funding status changes; always verify current status
          Funding is reviewed periodically. New vaccines may be added, eligibility criteria altered. Consult current PHARMAC schedule, NZF, or HealthPathways for up-to-date funding status before counselling patients.

      ✈️Travel-related vaccines▾
      
        **Travel medicine is the primary context for private vaccine use in NZ.** Patients travelling to endemic regions should receive pre-travel counselling ≥4–6 weeks before departure to allow time for vaccination & antibody response.

        #### Typhoid vaccine
        
          **Disease epidemiology:** Salmonella typhi transmitted via contaminated water/food; endemic in South Asia, Africa, some Latin American regions. ~11 million cases/year globally; risk to travellers ~1 per 30,000 to ~1 per 5,000 depending on region & hygiene practices.
          **Vaccine options:** Inactivated injectable (Vi CPS, single dose; booster every 3 years) or oral live attenuated (Ty21a, 4 doses over 10 days; booster every 3 years). Oral preferred for some (needle anxiety, repeated dosing acceptable); injectable more convenient (single visit).
          **Efficacy:** ~70–90% depending on vaccine type & compliance
          **Indications:** Travellers to endemic South Asia (India, Bangladesh, Pakistan), Sub-Saharan Africa, parts of Latin America; especially if visiting rural areas, staying with local families, or planning extended stays (>3 weeks)
          **Cost (NZ):** ~$60–80 per dose (private providers)
          **Safety:** Mild injection-site reactions common; rarely fever/myalgia. Oral vaccine may cause GI upset (take with food, adequate water)
        
        #### Yellow fever vaccine
        
          **Disease epidemiology:** Arbovirus transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes; endemic in Sub-Saharan Africa & tropical South America. Mortality ~15% in hospitalised cases; haemorrhagic form devastating.
          **Vaccine:** Live attenuated (17DD strain); single dose provides lifelong immunity (duration debated; some recommend booster at 10 years, others consider single dose sufficient)
          **Indications:** Required entry to certain countries (≥50 countries require proof of vaccination; check country-specific requirements). Travellers to endemic regions (Sub-Saharan Africa, parts of South America, Paraguay) should be vaccinated.
          **Special populations:** Pregnancy & yellow fever vaccine use debated; generally avoid unless risk substantial. Immunocompromised NOT recommended (live vaccine). Post-vaccine adverse reactions (YEL-AVD: vaccine-strain yellow fever infection, rare; YEL-AND: viscerotropic illness, very rare) ~1 per 300,000–500,000 doses.
          **Cost (NZ):** ~$100–120 (must be administered by accredited travel clinic, documented in International Certificate of Vaccination)
          **Documentation:** Yellow fever vaccine is one of few requiring international certification (due to legal requirement for entry to certain countries). Certificate valid 10 years from vaccination date.
        
        #### Hepatitis A vaccine
        
          **Disease epidemiology:** Fecal-oral transmission; endemic in areas with poor sanitation (much of South/Southeast Asia, Africa, parts of South America). Risk to NZ travellers moderate if hygiene practices good.
          **Vaccine:** Inactivated (Havrix, Vaqta, others); 2 doses 6–12 months apart; or combined Hep A+B (3 doses). Single dose provides ~90% efficacy; second dose reinforces protection to >95%, likely durable for life.
          **Indications:** Travellers to endemic regions, especially if staying in rural areas or with local families. Also: healthcare/sewage workers, men who have sex with men, people with chronic liver disease.
          **Cost (NZ):** ~$50–80 per dose (private)
          **Note:** Many NZ-born individuals travelled to endemic countries as children & have natural immunity (serologically). Testing for anti-HAV IgG prior to vaccination reasonable if travel history suggests possible exposure; vaccination unnecessary if already immune.
        
        #### Hepatitis B vaccine (for at-risk travellers)
        
          **Indications:** Travellers with planned healthcare access in high-HBV-prevalence regions (East Asia, Pacific Islands, Sub-Saharan Africa); healthcare workers; unvaccinated persons with sexual exposure risk; needle-stick risk (eg occupational exposure in healthcare abroad).
          **NZ-specific:** Routine childhood Hep B vaccination standard since 2000s; most NZ-born 
          **Cost:** ~$50–100 per dose
        
        #### Japanese encephalitis (JE) vaccine
        
          **Disease:** Arbovirus transmitted by Culex mosquitoes; endemic in East & Southeast Asia. Mortality ~20–30%; survivors often have neurological sequelae. Risk to short-term travellers low; higher for extended stays, particularly during rainy season (wet months = mosquito breeding).
          **Vaccine options:** Inactivated (Jespect) or live attenuated (SA 333-5-3, less common). Inactivated: 2 doses 28 days apart (or accelerated 0, 7 days if last-minute).
          **Indications:** Travellers spending ≥2 weeks in endemic areas during transmission season; particularly rural/agricultural regions (mosquitoes breed in paddies). Short-term urban travel (Bangkok, Ho Chi Minh City) low risk; omit JE vaccine unless significant risk.
          **Cost (NZ):** ~$60–100 per dose (often difficult to source in NZ; may need to arrange through travel clinic or international providers)
          **Safety:** Mild local reactions common. Rare allergic reactions & acute disseminated encephalomyelitis reported (very rare).
        
        #### Rabies vaccine (pre-exposure & post-exposure)
        
          **Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP):** For travellers with occupational risk (wildlife researchers, veterinarians, animal handlers) or those travelling to endemic regions where medical care distant (rural Africa, parts of Asia). 3-dose series (days 0, 7, 21 or 28); antibodies develop over 3–4 weeks.
          **Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP):** Emergency treatment after suspected rabies exposure (animal bite/scratch, saliva contact with mucous membrane). Extremely time-sensitive: must start within hours–days of exposure (ideally within 24 hours). Includes wound care, rabies immunoglobulin (RIG; if not previously vaccinated), + rabies vaccine series (4 doses over 2 weeks). Funded in NZ if exposure confirmed/suspected; managed in hospital.
          **Indications for PrEP:** Occupational (bat researchers, animal handlers), travellers spending weeks in remote endemic areas (Africa, Asia, parts of South America) with high rabies-in-animals prevalence & limited access to PEP. Short-term urban travel typically no indication.
          **Cost (NZ, PrEP):** ~$150–250 total for 3-dose series (private vaccination)
          **Safety:** Mild local reactions; rare systemic side-effects
        
        #### Tick-borne encephalitis (TBE) vaccine
        
          **Disease:** Flavivirus endemic in Central & Eastern Europe, parts of Russia, Scandinavia. Transmitted by Ixodes tick. Neurological complications; mortality 1–20% depending on subtype & healthcare access.
          **Vaccine:** Inactivated (Encepur, FSME-Immun); 3 doses over 5–12 months (schedule varies by formulation); booster every 3–5 years.
          **Indications:** Travellers to endemic forest regions of Central/Eastern Europe (hiking, camping); especially late spring–early autumn (tick season). TBE vaccination rarely indicated for NZ travellers (low travel volume to endemic regions); typically not stocked by NZ pharmacies; requires special order.
          **Cost & access:** Limited availability in NZ; ~$80–100 per dose if obtainable
        
        #### Dengue vaccine (future consideration)
        
          **Status in NZ:** Not yet approved for general use (as of 2024). Dengvaxia available in endemic regions; approved by WHO for use in endemic countries. Controversy around potential antibody-dependent enhancement in seronegative individuals.
          **Future role:** If approved for NZ travel use, indication likely travellers to endemic SE Asia, Pacific Islands, Americas with planned extended stays or high exposure risk (occupational, frequent travel).
          **Current traveller counsel:** Recommend mosquito avoidance (bed net, insect repellent, protective clothing) rather than vaccine; inform of dengue risk if endemic region visited.

      💼Occupational & at-risk group vaccines▾
      
        #### Healthcare workers (HCWs)
        
          **Influenza vaccine:** Annually recommended; funded for HCWs in some DHB employment contexts (check with occupational health). Out-of-pocket cost ~$30–50 if not funded. Reduces influenza illness & patient-to-HCW transmission.
          **Hepatitis B:** Routine vaccination for all HCWs; NZ-born 
          **Varicella:** Vaccination (or serology confirmation of immunity) required for HCWs; non-immune HCWs must receive 2 doses 4 weeks apart. Funded as Special Authority in many settings; check with occupational health.
          **Meningococcal ACWY & B:** Not routinely funded for HCWs; considered if high exposure risk (microbiology lab) or during outbreaks. Cost ~$100–120 per dose.
          **Pertussis booster (Tdap):** Recommended every 10 years or as needed for high exposure risk (neonatal/paediatric units); cost ~$50–80.
        
        #### Animal handlers & veterinary professionals
        
          **Rabies (PrEP):** Pre-exposure prophylaxis recommended for those with frequent animal exposure (wildlife researchers, veterinarians, zoo staff, dog catchers). 3-dose series; cost ~$200–250. Note: PEP still needed post-exposure even if PrEP previously given (PEP protocol differs).
          **Leptospirosis:** Not vaccine-preventable; counsel on occupational hygiene, wound care, antibiotic prophylaxis after exposure.
          **Q fever (Coxiella burnetti):** Vaccine available internationally for occupational exposure (abattoir workers, farmers); not approved/available in NZ. Counsel on occupational hygiene & early treatment of febrile illness.
        
        #### Laboratory & research staff
        
          **Vaccinia virus exposure risk (smallpox lab):** Vaccinia vaccine available internationally for laboratory staff; not relevant to routine NZ practice (no active smallpox labs in NZ). High-containment research facilities manage exposure risk via occupational health protocols.
          **Hepatitis A & B:** Recommended for staff handling blood/body fluids or working in virology labs. Funded or employer-covered.
          **Influenza, pertussis, varicella:** Standard HCW recommendations apply.
        
        #### Men who have sex with men (MSM)
        
          **Hepatitis A & B:** Vaccinations recommended if non-immune; herd exposure risk. Hepatitis A vaccine funded (check current status); Hep B cost ~$50–100 if not covered.
          **Human papillomavirus (HPV):** Gardasil 9 recommended up to age 45 years (some guidelines); benefits with age debated. Cost ~$150–200 per dose (3 doses). Funded via routine childhood schedule for 11-year-olds; cost for older individuals.
          **Meningococcal B & ACWY:** Considerations for MSM (increased invasive meningococcal disease risk); funding variable (may be Special Authority). Cost ~$100–120 per dose.
          **Mpox (monkeypox):** Not vaccine-funded in NZ; mpox vaccinations (Jynneos) increasingly available through sexual health clinics for high-risk MSM during outbreaks or if significant contact risk. Cost variable; ~$100–200 if available.
        
        #### Immunocompromised individuals
        
          **General principle:** Live vaccines (MMR, varicella, rotavirus, oral typhoid) contraindicated; inactivated vaccines recommended with consideration of timing relative to immunosuppressive therapy (eg before chemotherapy, or ≥3 months post-transplant depending on type).
          **Influenza (annual), pneumococcal (PCV13 + PPSV23), Hepatitis A & B, meningococcal ACWY & B, Tdap:** Recommended. Funding status varies (some funded as Special Authority, others private). Consult with immunising provider or immunology specialist for tailored schedule.
          **Timing & response:** Vaccinations ideally before immunosuppression begins (eg pre-transplant). Post-transplant vaccination timing depends on degree of immunosuppression; provider-specific protocols vary widely.

      🔬Varicella (chickenpox) & herpes zoster (shingles) vaccines▾
      
        #### Varicella vaccine (chickenpox prevention)
        
          **Funding status:** Not in routine childhood schedule (NZ does not vaccinate all children against chickenpox). Funded post-exposure (within 3 days of exposure) or for healthcare workers; otherwise cost ~$50–80 per dose (2 doses 4 weeks apart).
          **Rationale for non-funding:** Chickenpox generally mild in childhood; complications rare. Vaccination redirects risk to older adolescents/adults (where complications higher). NZ strategy: accept chickenpox in childhood (cost of illness manageable), vaccinate at-risk groups (HCWs, seronegative adolescents, immunocompromised, pregnant planning pregnancy).
          **Indications for private vaccination:** Seronegative adolescents/young adults planning pregnancy (reduces congenital varicella risk), occupational exposure risk (HCW, childcare), immunocompromised not previously infected (if live vaccine safe), planned international travel to countries with higher adult chickenpox rates.
          **Safety:** Live attenuated vaccine (Varivax); mild injection-site reactions, rarely (1 per 30,000 doses) vaccine-strain VPD (varicella-like rash post-vaccination)

      💉Other non-funded vaccines: RSV, pertussis boosters, maternal vaccines▾
      
        #### RSV (respiratory syncytial virus) vaccine
        
          **Status:** Arexvy (GSK RSV vaccine, inactivated subunit) approved internationally for older adults (≥60 years). NZ approval & funding status in progress (not yet widely available 2024).
          **Indications:** Older adults at high risk of severe RSV (age ≥75, chronic cardiopulmonary disease, immunocompromised). Single-dose vaccine; efficacy ~83% in preventing RSV-associated lower respiratory tract disease in older adults.
          **When available in NZ:** Likely cost ~$200–300 per dose (private initially, pending PHARMAC funding review).
        
        #### Pertussis booster (Tdap) for selected groups
        
          **Funding:** Funded for pregnancy (per-pregnancy basis in NZ, aiming to boost neonatal protection), close contacts 
          **Indications for private Tdap:** Adults 10+ years from last Td booster with occupational pertussis risk (healthcare, childcare, education), or close contacts of vulnerable infants. Cost ~$50–80.
          **Rationale:** Pertussis immunity wanes; periodic boosters maintain population-level protection (prevent transmission to high-risk groups like neonates).
        
        #### Maternal vaccines (influenza, Tdap, RSV future)
        
          **Influenza in pregnancy:** Funded annually; reduces maternal illness & protects neonate via passive antibodies (maternal IgG).
          **Tdap in pregnancy:** Funded per-pregnancy basis in NZ (booster in pregnancy; timing varies by last Td & pertussis circulation). Boosts neonatal pertussis protection pre-vaccination.
          **RSV vaccine in pregnancy (future):** Arexvy being studied for maternal vaccination (transfer of maternal RSV antibodies to protect neonate 
          **Group B Streptococcus (GBS) vaccine:** Not yet approved; in clinical trials for maternal vaccination (early 3rd trimester) to reduce neonatal GBS infection. May become available within 2–3 years; when approved, likely cost initially ~$100–200.

      💡Clinical pearls & access/cost considerations▾
      
        #### Pearls
        
          **Proactive travel medicine counselling saves last-minute rushed decisions.** Patients should be advised ≥4–6 weeks before travel to allow time for vaccination & antibody response. Early consultation prevents vaccine unavailability, inadequate immunity window, or omitted protection.
          **Verify current PHARMAC funding before counselling.** Funding changes; updated NZF & HealthPathways reflect current status. Outdated info undermines patient confidence.
          **Cost is significant barrier; discuss proactively.** $200–500 for travel vaccine series substantial for many families. Explore employer sponsorship (occupational), check for practice-level discounts, or refer to travel clinics (sometimes lower-cost group pricing).
          **Combination vaccines reduce number of visits & needles.** Hep A+B, Tdap combinations available; encourage consolidated vaccination for convenience & compliance.
          **Live vaccine spacing matters for private vaccines too.** If patient receiving live vaccine (varicella, MMR, rotavirus if applicable), ensure ≥28 days between live vaccines or simultaneous administration; do not stagger.
          **Document vaccination clearly.** Record in patient's notes & provide documentation to patient (especially travel vaccines with international certificate requirement, yellow fever vaccine).
        
        #### Access & cost management
        
          **Travel clinics vs GP practice:** Specialist travel clinics often have vaccines in stock; may charge consult fee (~$40–60) + vaccine cost. GP practice may need to special-order; potentially cheaper if practice-negotiated discounts with suppliers. Discuss both options with patient.
          **Special order logistics:** Some vaccines (Japanese encephalitis, tick-borne encephalitis, certain Hep A/B combinations) not commonly stocked in NZ. GP practices must order ahead; allow 1–2 weeks for arrival. Plan accordingly in travel timeline.
          **Post-travel serology (for hepatitis vaccines):** Optional; if vaccinated, post-vaccination serology confirms protective antibody levels. Cost ~$50 per test (not routine; only if specific indication). Hep B post-vaccination serology recommended for HCWs; Hep A serology usually unnecessary (2-dose series reliably protective).
          **Employer sponsorship (occupational vaccines):** Encourage patient to check with occupational health department or employer. Many cover HCW vaccinations (influenza, Hep B, varicella, Tdap, meningococcal); saves patient cost.
        
        #### Pitfalls
        
          **Recommending yellow fever vaccine without verifying entry requirements.** Not all African/South American countries require it. Check country-specific legal requirements (WHO Yellow Book or Smartraveller); do not vaccinate unnecessarily.
          **Underselling cost implications of travel vaccines.** Patients surprised at $300–500 total for typhoid, yellow fever, Hep A/B, JE combination. Discuss upfront; allows patient to budget & prioritise.
          **Forgetting post-travel illness risk in patients who skipped vaccines.** Patient deferred yellow fever vaccine, then develops fever/jaundice post-travel. Consider yellow fever (laboratory confirmation feasible if caught early). Document decision & counsel on symptom recognition.
          **Not integrating private vaccines with funded schedule.** Patient receiving travel vaccines & due for booster (eg MMR catch-up). Coordinate timing & spacing; avoid over-vaccinating or missing scheduled boosters.
          **Assuming all HCWs vaccinated against varicella/Hep B.** Some older HCWs, international staff, or those with incomplete documentation not immune. Verify & vaccinate as needed; don't assume based on birth cohort.
