## Overview


FeatureVenousArterial
   SiteGaiter area (medial malleolus)Toes, heel, pressure points, lateral
   Edge/baseShallow, irregular, granulating, exudativePunched-out, deep, pale/necrotic
   PainLess; better on elevationSevere; worse on elevation, better hanging down
   Surrounding skinHaemosiderin, varicose eczema, lipodermatosclerosis, oedemaCool, shiny, hairless, weak pulses
  
  Mixed disease is common. Diabetic/neuropathic ulcers occur at pressure points with neuropathy and need their own foot-care pathway.

      📏Assessment▾
      
   **Measure ABPI before any compression.** ABPI 0.8-1.3 supports safe compression; <0.8 suggests arterial disease (refer); >1.3 suggests calcification (diabetes) and an unreliable reading.
   **Examine** pulses, sensation, the ulcer characteristics, and surrounding skin.
   **Screen for contributors:** diabetes, anaemia, nutrition, malignancy (a non-healing or atypical ulcer needs biopsy - consider Marjolin’s).
   - Swab only if clinically infected - all chronic ulcers are colonised.
  
  Compression on an ischaemic leg can cause necrosisNever apply compression bandaging until arterial supply is confirmed adequate by ABPI/vascular assessment.

      🩹Management▾
      
   **Venous ulcers:** graded **compression therapy** is the mainstay (with adequate arterial supply); elevation, exercise, simple non-adherent dressings, emollients and treatment of varicose eczema.
   **Endovenous/surgical treatment of superficial venous reflux** speeds healing and reduces recurrence - refer.
   **After healing, compression hosiery** prevents recurrence (lifelong for many).
   **Arterial ulcers:** vascular referral for revascularisation; do not compress.
   - Treat clinical infection; address pain, nutrition and mobility.

   ✦**ABPI before compression** - every time.
   ✦**Compression prevents recurrence,** not just heals - keep hosiery going.
   ✦**Atypical/non-healing ulcer** → biopsy (malignancy).
   ✦**Don’t swab routinely** - treat the patient, not the colonised wound.
