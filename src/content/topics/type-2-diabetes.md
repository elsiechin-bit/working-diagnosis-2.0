#### Diagnostic criteria (any one sufficient, must confirm if asymptomatic)
      
        - HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (6.5%)
        - Fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L
        - 2-hour glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L on 75 g OGTT
        - Random glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L with classic symptoms
      
      #### Pre-diabetes
      
        - HbA1c 41–47 mmol/mol; or FPG 6.1–6.9 mmol/L; or IGT on OGTT
        - ~5–10% progress to T2DM per year; lifestyle intervention reduces progression by 50–58%
        - Refer to diabetes prevention programme; recheck HbA1c annually
      
      #### Initial assessment
      
        - HbA1c, FPG, lipids, renal function (eGFR + creatinine), urine ACR, LFTs
        - Blood pressure, BMI, waist circumference
        - Retinal photograph (baseline), foot assessment (monofilament, pulses, ABI if vascular concern)
        - CV risk calculation (PREDICT or NZ CVD risk tool)
        - Consider GAD antibodies / C-peptide if diagnostic uncertainty (LADA vs T2DM)

    💊Pharmacotherapy▾
    
      #### Starting treatment
      Start metformin at diagnosis alongside lifestyle for most patients. In high CV/renal risk, consider early SGLT2i or GLP-1 regardless of HbA1c.

        #### Metformin
          **First-line** for most T2DM
          HbA1c reduction: ~10–15 mmol/mol
          Start 500 mg with meals; titrate to 1–2 g BD
          Withhold if eGFR <30; reduce dose eGFR 30–45
          GI side effects — take with food; modified-release better tolerated
        
        #### SGLT2 inhibitors
          **CV + renal benefit** independent of glucose
          Empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin
          Reduce HF hospitalisation, CKD progression, CV death
          Weight neutral to weight-reducing; low hypo risk
          Avoid if eGFR <20; UTI/DKA risk; hold perioperatively
        
        #### GLP-1 receptor agonists
          **CV + weight benefit** — preferred if BMI >30 or CV disease
          Semaglutide (SC weekly or oral), dulaglutide, liraglutide
          HbA1c reduction: ~15–20 mmol/mol; weight loss 3–15 kg
          GI side effects common initially; pancreatitis (rare)
          Low hypo risk; avoid in MEN2, medullary thyroid cancer history
        
        #### Other agents
          **DPP-4 inhibitors** (sitagliptin, alogliptin) — modest HbA1c reduction; weight neutral; safe in CKD
          **Sulfonylureas** (gliclazide MR) — cheap and effective; hypoglycaemia risk; weight gain
          **Pioglitazone** — useful in fatty liver; fluid retention, fracture risk, bladder cancer risk
          **Insulin** — when HbA1c persistently above target; start basal (insulin glargine/detemir)

      High CV or renal risk — use SGLT2i and/or GLP-1 earlySGLT2i and GLP-1 agonists with proven CV/renal benefit (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide) should be used **irrespective of baseline HbA1c** in patients with established CVD, CKD (eGFR <60 or ACR >3), or high CV risk. These are outcomes drugs, not just glucose drugs.

      #### BP and lipid targets
      
        **BP target:** <130/80 mmHg — ACEi or ARB first-line (renal protection)
        **Lipids:** high-intensity statin for all with T2DM + CVD or >10% CV risk; LDL target <1.8 mmol/L in high-risk

    🍽️Lifestyle, sick-day rules & hypoglycaemia▾
    
      #### Lifestyle
      
        - Dietary pattern: Mediterranean, low-carbohydrate or DASH — all effective; choose by preference and sustainability
        - Physical activity: ≥150 min moderate aerobic + resistance training 2–3×/week
        - Weight loss: 5–15% reduces HbA1c, BP, lipids; remission possible with >15% loss
        - Smoking cessation: worsens insulin resistance and CV risk
        - Alcohol: limit to <2 standard drinks/day; hypoglycaemia risk with insulin/SU
      
      #### Sick-day rules (on metformin or SGLT2i)
      
        **SICK-DAY rule:** withhold metformin and SGLT2i during acute illness, dehydration, contrast studies, or perioperatively
        - SGLT2i: hold ≥3 days before surgery; resume when eating and drinking normally
        - Monitor blood glucose more frequently when unwell
      
      #### Hypoglycaemia management
      
        - Mild-moderate: 15 g fast-acting carbohydrate (glucose tablets, regular soft drink, juice); recheck in 15 min; repeat if still <4 mmol/L
        - Severe (unable to self-treat): glucagon 1 mg IM or SC; IV glucose 50% 25 mL if IV access
        - Review insulin/SU dose after any severe episode
