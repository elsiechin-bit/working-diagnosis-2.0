## Overview


PTSD follows exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury or sexual violence, with four symptom clusters persisting >1 month and causing impairment:

   **Intrusions** - flashbacks, nightmares, distressing memories
   **Avoidance** - of reminders, thoughts, places, people
   **Negative alterations in cognition/mood** - guilt, detachment, numbing
   **Hyperarousal** - hypervigilance, startle, sleep disturbance, irritability
  
  It is frequently **missed** because patients present with insomnia, pain, substance use or depression rather than naming trauma. Complex PTSD adds difficulties with emotional regulation, self-concept and relationships after prolonged/repeated trauma.

      🧠PTSD - management▾
      
   **First-line is trauma-focused psychological therapy:** trauma-focused CBT or EMDR (eye movement desensitisation and reprocessing).
   **Medication** (SSRI/SNRI - sertraline, paroxetine, venlafaxine) where therapy is unavailable, declined, or for comorbid depression - second-line to therapy.
   **Prazosin** can help trauma nightmares in some.
   **Avoid benzodiazepines** - ineffective for core PTSD and risk dependence.
   - Watch-and-wait with support is appropriate in the first month (most acute stress reactions resolve); don’t medicalise normal early distress.
  
  Ask, gentlyNormalising a trauma enquiry ("difficult or frightening experiences can affect sleep and mood - has anything like that happened to you?") surfaces a diagnosis that patients rarely volunteer.

      🔁OCD - recognition & management▾
      OCD pairs **obsessions** (recurrent, intrusive, unwanted thoughts/images/urges causing anxiety) with **compulsions** (repetitive behaviours or mental acts performed to neutralise the anxiety). Common themes: contamination/washing, checking, symmetry/ordering, taboo intrusive thoughts. Insight is usually retained - patients know it is excessive, which fuels shame and concealment.

   **First-line therapy: ERP** (exposure and response prevention) - a specific form of CBT, the most effective treatment.
   **Medication: SSRIs**, often at **higher doses and longer trials** (10-12 weeks) than for depression; clomipramine is an effective alternative.
   **Combination** (ERP + SSRI) for moderate-severe disease; specialist referral if refractory.
   - Screen for comorbid depression and suicidality.

   ✦**PTSD hides** behind insomnia, pain and substance use - ask about trauma.
   ✦**Therapy is first-line** for both - medication supports, it doesn’t replace.
   ✦**OCD needs higher SSRI doses** and longer trials than depression.
   ✦**No benzodiazepines** for core PTSD.
