## Overview

Gout management divides into three phases: acute flare, prophylaxis during urate-lowering therapy (ULT) initiation, and long-term urate-lowering.

The major guidelines (BPAC NZ, RACGP Australia, BSR UK, ACR/EULAR) agree on broad principles but diverge on details: HLA-B*5801 screening thresholds, specific drug choices, and target urate levels.

## Acute Flare Treatment

| Decision | NZ (BPAC) | AU (RACGP) | UK (BSR) | International (ACR/EULAR) |
|----------|-----------|-----------|---------|---------------------------|
| First-line | NSAID or colchicine | NSAID or colchicine | NSAID | NSAID or colchicine |
| Colchicine dosing | Low-dose (1 mg/0.5 mg) | Low-dose (1 mg/0.5 mg) | Low-dose | Low-dose |
| Prednisone option | Yes, if NSAID/colchicine contraindicated | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Start ULT during flare? | No | No | No | No |

**Consensus:** All guidelines favour NSAIDs or low-dose colchicine, started early.

## Urate-Lowering Therapy

| Decision | NZ | AU | UK | International |
|----------|----|----|----|----|
| First-line agent | Allopurinol | Allopurinol | Allopurinol | Allopurinol |
| Starting dose | 50-100 mg daily | 50-100 mg daily | 50 mg daily | 50-100 mg daily |
| Target serum urate | < 0.36 mmol/L | < 0.36 mmol/L | < 0.30 mmol/L | < 0.36 mmol/L (< 0.30 if tophi) |
| HLA-B*5801 screening | Han Chinese, Korean, Thai descent | Yes, Han Chinese, Korean, Thai | No routine recommendation | No routine recommendation |

**Key difference:** UK targets lower urate (< 0.30 mmol/L). NZ recommends HLA-B*5801 screening for at-risk ethnicities; international guidelines are silent on this.

## Flare Prophylaxis

| Duration | NZ | AU | UK | International |
|----------|----|----|----|----|
| Prophylaxis duration | Minimum 6 months after starting ULT | 3-6 months | 2 weeks before to 3 weeks after ULT start | 2 weeks before to 3-6 months after |
| Agent | Colchicine or NSAID | Colchicine or NSAID | Low-dose colchicine | Colchicine or NSAID |

**Consensus:** All require prophylaxis when initiating ULT to prevent flare rebound.

## Equity and NZ-Specific Issues

<div class="callout info">
**Māori and Pasifika health:** Gout prevalence in Māori is 6-8% in men (approximately 15-fold higher than European New Zealanders). This is substantially genetic, driven by loss-of-function variants in urate transporters (ABCG2) more common in these populations. Clinical framing matters: this is not primarily diet or lifestyle, and stigmatising language compounds harm.
</div>
