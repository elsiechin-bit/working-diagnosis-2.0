#### Definitions
        
          **Infertility:** Failure to conceive after 12 months of unprotected intercourse (or 6 months if woman >35 years)
          **Subfertility:** Reduced fertility (may conceive eventually without intervention, but may take longer)
          **Primary infertility:** Never achieved conception
          **Secondary infertility:** Inability to conceive after previous pregnancy (any outcome)

        #### Epidemiology
        
          - Affects 1 in 7 couples globally; varies by age, socioeconomic status, region
          - Female factor: 30-40% (ovulation disorders, tubal pathology, endometriosis, uterine/cervical factors)
          - Male factor: 30-40% (low sperm count, motility, morphology)
          - Combined: 15-20%
          - Unexplained: 10-20%

        #### Initial assessment in primary care
        
          **Both partners:** General health, sexual history, medical/surgical history, medication, STI status
          **Female:** Menstrual history (regularity, cycle length, dysmenorrhoea), contraceptive history, obstetric history, signs of PCOS or endometriosis
          **Male:** Erectile/ejaculatory function, past testicular torsion, mumps orchitis, sexual dysfunction, hot environment exposure (occupational), smoking, substance use
          **Duration of unprotected intercourse:** Confirm 12 months (6 if woman >35)
          **Lifestyle factors:** BMI, smoking, alcohol, stress, frequency of intercourse (ideally every 2-3 days during fertile window)

        #### Physical examination
        
          **Female:** BMI, signs of PCOS (hirsutism, acanthosis nigricans), thyroid, breast (galactorrhoea), pelvic exam if indicated
          **Male:** Testicular size/consistency (normal 15-25 mL), epididymis, varicocele assessment, penis/urethra exam

      🧬Female subfertility: causes and investigations▾
      
        #### Ovulation disorders (30-40% of female subfertility)
        
          **PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome):** Most common; irregular cycles, anovulation, hyperandrogenism
          **Hypothalamic amenorrhoea:** Low BMI, stress, excessive exercise; low GnRH/FSH
          **Hyperprolactinaemia:** Galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea; pituitary adenoma, medications
          **Primary ovarian insufficiency (POI):** Early menopause 
          **Hypothyroidism:** Anovulation, hyperprolactinaemia

        #### Tubal and peritoneal factors (20-30%)
        
          **Tubal blockage or damage:** STI (chlamydia, gonorrhoea), pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), post-surgical adhesions, endometriosis
          **Endometriosis:** 5-10% of women; 30-50% of women with endometriosis subfertile; mechanical and inflammatory factors

        #### Uterine and cervical factors (5-10%)
        
          **Uterine anomalies:** Septate uterus, unicornuate uterus, fibroids, asherman syndrome (intrauterine adhesions)
          **Cervical factors:** Cervicitis, cervical stenosis, hostile cervical mucus (rare in modern practice)

      🧬Male subfertility: causes and investigations▾
      
        #### Causes of male subfertility
        
          **Pre-testicular (10%):** Hypogonadism, hormonal disorders, erectile/ejaculatory dysfunction
          **Testicular (75%):** Idiopathic oligospermia, varicocele, testicular torsion history, cryptorchidism, infections (mumps, STI), radiation, chemotherapy, heat exposure
          **Post-testicular (15%):** Vasectomy, ejaculatory duct obstruction, retrograde ejaculation, absence of vas deferens (CFTR mutations), infections

        #### Semen analysis
        
          **Indication:** All couples with subfertility (mandatory in primary care workup)
          **Specimen:** Abstinence 2-7 days; laboratory collection preferred
          **WHO 2021 lower reference limits (5th percentile):**
          - Volume: >1.4 mL
          Sperm concentration: >16 million/mL (oligozoospermia if 
          - Total sperm count: >39 million/ejaculate
          - Motility: >42% total motility, >30% progressive motility
          - Morphology: >4% normal forms (strict criteria)
          **Repeat after 2-3 months if abnormal** (high variability)

        #### Other male investigations
        
          **Testosterone, FSH, LH:** If oligozoospermia or clinical hypogonadism
          **Testicular ultrasound:** If low testosterone, small testes, or abnormal exam
          **Genetic testing:** If azoospermia (CFTR, Y chromosome microdeletion)

      💊Management and treatment options▾
      
        #### General measures (all couples)
        
          **Lifestyle:** BMI 18.5-25 (both partners); stop smoking; limit alcohol; regular intercourse (every 2-3 days)
          **Folic acid:** Women 400-800 mcg daily (or 5 mg if PCOS); men if oligozoospermia
          **Stress reduction:** Counselling, support groups
          **Sexual dysfunction:** Treat erectile dysfunction or ejaculatory issues

        #### Female factor treatment
        
          **PCOS:** Weight loss (if overweight); metformin; clomiphene citrate or letrozole for ovulation induction; referral for ART if medical ovulation induction fails
          **Anovulation (hypothyroidism, hyperprolactinaemia):** Treat underlying cause (levothyroxine, dopamine agonist)
          **Endometriosis:** Medical therapy (hormonal contraceptives, progestins, GnRH agonists) may improve fertility; surgery for severe disease
          **Tubal damage:** IVF if blocked tubes; may need tubal surgery if minimal damage

        #### Male factor treatment
        
          **Lifestyle modification:** Avoid heat exposure, smoking, alcohol excess
          **Varicocele repair:** If symptomatic or infertile with abnormal semen
          **Hormonal therapy:** Testosterone replacement if hypogonadal (may paradoxically reduce fertility acutely)
          **Antibiotics:** If infection identified
          **Assisted reproductive techniques:** ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection) for severe oligozoospermia; sperm retrieval procedures for azoospermia

        #### Assisted reproductive technology (ART)
        
          **Intrauterine insemination (IUI):** Washed sperm placed in uterus; less invasive than IVF; success rate 10-20% per cycle
          **In vitro fertilization (IVF):** Egg retrieval, fertilization in lab, embryo transfer; success rate 30-50% per cycle (varies by age and cause)
          **Intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI):** Single sperm injected into egg; for severe male factor
          **Preimplantation genetic testing (PGT):** For genetic disorders, recurrent pregnancy loss
          **Donor gametes/surrogacy:** For azoospermia, POI, or previous failed ART attempts

        #### Prognosis and referral to secondary care
        
          **Refer to fertility clinic if:** After 12 months (6 if woman >35) of unprotected intercourse and basic investigations done
          **Expected outcomes:** 80-90% of couples conceive within 2 years without treatment; ART improves outcomes significantly
          **Age is critical:** Female fertility declines sharply after age 35; egg quality deteriorates with age
