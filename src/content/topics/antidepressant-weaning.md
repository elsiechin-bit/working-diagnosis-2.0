        ### What to expect
        Mild withdrawal effects - dizziness, flu-like feelings, "brain zaps", irritability, vivid dreams or broken sleep - can begin within a few days of each reduction and usually ease over 1-2 weeks as your body adjusts.

        ### When to hold or step back
        If symptoms are troublesome or have not settled, stay on your current dose for longer, or go back to the previous dose until you feel steady - then reduce more slowly. There is no rush; the schedule can always be stretched out.

        ### When to talk to your doctor
        Get in touch if withdrawal is severe or not improving after about two weeks, or if the original problem returns - persistent low mood, loss of interest, marked anxiety or difficulty coping. **Seek help urgently if you have any thoughts of harming yourself.**

      How this works & safety notes hyperbolic tapering

          **Hyperbolic tapering** reduces the dose by a proportion of the *current* dose each step, so absolute reductions shrink as you go down - matching the steep dose-receptor curve at low doses.
          **Distinguish withdrawal from relapse:** withdrawal starts within days of a reduction and settles; relapse is later and resembles the original illness. If withdrawal appears, hold or go back a step and slow down.
          **Small final doses** often need a **liquid formulation** or tapering strips - tablet splitting is rarely accurate below a few milligrams.
          **Short half-life agents** (paroxetine, venlafaxine) cause more withdrawal - taper slowest. **Fluoxetine**'s long half-life makes it more forgiving and sometimes useful as a switch before stopping.
          **Never stop abruptly** after regular use beyond a few weeks. Review at each step and individualise the pace.
