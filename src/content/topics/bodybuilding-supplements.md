#### Creatine monohydrateLegal OTC
              
                Evidence  The most studied, most effective legal ergogenic aid. Improves strength, power and lean mass with resistance training.
                SafetyExcellent long-term safety in healthy people. Does **not** damage healthy kidneys; can transiently raise creatinine (a benign lab effect, not injury). Mild water-weight gain.
                NoteNo need for "loading" or expensive forms - monohydrate is the standard.

              #### Protein powder (whey, casein, plant)Legal OTC
              
                Evidence  A convenient way to hit a total daily protein target (~1.6 g/kg supports muscle gain). Food works just as well.
                SafetySafe in healthy people. It is food, not magic - whole-diet protein is equivalent.

              #### CaffeineLegal OTC
              
                Evidence  Reliable improvement in endurance, power and perceived exertion.
                SafetyWatch total daily dose from stacked pre-workouts and energy drinks: anxiety, palpitations, insomnia, raised BP. High-dose pure caffeine powder has caused deaths.

              #### Beta-alanineLegal OTC
              
                Evidence  Modest benefit for high-intensity efforts of 1-4 minutes.
                SafetyHarmless tingling (paraesthesia) is the classic, benign effect.

          🤷Legal, but little or no evidencemostly wasted money▾

              #### BCAAs & glutamineLegal OTC
              
                Evidence  Redundant if total protein intake is adequate. Glutamine offers no benefit in healthy trainees.
                VerdictHarmless but unnecessary - the money is better spent on food.

              #### "Testosterone boosters" (tribulus, D-aspartic acid, ZMA)Legal OTC
              
                Evidence  Do not meaningfully raise testosterone or build muscle.
                CatchA recognised vehicle for **adulteration** - some have been found spiked with actual steroids or unapproved drugs.

              #### Fat-burners / "thermogenics"Legal varies
              
                Evidence  Trivial effect beyond the caffeine they contain.
                CatchOften stacked stimulants; some contain banned or undeclared compounds. Read the label sceptically.

          ⚠️Grey-market & adulteratedunregulated, often illegal to sell▾

              #### SARMs (e.g. ostarine, ligandrol, RAD-140)Not approved · illegal to sell for human use
              
                ClaimMarketed as "selective" muscle-building with fewer steroid side effects, often sold as "research chemicals".
                RealityNot approved medicines anywhere for this use. Cause **testosterone suppression**, and are linked to **drug-induced liver injury** and cardiovascular effects. Products frequently mislabelled or contaminated.
                StatusBanned in sport (WADA); selling them as supplements is unlawful in NZ.

              #### Prohormones / designer steroidsOften controlled
              
                WhatPrecursors that convert to anabolic steroids in the body - effectively steroids in disguise.
                HarmsSame risks as anabolic steroids plus a high rate of **hepatotoxicity**; many are controlled drugs.

              #### Banned pre-workout stimulants (DMAA, DMHA)Banned
              
                WhatAmphetamine-like stimulants that have appeared in pre-workouts.
                HarmsSevere hypertension, cerebral haemorrhage, cardiac events and death. Withdrawn/banned by regulators.

            Contamination is the rule, not the exceptionIndependent testing repeatedly finds supplements - especially "test boosters", fat-burners and pre-workouts - spiked with undeclared steroids, stimulants or unapproved drugs. An athlete can fail a doping test, or a patient present with toxicity, from a product they believed was "natural".

          ⛔Prescription-only & illicit drugsimage- and performance-enhancing drugs▾

              #### Anabolic-androgenic steroids (AAS)Class C controlled drug (NZ)
              
                UseInjectable or oral testosterone and derivatives, usually in "cycles" and "stacks". Prescription only; illicit use is widespread and under-disclosed.
                Harms**Cardiac:** LV hypertrophy, cardiomyopathy, premature coronary disease. **Endocrine:** testicular atrophy, infertility, gynaecomastia, suppressed natural testosterone. **Hepatic** (oral 17-alkylated), **dyslipidaemia**, acne, **mood/aggression** and dependence. Injecting risks: abscess, BBV transmission.
                ClinicalDon't just say stop - many need support for post-cycle hypogonadism, mental health and cardiovascular risk. Offer monitoring and safe-injecting/needle-exchange info.

              #### ClenbuterolNot approved for humans (NZ)
              
                UseA beta-agonist used illicitly for fat loss.
                HarmsTachycardia, arrhythmia, tremor, hypokalaemia, chest pain; overdose can be life-threatening.

              #### Growth hormone & insulinPrescription medicines
              
                UseUsed illicitly for muscle/leanness; insulin sometimes combined with GH.
                HarmsGH: acromegalic changes, glucose intolerance, fluid retention, carpal tunnel. **Insulin misuse can cause fatal hypoglycaemia** - a genuine emergency.

              #### 2,4-Dinitrophenol (DNP)Illegal for human consumption
              
                UseAn industrial chemical sold online for extreme fat loss.
                Harms**Potentially fatal.** Uncouples oxidative phosphorylation causing uncontrolled hyperthermia; there is no antidote and deaths are well documented. Treat any suspected ingestion as a medical emergency.

        In the consult

        ## How to handle it clinically
        Ask without judgment"A lot of people training hard use supplements or other things to help - is there anything you're taking I should know about?" People disclose AAS, SARMs and stimulants far more readily when the question is routine and non-moralising. Disclosure is the gateway to monitoring and harm reduction.

        Think of it as a causeConsider undisclosed image-enhancing drug use behind: new acne with mood change in a young man, gynaecomastia, unexplained LV hypertrophy or cardiomyopathy, deranged LFTs, very low HDL, infertility/low sperm count with suppressed LH/FSH, or hypoglycaemia in a gym-goer.

        EmergenciesSuspected **DNP** ingestion (hyperthermia, agitation, tachycardia), **insulin**-induced hypoglycaemia, and stimulant cardiotoxicity (DMAA/clenbuterol) are time-critical - escalate and contact the National Poisons Centre (0800 POISON).

        Harm reduction, not just abstinenceFor those who will continue: offer cardiovascular and metabolic monitoring, blood-borne virus screening and vaccination, needle-exchange access, mental-health support, and a realistic plan for post-cycle recovery. Meeting people where they are keeps them engaged.
