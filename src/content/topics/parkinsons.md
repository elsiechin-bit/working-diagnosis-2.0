        ## What is Parkinson's?
        Parkinson's disease is a long-term condition that affects the nervous system. It develops when certain nerve cells in the brain gradually decline - and because those nerves connect to the whole body, Parkinson's can affect far more than movement.

            🧪
            ### A messenger runs low
            Dopamine, a brain chemical, helps movement stay smooth. In Parkinson's, cells that make it slowly fade.

            📍
            ### It starts in one spot
            Changes begin in the substantia nigra (deep brain), then spread along nerves to many parts of the body.

            🧩
            ### A protein clumps
            Alpha-synuclein misfolds into Lewy bodies inside nerve cells, stopping them working well.

            🌐
            ### More than movement
            Parkinson's can affect sleep, mood, digestion, smell, blood pressure and more-not just movement.

          ### Everyone's Parkinson's is different
          No one has all of these symptoms. They vary a lot from person to person, and often come on gradually over many years. This guide shows the full range so nothing comes as a surprise.

            🏃 Motor symptoms
            ### The part people see
            
              Tremor
              Slowness
              Stiffness
              Balance
              Shuffling
              Soft voice
            
            These movement changes are how Parkinson's is usually recognised and diagnosed.

            🧠 Non-motor symptoms
            ### The part often hidden
            
              Sleep changes
              Low mood
              Anxiety
              Constipation
              Loss of smell
              Thinking changes
            
            These are extremely common, often have the biggest impact, and are very treatable once recognised.

        ## Signs that can show up years early
        Some of the first hints of Parkinson's are not about movement at all. These "early warning" signs can appear a decade or more before tremor or slowness.

          👃#### Loss of smellFood loses its aroma

          🚽#### ConstipationThe gut slows down

          😴#### Acting out dreamsREM sleep behaviour

          🙁#### Low moodDepression or anxiety

          ✍️#### Smaller writingHandwriting shrinks

          **Important:** Having one of these does not mean you have Parkinson's. They're common and usually have ordinary causes. Only a doctor can put the pattern together.

        ## Living well with Parkinson's
        Parkinson's cannot be cured yet, but there is a great deal that helps - and many people live full, active lives for many years.

            💊
            ### Medicines
            Medicines that mimic dopamine can greatly ease symptoms. Doses are personalised and fine-tuned.

            🏃
            ### Movement is medicine
            Regular exercise genuinely helps movement, balance and mood. It is one of the most powerful tools.

            🗣️
            ### Therapies
            Physiotherapy, occupational therapy and speech therapy keep you moving and independent.

            🌙
            ### Mind & sleep
            Counselling and good sleep routines matter as much as movement. They improve nearly everything else.

          ### Real reason for hope
          Parkinson's progresses slowly for many people, treatments keep improving, and research is active worldwide. With the right support, life with Parkinson's can still be rich and meaningful.

        ## Good questions to ask your care team
        Bring this list to your next visit. There are no silly questions about your own health.

          ?What stage am I at, and what might the next year or two look like for me?
          ?Which of my symptoms are from Parkinson's, and which might be something else?
          ?What can help my non-motor symptoms - sleep, mood, constipation, dizziness?
          ?What exercise is best for me, and can I see a physiotherapist?
          ?What are my medicines doing, and which side effects should I report?
          ?What should I do when a dose wears off or I feel "off"?
          ?Who is on my care team, and who do I call between visits?
          ?Are there support groups or resources for me and my whānau?
