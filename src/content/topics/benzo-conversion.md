        ### What to expect
        Withdrawal effects - rebound anxiety, broken sleep, irritability, tremor, sweating or heightened senses - can begin within days of a reduction and usually ease over 1-2 weeks as your body adjusts.

        ### When to hold or step back
        If symptoms are troublesome or have not settled, stay on your current dose for longer, or go back to the previous dose until you feel steady - then reduce more slowly. Benzodiazepine tapers often take months, and that is normal.

        ### When to talk to your doctor
        Get in touch if withdrawal is severe or not improving, or if anxiety or sleep problems return strongly. **Seek help urgently for any fits or seizures, confusion, or thoughts of harming yourself.** Never stop suddenly.

          **Switching to diazepam** (long half-life) smooths withdrawal and allows small, even dose reductions - the basis of the Ashton method.
          **Taper slowly:** typically reduce by about 1/8th to 1/4 of the dose every 1–2 weeks, slower at lower doses and if symptomatic.
          **Z-drugs** (zopiclone, zolpidem) act at the same receptor; equivalences are especially approximate.
          **Short-acting agents** (alprazolam, lorazepam) cause more interdose withdrawal - a reason to switch before tapering.
          - Avoid in significant respiratory disease; caution with opioids, alcohol and in older adults (falls, cognition).
