## Overview


ConditionPicture
   Biliary colicRUQ/epigastric pain after fatty food, lasts minutes-hours then settles; **no fever**, normal inflammatory markers
   Acute cholecystitisPersistent RUQ pain, fever, Murphy’s sign positive, raised WCC/CRP
   CholedocholithiasisStone in CBD; pain + obstructive LFTs ± jaundice
   Ascending cholangitis**Charcot’s triad**: RUQ pain, fever, jaundice (+ hypotension/confusion = Reynolds’ pentad) - septic emergency
   Gallstone pancreatitisEpigastric pain to back, raised lipase

      🔍Assessment▾
      
   **Ultrasound** first-line: stones, gallbladder wall thickening, duct dilatation.
   **LFTs**: a cholestatic (obstructive) pattern - raised ALP/GGT/bilirubin - points to duct involvement; **lipase** for pancreatitis.
   **MRCP** to define duct stones; **ERCP** is therapeutic (stone extraction, stenting).
   **Bloods**: FBC, CRP for inflammation/infection.
  
  Fever + jaundice + RUQ pain = cholangitisThis is a surgical/medical emergency needing resuscitation, IV antibiotics and urgent biliary drainage (ERCP). Don’t sit on it.

      🔪Management▾
      
   **Asymptomatic stones:** no treatment (exceptions: porcelain gallbladder, large stones in some, certain high-risk groups).
   **Symptomatic stones / biliary colic:** analgesia, low-fat advice, and elective **laparoscopic cholecystectomy** - the definitive treatment.
   **Acute cholecystitis:** admit, analgesia, antibiotics; cholecystectomy (increasingly during the same admission).
   **Duct stones:** ERCP clearance, then cholecystectomy.
   **Cholangitis/pancreatitis:** emergency management as above.

   ✦**Don’t treat asymptomatic stones** found incidentally.
   ✦**Fever separates colic from cholecystitis.**
   ✦**Obstructive LFTs** mean look at the duct (MRCP/ERCP).
   ✦**Check lipase** in any severe epigastric pain - don’t miss gallstone pancreatitis.
