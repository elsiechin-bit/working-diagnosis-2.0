## Diagnosis


Diagnosis is clinical, supported by imaging and neuropsychology, and usually requires specialist (neurology, old-age psychiatry, or memory service) input.

          **History and collateral** are central - the informant account of behavioural or language change carries more weight than any office test, because standard cognitive screens are often normal early in bvFTD.
          **Criteria:** consensus diagnostic criteria exist for behavioural variant FTD and for the primary progressive aphasias, combining clinical features with imaging support.3,4
          **Neuroimaging:** MRI showing frontal and/or anterior temporal atrophy; FDG-PET showing frontotemporal hypometabolism where MRI is equivocal.
          **Neuropsychology:** a dysexecutive profile with social-cognition deficits and relative sparing of memory and visuospatial function.
          **Excluding Alzheimer's:** amyloid biomarkers (PET, CSF, or emerging blood tests) are typically negative in FTD - useful when the distinction is unclear.
          **Motor assessment:** screen for signs of motor neurone disease and of parkinsonism (PSP/CBS).
          **Genetic testing and counselling** where there is a family history or young onset.


## Management


There is, as yet, no disease-modifying or curative treatment. Management is supportive, symptomatic, and - because of the behavioural burden and the young age of those affected - intensely practical and family-centred.

        ### Pharmacological, with important cautions
        
          **SSRIs** (for example sertraline, citalopram) and trazodone have the best, if modest, evidence for the behavioural symptoms of bvFTD - disinhibition, compulsive behaviours, and overeating.
          **Cholinesterase inhibitors are not recommended.** Unlike in Alzheimer's, they confer no benefit and may worsen agitation and behaviour. Memantine has likewise not shown benefit in FTD trials.5
          **Antipsychotics:** use sparingly and cautiously. People with FTD can be markedly sensitive to extrapyramidal side effects, and these agents carry the same stroke and mortality risks seen across dementia. Reserve for severe risk or distress, lowest dose, shortest time.

          ### The behavioural and safety work
          Non-pharmacological strategies are first-line: structure and routine, identifying and removing triggers, redirecting compulsions, and educating and supporting the family. Disinhibition and impaired judgement create early, serious risks that memory-led dementias do not - **financial vulnerability and impulsive spending, risky or socially inappropriate behaviour, and driving**. Early enduring power of attorney, financial safeguards, and a frank driving assessment matter sooner in FTD than in Alzheimer's.
