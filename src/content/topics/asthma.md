#### Criteria
      Asthma is a clinical diagnosis supported by objective evidence of variable airflow obstruction. Do not diagnose on symptoms alone.

        **Spirometry:** obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC <0.70) with significant bronchodilator reversibility (>12% and >200 mL FEV1)
        **Peak flow variability:** >20% diurnal variation on ≥3 days over 2 weeks
        **Bronchial provocation:** methacholine challenge (if spirometry normal but clinical suspicion high)
        **FeNO (fractional exhaled NO):** ≥40 ppb suggests eosinophilic airway inflammation  -  predicts steroid response
      
      Asthma-COPD overlapIn adults ≥40 with smoking history and persistent airflow obstruction after bronchodilator  -  may be asthma-COPD overlap (ACO). Treat with ICS-containing therapy; avoid LABA monotherapy without ICS.

      #### Differential diagnoses
      
        - COPD (spirometry post-BD, smoking history, age)
        - Vocal cord dysfunction (flow-volume loop, laryngoscopy)
        - Cardiac failure (BNP, echo)
        - Bronchiectasis (HRCT)
        - GORD, chronic rhinosinusitis (common asthma mimics and triggers)

      #### SMART therapy
      Single inhaler Maintenance And Reliever Therapy using **budesonide-formoterol**. Used for both maintenance and as-needed relief. Reduces severe exacerbations by ~30% vs fixed-dose ICS + SABA rescue. Available in NZ: Symbicort Turbuhaler.

      #### ICS delivery
      
        - pMDI + spacer = DPI for efficacy; spacer essential for pMDI
        - Rinse mouth after ICS to prevent oral candidiasis
        - Check technique at every visit

    🚨Acute severe asthma▾
    
      #### Assessment
      
        SeverityFeaturesPEFR
        
          ModerateIncreasing symptoms, PEFR 50 - 75%, no severe features50 - 75%
          Acute severeRR >25, HR >110, unable to complete sentences33 - 50%
          Life-threateningSilent chest, cyanosis, exhaustion, SpO2 <92%, altered consciousness<33%

      #### Management
      
        **Oxygen:** high-flow to maintain SpO2 94 - 98%
        **SABA:** salbutamol 2.5 - 5 mg nebulised every 20 - 30 min, or 4 - 8 puffs pMDI + spacer
        **Ipratropium:** 0.5 mg nebulised, add to SABA in severe/life-threatening
        **Systemic corticosteroid:** prednisolone 40 - 50 mg immediately (or IV hydrocortisone if unable to swallow)
        **IV magnesium sulphate:** 1.2 - 2 g IV over 20 min for severe/life-threatening  -  significant bronchodilation
        - Admit if PEFR <75% after 1 hour, any life-threatening features, or unreliable home situation
      
      Life-threatening features  -  call 111Silent chest, SpO2 <92% despite oxygen, exhaustion, cyanosis, altered consciousness  -  these represent near-fatal asthma. Immediate resuscitation, senior clinician, prepare for intubation.

    🧬Biologics for severe asthma▾
    
      For adults with Step 4 - 5 asthma uncontrolled on high-dose ICS + LABA, consider specialist referral for biologic therapy. All require ≥2 severe exacerbations/year or oral steroid dependence.

        DrugTargetIndication
        
          OmalizumabAnti-IgEAllergic asthma, elevated IgE + allergen sensitisation
          Mepolizumab / BenralizumabAnti-IL-5/IL-5REosinophilic asthma (blood eos ≥300 cells/µL)
          DupilumabAnti-IL-4/IL-13Eosinophilic or OCS-dependent asthma; also treats co-existing eczema, nasal polyposis
          TezepelumabAnti-TSLPSevere uncontrolled asthma regardless of eosinophil count

    📈Monitoring, triggers & pearls▾
    
      #### Monitoring
      
        - ACT score at every visit  -  drives step up/down decisions
        - Spirometry annually in stable asthma; more often if uncontrolled
        - Reliever use: >2 SABA puffs/week = indicator of uncontrolled asthma  -  step up
        - Written Asthma Action Plan for all patients
      
      #### Common triggers
      
        - Allergens (house dust mite, cat, grass pollen)  -  consider allergen testing and immunotherapy
        - Respiratory infections  -  most exacerbations in adults; ensure influenza vaccination
        - NSAIDs / aspirin  -  aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD) in ~10%
        - Beta-blockers (including eye drops)  -  can precipitate severe bronchospasm
        - Exercise  -  warm up, pre-treat with SABA; consider LTRA
        - Occupational exposures  -  consider occupational asthma if workplace pattern
      
      #### Pearls
      
        - Poor inhaler technique accounts for a large proportion of "uncontrolled" asthma
        - SABA overuse (>3 canisters/year) is independently associated with increased mortality
        - Do not stop ICS during pregnancy  -  uncontrolled asthma poses greater risk than ICS
        - Smoking cessation reduces exacerbations and improves steroid response
