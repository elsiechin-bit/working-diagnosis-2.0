#### DSM-5 criteria
          
            - Restriction of energy intake relative to needs; abnormally low weight.
            - Intense fear of weight gain, despite low weight.
            - Disturbance in self-perception of weight/shape, or denial of seriousness of low weight.
          
          #### Subtypes
          
            **Restricting:** Weight loss through dieting, exercise, fasting; no regular binge/purge.
            **Binge-eating / purging:** Regular episodes of binge-eating or purging (self-induced vomiting, laxatives).
          
          #### Severity (by BMI)
          
            - Mild: BMI ≥17.
            - Moderate: BMI 16–16.99.
            - Severe: BMI 15–15.99.

        🏥Multisystem medical complications▾
        
          #### Cardiac
          
            - Bradycardia, hypotension (protective mechanism).
            - Cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias (QTc prolongation risk with electrolyte abnormalities).
            - Sudden cardiac death possible, especially with purging and hypokalaemia.
          
          #### Electrolyte and metabolic
          
            - Hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hyponatraemia, hypophosphataemia.
            - Metabolic alkalosis (from vomiting) or acidosis.
            - Refeeding syndrome risk with reintroduction of nutrition (see below).
          
          #### Skeletal
          
            - Reduced bone mineral density; increased fracture risk; osteoporosis in 25–50%.
            - Oestrogen deficiency (amenorrhoea) and malnutrition both contributory.
          
          #### Endocrine
          
            - Hypothalamic hypogonadism (low LH/FSH, oestrogen/testosterone); amenorrhoea.
            - Low T3 syndrome; growth hormone resistance.
            - Hypercortisolaemia (HPA axis dysregulation).
          
          #### GI
          
            - Delayed gastric emptying; bloating, early satiety.
            - Oesophageal erosions, parotid gland enlargement (from vomiting).
            - Constipation, increased laxative dependence.
          
          #### Haematological
          
            - Anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia (from marrow suppression).
          
          #### Neuropsychiatric
          
            - Cognitive impairment, depression, anxiety, obsessive-compulsive features.
            - High suicide risk (10–15% mortality if untreated long-term).

        ⚠️Refeeding syndrome▾
        
          #### Pathophysiology
          Reintroduction of nutrition causes shift from catabolic to anabolic metabolism. Phosphate, potassium, and magnesium move intracellularly to build ATP and structural proteins; serum levels drop precipitously, causing arrhythmias, seizures, respiratory failure, and death.

          #### Prevention and management
          
            - Slow refeeding: start at 1,200 kcal/day, increase by 200–300 kcal daily; IV nutrition preferred if severe malnutrition.
            - Frequent electrolyte monitoring (daily initially; K+, Mg2+, PO43-).
            - Phosphate, potassium, magnesium supplementation as needed.
            - Thiamine before glucose to prevent Wernicke's encephalopathy.

        🗣️Integrated treatment▾
        
          #### Hospitalization indications
          
            BMI 
            Severe medical instability (hypotension, bradycardia 
            - Suicidal ideation or inability to comply with outpatient treatment.
          
          #### Inpatient refeeding
          
            - Controlled environment; nursing support; medical monitoring (daily vitals, electrolytes, ECG).
            - Refeeding starting 1,000–1,200 kcal/day, advance cautiously.
          
          #### Psychotherapy
          
            **CBT-E (Enhanced Cognitive Behaviour Therapy):** Outpatient gold standard; 20 weeks; addresses eating disorder thinking and behaviour.
            **Family-based therapy (FBT):** For adolescents; parents control meals and support weight restoration.
            **Psychodynamic or interpersonal therapy:** Address underlying trauma, relationships, perfectionism.
          
          #### Pharmacotherapy
          
            - Antidepressants (SSRIs) for comorbid depression/anxiety; limited efficacy for core eating disorder.
            - Atypical antipsychotics (quetiapine, olanzapine) may reduce obsessive thoughts about food/weight in some; modest evidence.
          
          #### Bone health
          
            - Weight restoration is primary intervention; hormone replacement therapy (HRT) not routinely recommended unless amenorrhoea persists after weight recovery.

        ❓Common questions▾
        
          #### Can anorexia nervosa be cured?
          Recovery is possible; 50–60% achieve full remission with treatment, especially early intervention and in younger patients. Others have ongoing struggles but can achieve stable weight and manage psychological symptoms.

          #### What is the mortality rate?
          Mortality 5–10% over 10 years if untreated or inadequately treated; lower with intensive early intervention. Leading causes: cardiac complications, suicide, infections.
