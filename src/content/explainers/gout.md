## Serum urate - treatment targets

All gout patients should be treated to **< 0.36 mmol/L** (below monosodium urate solubility threshold). Patients with severe or tophaceous gout require **< 0.30 mmol/L**.

**Do not measure urate during an acute attack** - it may be falsely normal. Measure 4+ weeks after the attack. Urate-lowering therapy (ULT) is lifelong once indicated.

## Acute attack management

### Diagnosis
- Clinical: acute onset severe joint pain (often nocturnal), warmth, erythema, swelling - first MTP joint (podagra) most classic
- Joints affected: first MTP, ankle, knee, wrist, finger joints, olecranon bursa
- MSU crystals in synovial fluid (needle-shaped, negatively birefringent under polarised light) - gold standard
- Dual-energy CT or ultrasound (double-contour sign) when diagnosis uncertain
- **Do not use serum urate during acute attack** - often normal or low during flare

### Treatment options (choose one; add if needed)

**First-line NSAIDs** (if tolerated and no contraindications)
- Indomethacin 50 mg TDS for 5-7 days, or
- Naproxen 500 mg BD for 5-7 days, or
- Ibuprofen 2.4 g daily for 5-7 days

**Colchicine**
- Start immediately: 1 mg loading, then 500 mcg at 2-3 hour intervals (max 3 mg per day)
- Stop when diarrhoea develops or max dose reached
- Most effective within 24 hours of attack onset
- **Reduce dose in renal impairment** (CrCl < 50 mL/min)

**Corticosteroids**
- Prednisolone 30-35 mg daily for 5-7 days, then taper
- Triamcinolone acetonide 40 mg IM single dose
- Intra-articular steroid injection if monoarticular attack

### Adjuncts
- Bed rest, elevation
- Ice packs to affected joint
- Fluids (uric acid dilution)
- NSAIDs or paracetamol for systemic symptoms

## Urate-lowering therapy

### When to start ULT

**Indications for starting ULT:**
- Recurrent gout attacks (≥ 2 per year)
- Tophaceous gout (visible deposits)
- Chronic kidney disease (any stage)
- Uric acid stone disease (history)
- Asymptomatic hyperuricaemia with significant risk factors

### First-line ULT agents

**Allopurinol**
- Start low: 50-100 mg daily (especially in renal impairment)
- Titrate by 50-100 mg every 2-4 weeks
- Target: dose that achieves urate < 0.36 mmol/L
- Typical maintenance: 200-300 mg daily (range 100-800 mg)
- Monitor LFTs and renal function at baseline and after dose changes

**Febuxostat** (if allopurinol intolerant)
- Start 80 mg daily
- Can increase to 120 mg daily
- Monitor cardiovascular risk
- **Caution: avoid if HAS-BLED score ≥ 3**

**Benzbromarone** (if allopurinol/febuxostat contraindicated)
- Start 50 mg daily, titrate to 50-200 mg daily
- Uricosuric agent
- Check baseline urate > 0.60 mmol/L

### Urate-lowering therapy prophylaxis

**Prevent flares during ULT initiation:**
- NSAIDs (e.g. indomethacin 50 mg daily) for 4-6 weeks, OR
- Low-dose colchicine (500 mcg BD) for 4-6 weeks

**Do not start or stop ULT during acute flare** - increases flare risk

## Common prescribing errors

1. **Starting high-dose allopurinol immediately** - causes flares. Always start low and titrate.
2. **Fixed dosing instead of target-based** - titrate to serum urate level, not to a set dose.
3. **ULT during acute attack** - wait 2-4 weeks post-attack
4. **Missing colchicine toxicity** - dose should be reduced significantly in renal disease
5. **Measuring urate acutely** - doesn't reflect true baseline. Wait ≥ 4 weeks.
6. **Stopping ULT between flares** - therapy is lifelong in recurrent gout
7. **No prophylaxis when starting ULT** - NSAID or colchicine prophylaxis prevents flares during initiation

## Lifestyle and diet

- **Weight loss** - reduces urate by ~1.5 mmol/L per 10 kg
- **Limit alcohol** - especially beer and spirits
- **Avoid high-purine foods** - red meat, organ meats, certain seafood (anchovies, sardines, shellfish)
- **Limit fructose** - soft drinks, fruit juices, table sugar
- **Stay hydrated** - reduces crystal formation
- **Consider dairy** - may have protective effect
